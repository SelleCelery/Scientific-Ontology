# Stopping Correctness on Autopilot
## Business, the Natural-Disaster Designation, and the Inheritance of Mind: Structure and Responsibility for Judgment

The fact that a mechanism keeps operating is not a sufficient reason to keep it operating. Yet the more smoothly a mechanism runs, the easier it becomes to forget that distinction. If it makes a profit, the business appears successful. If a relationship continues, coordination appears to be working. If lessons are passed down, wisdom appears to have been preserved.

At such times, what was achieved may be checked while what was lost in achieving it is pushed elsewhere. Something treated as processed inside a mechanism remains unprocessed in people's bodies and lives. Conclusions alone pass onward, while the circumstances that made them necessary disappear. The person who asks about those lost circumstances is then treated as a nuisance who holds up the work.

This essay approaches that problem from three places. In Business, it separates successful optimization from the validity of its purpose. In interpersonal relationships, it asks where the boundary lies between continuing to seek mutual understanding and protecting a finite body. It then considers what is inherited, and what falls away, when judgments gained through those processes remain beyond the life of the person who made them.

These are not the same phenomenon. I do not intend to transfer market mechanisms directly into the workings of the mind, or to reduce interpersonal relationships to natural laws. But the same question can be put to different domains: **What does this structure leave out in order to operate? And does it retain a route by which what it left out can change its judgment?**

That question also applies to this essay. Once an account of the dangers of structures becomes useful, it too can become a structure for classifying people without understanding them. What follows is not criticism from a safe spectator's seat. It is an attempt to examine a tool I intend to use, up to the point where its blade turns toward me.

## Part I: The Dangers of Business
### 1. A mechanism that works without mutual understanding

One strength of Business is that people can cooperate without deeply understanding one another. They do not have to like each other or share a view of life. If they specify what to provide, what to receive, and under what conditions the transaction ends, they can connect the activities of different people. Without such arrangements, the burden of mutual understanding alone would probably make many kinds of work impossible.

Treating people through roles or numbers therefore cannot immediately be called inhuman. A degree of abstraction may be necessary precisely to avoid treating people according to familiarity, liking, or dislike. A society that demands deep understanding of everyone's personality could also become a society that excludes from exchange anyone it cannot understand.

The problem comes after abstraction. Selecting only the information needed for a transaction is not the same as grasping every important aspect of the other person. Knowing working hours and a unit rate does not tell us whether the person will be able to recover afterward. Even when deadlines and quality requirements are met, that does not show whether meeting them has depleted the ability to take on the next job.

What becomes dangerous here is not treating a human being as a variable. It is **beginning to treat what was left out of the variables as though it did not exist**. An omission made for calculation turns into a judgment about reality. A range we decided not to handle is replaced by a range we need not handle. The numbers themselves do not reveal that transition.

Nor is the problem solved by putting feelings ahead of numbers. Exhaustion and anxiety can be important information, but those feelings alone do not establish causes or responsibility. What is needed is not an end to quantification, but a way back to what the numbers represent and what they do not.

### 2. The problem is not an absence of purposes, but purposes that are no longer questioned

The statement "Business can optimize, but it does not tell us where we ought to go" needs qualification. Companies do have purposes, and they can change their goals internally. Managers may value things other than profit. It is therefore inaccurate to assert literally that Business has no direction.

The distinction I want to make is between being able to set a goal and being able to derive, from the result of optimization alone, a reason to prioritize that goal over other values. Discovering how to increase profit does not settle whether it is permissible to sacrifice what will be lost in doing so. Accurate calculation does not guarantee the legitimacy of the purpose given to that calculation.

Suppose, for example, that a proposal to reduce employees' learning time is made under a goal of increasing the number of cases processed. Even if the proposal raises the number processed this period, that alone does not show that future capacity to respond has not been lost. On the other hand, stopping currently necessary work to protect learning time can also create a problem. The case-count indicator alone cannot decide between them.

The dangerous state, then, is not "optimization taking place although no one has any purpose." It is **a particular purpose being selected as the default while the reasons for continuing to select it cease to be re-examined**. The workplace has a clear goal. What it lacks is a route for asking what that goal was meant to protect.

Likewise, when we say that "the objective function must be supplied from outside," that outside need not be outside the company or outside economics. What is needed is another judgment capable of treating the current optimization procedure itself as an object. Such a judgment can be located inside a company or arise through negotiation with users and workers. But the mere fact that the current indicator improved cannot substitute for it.

> Note 1: In this essay, Business is not a single actor representing all of economics or every corporate activity. It is shorthand for practices that pursue revenue, exchange, and organizational operations under particular indicators. "Optimization" likewise does not mean that every company computes a mathematical optimum. It includes the tendency to reorganize activities in response to indicators and evaluation criteria.

### 3. Resources widen the range within which one can keep going after a mistaken judgment

Money increases options. That does not imply that people with money exercise less judgment. Resources can make it possible to consult experts, investigate, and decide without haste. Conversely, someone with few resources may be unable to secure the time needed for careful judgment even when they wish to do so. Poverty does not automatically cultivate wisdom.

Even so, an asymmetry between resources and judgment should not be overlooked. The same choice means something different to a person who can try again after failure and to someone who could lose the basis of their livelihood after a single failure. A person with many resources may be able to proceed while omitting judgment, or delay the point at which the consequences of a mistaken judgment return to them.

Furthermore, when the decision-maker and the cost-bearer differ, failure that should prompt revision can be processed as someone else's burden. Consider a situation in which the person setting an unreasonable deadline sees only successful delivery, while the person absorbing the strain is left with exhaustion and delayed recovery. The problem in this example is not whether the decision-maker is wealthy. It is that success and burden travel along different paths back from the decision.

Rather than saying "with money, one need not judge," I would therefore say: **resources and authority can sometimes increase the room for avoiding the return of a mistaken judgment to one's own decision-making**. The same resources can support better investigation or insulation from consequences. The issue is not only how much resource one has, but what paths of correction are built around it.

### 4. Profit does not exhaust the question of what has been left behind

"Economically successful, but leaving nothing behind" makes for a forceful criticism. Yet it is not literally true that nothing remains. Goods, wages, and equipment may remain. Corporate accounting deals with stocks, and economics includes discussions of externalities and public goods. If I sum economics up as seeing only immediate monetary flows, my criticism becomes the cruder account.

> Note 2: Externalities include the problem of effects on parties outside a transaction not being adequately reflected in prices, leading to divergence between private and social costs and benefits. Thomas Helbling's IMF explanation makes this difference explicit. This essay does not claim that economics is unaware of the problem; it asks whether known distinctions actually enter particular decisions. Reference: Thomas Helbling, [Externalities: Prices Do Not Capture All Costs](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/fandd/issues/series/back-to-basics/externalities), IMF, Finance & Development.

The more precise question is: **Are what was lost, and the capacities not passed on to the next occasion, recognized as failures of the transaction?** A business can be profitable while the costs of repairing the local environment on which it depended are passed to another actor; in that case, profit does not represent the balance as a whole. A business may continue while replacement staff remain available even if employees are not being developed. But who is maintaining the reproduction of skills is then a separate question.

We must not conclude that "eventually the company itself is certain to pay." Burdens may remain externalized for a long time, and those who made the decisions may leave with the gains. There is no guarantee that an inappropriate structure will be naturally punished. That is why return needs to be institutionally arranged.

Return is not only a loss mechanically coming back to the same actor. It also includes workplace experience changing requirements and the provision of the costs and authority needed to make those changes. Even where a complaints desk exists, if what it hears cannot change the way decisions are made, the burden has arrived without returning to judgment.

Asking what remains does not mean demanding growth or self-realization from every job. Earning wages, maintaining one's livelihood, and retaining time and physical strength for life outside work may be enough. Before requiring work to add something, it matters more not to normalize a condition in which something irrecoverable is lost every time one works.

### 5. Reading something as a natural phenomenon is not blaming nature

Reading Business as a response system likely to move in certain directions under certain conditions can expose things that personal criticism misses. Before deciding whether a manager is good or bad, examining what the organization rewards and which losses it renders invisible may explain its behavior better.

But this reading also quickly becomes dangerous. "That is just the market" or "competition leaves no other choice" can make even conditions people can change appear to be natural laws. Acting rationally within an institution and being justified in maintaining that institution are separate matters.

Reading something as a natural phenomenon means not explaining it through will or malice alone. It does not erase responsibility. Who could change the conditions, who refused to change them, and who bore the costs remain part of the history. Structural explanation should be used not to excuse actors, but to locate points of change beyond the reach of personal criticism alone.

This leads to the next problem. Even if I understand a structure, the person in front of me may neither share that understanding nor agree to coordinate. Must I nevertheless continue explaining to them?

## Part II: Provisional Conditions for the Natural-Disaster Designation
### 6. A decision to stop investing in persuasion, not a verdict on personality

I deliberately call a way of thinking about withdrawal from relationships where mutual coordination is not working a "natural-disaster designation." It is quite a name. It sounds as though it places people alongside typhoons, and misused, it really could become an excuse to stop treating someone as human. I therefore want to address the unease of the name rather than remove it from the essay.

But we must not misidentify what is being designated. What I can observe is that, within particular conditions and a particular relationship, certain attempts at explanation and coordination did not produce the expected change. I have neither read the person's mind completely nor proved their permanent inability to change.

They may accept an explanation from someone else. They may understand its content but be unable to act differently in their present role. They may understand my request and refuse for another reason. Without being able to distinguish these possibilities, I cannot establish a permanent category of "people incapable of updating."

The judgment must therefore be limited: **Given the responses currently observable and the burden incurred so far, I will stop investing additional resources in this relationship with mutual understanding or persuasion as my main means.** That is what the designation means here. It is an operational decision to change my mode of contact, not a verdict on a person's essence.

The image of not demanding that a typhoon show remorse is useful on this point alone. I do not make the other person's remorse a precondition for my own safety. I do not stay until they give an explanation that satisfies me. Not because they lack personhood, but because my finite time and body are being used while I wait for mutual understanding.

> Note 3: "Natural-disaster designation" is not an official designation, medical diagnosis, or personality-assessment scale. It is a provisional name used in this essay. In real consultations and records, it is better to describe specific actions and their effects on oneself than to label the other person. The disaster metaphor also does not transfer responsibility for human action to nature so as to excuse it.

### 7. Distinguishing differences in values from the absence of a route for coordination

Disagreement alone does not ground this judgment. I may prioritize safety while the other person prioritizes speed. Each may hear the other's explanation and still retain a different judgment. Updating does not necessarily mean agreeing with the other person.

What matters more is the procedure for handling disagreement. Can I acknowledge someone's role-based authority while asking about the grounds of their decision? Is correction about facts distinguished from defiance of their position? When something goes badly, is there a path back to the original demand or allocation of resources? If these routes are closed, different values cease to be matters for negotiation and become reasons for demanding compliance.

A person in a managerial role may have authority to make a decision. But that authority does not entail that "my values are superior as a matter of personal standing." A subordinate's undertaking necessary work is distinct from accepting the superior's understanding of the world as correct. If this separation is refused and asking for reasons is itself treated as disrespect, the path for adjusting substantive matters has been narrowed.

If, even after explanations change or concrete results are shown, the problem is invariably returned to the speaker's attitude, it is worth asking whether input is being treated as material for judgment. Repeatedly receiving the same answer is not enough by itself, however. When a request is inappropriate, maintaining the same refusal can be justified. The question is not whether the reaction changes as such, but whether changed grounds or conditions can be received.

There is no need here to settle the other person's psychology. Even without concluding "they must be protecting their pride," concrete information going unexamined and results remaining disconnected from reconsideration may give reason to change the mode of contact. If withdrawal requires a successful decoding of someone's inner life, this model becomes another burden.

### 8. Do not turn a higher level of meta-recognition into a new rank

It is intuitively useful to picture differences in values as a horizontal plane and the ability to see one's own values as an object as the "height of meta-recognition." Recognizing where I am looking from can help me notice that disagreement is not simply a matter of right and wrong.

But that height must not become a ranking of human beings. If "I can see the structure, whereas they cannot" turns directly into "therefore adopt my judgment," I have criticized the advantages of wealth and position only to create a different hierarchy of status.

The ability to articulate one's presuppositions and the ability to revise behavior appropriately are not identical. Some people can respect another person's refusal without offering an elaborate explanation. Conversely, someone may deliver refined self-analysis while accepting no correction at all. The fact that another person does not understand my theoretical vocabulary must therefore not count as evidence that mutual coordination is impossible.

What this model requires is not a test of sophisticated self-explanation. It asks whether, within a concrete relationship, another person's wishes and disadvantages can be addressed and necessary changes in behavior considered. **What matters more than being able to talk about one's meta-recognition is whether corrections and refusals can reach behavior.**

> Note 4: "Height" is a metaphor for the range of observation, including oneself, not a unified measurement scale. Acknowledging differences in values does not mean regarding all value judgments as equally valid, or treating harm as merely a difference in values.

### 9. Checking matters, but it must not become an endurance test

There is value in checking whether my explanation was adequate before withdrawing. The other person may only temporarily lack the capacity to respond, or institutional constraints beyond their individual will may be at work. I may be absolutizing my own values and treating every refusal as a failure to understand.

But these checks must not become a qualification exam for withdrawal. If one endlessly demands "Have you tried every way of explaining?" or "Have you understood their circumstances sufficiently?", withdrawing requires further exhaustion. A model intended to ease the obligation of mutual understanding becomes an obligation to collect proof that mutual understanding has failed.

An asymmetry is therefore needed. A broad negative public claim about the other person requires grounds commensurate with it. By contrast, deciding to take distance for one's safety or recovery need not settle the person's character or future. The need to withdraw can be judged from the burden generated by current contact rather than the person's essence.

Where there is danger, there is no need to repeat contact in order to complete the model. Nor is it the point of this essay to place ever more responsibility for improving explanations on someone already exhausted. The order is not: first understand the other person sufficiently, then earn permission to protect yourself.

### 10. Moving from persuasion to protection does not change the other person's humanity

After changing the mode of contact, changing the other person's values is no longer the central purpose. Necessary communication can be brief and concrete, with agreements and changes recorded in a form that can be checked. If direct exchange increases the burden, consider changing the person or channel handling it. The point is not to defeat the other person but to avoid repeating the same exhaustion.

Records made for this purpose should not be used to complete a portrait of the other person as a villain. Keep events distinct from interpretations. If it is clear what request received what response and what happened as a result, other explanations remain available later. If only records asserting the other person's intentions accumulate, the record itself strengthens a fixed preconception.

There is no need to distinguish treating someone as human from merely observing formalities on the assumption that they are human. The other person is human from beginning to end. The absence of confirmed understanding or empathy toward me does not change that. What changes is not their status as a person but the expectations and resources I place in the relationship.

Equally, their being human does not mean I owe them unlimited involvement. Recognizing dignity is not the same as intimacy. Necessary dealings are not the same as performing affection. This distinction makes it possible to limit a relationship while maintaining courtesy.

> Note 5: Specific occupational and legal duties differ by relationship. This withdrawal model must not be used by someone with support or supervisory responsibilities to abandon necessary responses because a person is "difficult." Nor does acquiring or sharing records permit disregard of third-party privacy or confidentiality.

### 11. Not resuming contact differs from declaring change impossible

The greatest danger in this model is that taking distance reduces opportunities to learn whether the other person has changed. "I have not confirmed any change" can then become "they have not changed," and eventually "they cannot change." Observation reduced for protective reasons becomes evidence permanently justifying that protection.

Even so, one cannot impose an obligation to return periodically and re-examine the other person. Renewed contact may be dangerous or too costly. A long-term policy of not resuming a relationship can coexist with holding one's understanding of the other person provisionally.

For example: not "this person will never change," but "I do not deny that they might change, but I will not undertake the work of finding out." This is not a weak retraction. It decides how to use one's own resources without controlling the other person's future. **Protecting one's own boundary does not require erasing uncertainty about someone else.**

If one says that "natural-disaster designation begins by observing the other's cessation of updating and is completed by one's own cessation of updating," what is completed is a closed explanation of the other person. That closure must not be equated with mature self-protection. Protection can remain while the explanation retains what is unknown.

The other person may also see me as a fixed response system. But the fact that both parties see each other that way does not make responsibility or power symmetrical. The same refusal has different effects for someone who can compel contact and someone who cannot easily leave because of their living conditions. Discovering a mirror image and assigning equal responsibility to both sides are different matters.

## Part III: The Body Dies. Mind Does Not Die in the Same Way
### 12. Do not confuse inherited effects with the person's continuation

The body dies. But calling that a fair fact does not settle anything. People do not live under the same conditions or have equal opportunities to recover. What I want to establish here is that the body cannot be treated as a resource available without limit.

The word "mind" requires a more limited use. This essay uses it for structures of experience and judgment while considering how words and habits arising from those structures affect other people and institutions. Yet an influence remaining is not the same as the person's subjective experience continuing.

Writing may be read, and someone's judgment may become incorporated into another person's thinking. That does not prove that the writer continues living inside the reader. On the receiving side, a new understanding is formed under another body and another history of experience.

Still, I want to retain the expression "the body dies; mind does not die in the same way." Patterns of judgment created by a person's activity can keep acting after that activity has ended. What remains, moreover, is not necessarily understanding. Misunderstandings and rules that have lost their purposes can remain too.

The point is not that mind is more durable than the body. **Continuing to act and preserving meaning are different things.** If words keep operating after their author can no longer correct them, inheritance is both a hope and a difficult matter to handle.

> Note 6: This section reaches no conclusion on detailed biological criteria for death or the physical basis of consciousness. "Mind does not die in the same way" is an expression about effects inherited through words, judgments, and institutions, not an empirical claim that individual consciousness or a soul is immortal.

### 13. Seeing a structure does not place us outside it

Human judgment does not arise from nowhere. In an environment where one choice earns approval and another requires explanation, the actions a person can readily choose differ even when their abilities are the same. Commands need not be issued: if the costs of choices differ, behavior is affected by that difference.

Criticizing Business does not remove the need to pay living expenses. Understanding how recognition works does not make relationships with others unnecessary. Recognizing "I am being moved by this structure" does not mean escaping it. Gaining understanding does not exempt one from bodily or material conditions.

That does not make the understanding worthless. If a burden I took to be a defect in my character turns out to intensify in relation to a particular evaluation system, the object of possible change shifts. The options now include changing the conditions of contact, not only training myself harder.

Meta-recognition provides not freedom itself, but clues to places that might be changeable. Changing them may require time, money, or help from others. A person who understands but cannot act should not immediately be assigned a lack of understanding or weakness of will.

The body is not merely a container in which judgment is implemented. It is also the condition that lives through the time judgment takes, pays the cost of trial and error, and undertakes life after a change. Making the next judgment possible requires not exhausting the present body. That also explains why mutual understanding cannot become an unlimited obligation.

### 14. Reading life as Git: What should be retained?

Here I borrow Git, knowing that the analogy is a stretch. Git manages the history of changes to files. Life does not run on Git, but the metaphor can help distinguish the present state from the history that led to it.

Treating a current policy as one branch makes it possible to consider moving from "if I explain, they should understand" toward "limit communication to what is necessary and take distance." But in life we cannot mechanically return to a previous state. Adopting a new policy does not erase previous experience or the body's burden.

The problem is how the past is read afterward. Reinterpreting "that withdrawal was not defeat" or "that anger was a response protecting a boundary" can matter. Yet if the interpretation also erases how frightened I was, what I misunderstood, or the burdens I imposed on others, history starts to look as though my current thinking had been complete from the beginning.

I am tempted to compare that over-tidying to rebasing one's life. But Git's rebase is not a dishonest operation. Legitimately replaying commits on a base and retelling past facts to suit oneself are different things. What I borrow is the caution that when a sequence is reorganized to be easier to read from the present, the earlier route can become harder to see.

> Note 7: Git's `rebase` reapplies commits on another base; interactive operation can also reorder or combine them. It does not change past reality, nor does it mean that the original history always disappears immediately. "Rebasing life" is used here only as a metaphor for arranging a present self-account. Reference: Git documentation, [git-rebase](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-rebase).

It may be easier to trace later if past facts are left in place and a new judgment is added: "I understood it this way then, but now I revise this part." Just as Git's revert records a new commit counteracting an earlier change, the revision is retained as a separate event. This is not about blaming one's former self forever. It is about not deleting from the present explanation what was revised.

> Note 8: Git's `revert` records changes that counteract existing changes as a new commit. Human experience cannot be undone in the same way, but the operation offers a way to think about the distinction between erasing an earlier event and correcting its effects through a later action. Reference: Git documentation, [git-revert](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-revert).

### 15. Carry away only the lesson, and wisdom becomes a commandment

Taking a useful part from another experience resembles cherry-pick. One brings a judgment such as "withdraw when it is too much" or "do not keep explaining" out of a past failure. That is useful in itself; there is no need to undergo everything again each time.

But if the conditions that made the judgment necessary are lost, what is brought back changes character. "Stop explaining," effective in a particular relationship, becomes "explaining to people is pointless." A conclusion that protected me under certain conditions becomes a preemptive refusal directed toward someone else.

Here too, the Git operation itself does not erase provenance. Cherry-pick applies changes from a selected commit, and there are ways to record a reference to the original. The question is how much of the circumstances on which a change depended is brought along with its content.

> Note 9: Git's `cherry-pick` has an `-x` option that adds a reference to the original commit to the message. It is therefore incorrect to say that cherry-pick necessarily loses provenance. But possessing a source identifier and sufficiently preserving the background and dependencies needed to understand the change are different matters. Reference: Git documentation, [git-cherry-pick](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-cherry-pick).

Human experience makes this more complicated still. A lesson learned from failure and the fear felt at the time cannot necessarily be extracted as neatly separate parts. Trying to discard fear might also weaken awareness of danger. Trying to preserve learning might spread wariness of one person across the whole world.

This is why provenance -- a clue for returning to origins -- matters. If we can see which experience produced a conclusion, what grounds changed it, and under which conditions it failed, we can receive it again as a conditional judgment rather than an absolute command.

Knowing the provenance does not, however, make the judgment correct. Judgments based on mistaken premises have origins too, and records themselves may have been tidied afterward. Provenance is not proof of correctness. It is an entrance for re-examining correctness.

> Note 10: W3C's PROV-DM treats provenance as information about entities, activities, and agents involved in producing data or things. It supplies material for evaluating quality and trustworthiness, but does not by itself guarantee the truth of recorded content. Reference: W3C, [PROV-DM: The PROV Data Model](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/).

### 16. I do not know whether the soul implements it, so I write it to a file

Let me insert one unsettling joke. If a soul exists, does it come with a `--preserve-provenance` option? If something remains, but only kindness or wariness remains while its origin falls away, the inheritance could be troublesome indeed.

This paragraph is, of course, fiction. I cannot confirm either that a soul implements such a function or that it does not. It therefore supplies no reason to introduce an unknown memory medium into an explanation of the mind's present workings. Still less can the metaphor support a prospect of resuming one's life after losing the body.

But the problem survives the joke. When people receive lessons from others, they may not receive the circumstances in which the lessons arose. At work, we may be told "it has always been done this way" without knowing what the procedure was intended to avoid. No afterlife is needed: judgments that have lost their sources are already being passed around on this side.

If so, at least artificial clues can be preserved. Beside a rule, write down the problem that gave rise to it. In a record of withdrawal, include not merely an overall evaluation of the other person but the exchanges from which continuation came to seem difficult. If the present interpretation differs from the record made at the time, preserve that difference as a revision.

The absence of a specification for the soul is not a reason we cannot create a file. The conclusion is decidedly mundane. But writing to an available record fits this discussion better than relying on an unconfirmed storage destination.

### 17. Do not make preserving history a new burden

Recording everything is not the answer either. Records cost resources; reading them again may itself be burdensome. Histories involving other people cannot be made public merely because I want to preserve them. Recording and publishing must also be separated.

Above all, an argument for protecting a finite body must not generate a new obligation to explain all that body's experience without omission. What is needed is not a complete archive of a life, but clues sufficient to re-examine important judgments to the extent necessary. Missing records are not evidence that suffering never occurred.

The past need not be the HEAD that constantly governs current judgment. It may be enough to keep it somewhere one can return to when needed while continuing to live. If only later recollections can be written down, that too can be recorded as a condition of the record. Inability to reconstruct completely does not mean nothing can be preserved.

There is also the freedom to leave nothing behind. People do not live only to bequeath ideas. The value of records is not something to maximize unconditionally at the expense of the person's life. I must return to my own desire to record the limit I asked Business to respect.

## Part IV: Final Considerations -- A Structure That Criticizes Structures Also Needs a Return Path
### 18. Responsibility after simplification runs through all three questions

The three questions share an operation. Business selects aspects needed for transactions to connect human activities. The natural-disaster designation temporarily narrows a broad understanding of another person into a prediction useful for current contact. Inheriting a lesson extracts a reusable judgment from a long experience.

Each enables action by omitting some of a complex object. In that sense, simplification is not merely an error to avoid. Finite human beings could not live if they had to understand everything, remember everything, and respond to everything before acting.

But this convenience has boundaries. The aspects omitted for a transaction do not cease to exist. The inner life we stop investigating in order to withdraw has not been explained. The formative circumstances removed from a lesson have not been proved unnecessary.

The shared danger is that **a simplified model made for handling something acquires authority to speak for its object as a whole**. Precisely because the model works locally, what it cannot see begins to appear as a defect in the object. Burdens absent from numbers, another person who does not satisfy me, and a past that does not fit the current lesson become the things expected to change.

That is where a return path is needed. This does not mean restoring everything to its original state or recovering all past information. It means that when something fails to fit the model, there is a route for changing the model's conditions or scope rather than correcting only the object.

> Note 11: "Compression," "residual," and "return path" here place shared structural questions across three domains. They do not assert an identical information-theoretic encoding scheme or a thermodynamic conservation law. Omitted differences are not guaranteed to be preserved or to return to the original subject. The possibility of irrecoverable loss is precisely why advance protection also matters.

### 19. You criticize Business for turning people into variables, while turning people into disasters yourself?

Here comes the clearest objection. If I criticize Business for treating people as numbers or reactions, then treat someone who does not respond to explanation as a natural disaster, am I not doing the same thing?

The observation that part of the operation is shared is correct. Both stop pursuing the other person's whole being and handle only certain aspects. My own model cannot therefore be justified unconditionally by goodwill or an experience of harm.

Yet sharing an operation and bearing the same responsibility are different matters. Simplifying someone in order to manage their time or behavior differs from simplifying in order to reduce my own contact: the scope of effects on others differs. In the former case, the model may restrict the other person's options. The latter also has effects, but centrally limits where I spend my own resources of judgment.

The distinction cannot rest on "I was hurt, so I am right." Evaluation must consider what the judgment is intended to change, whose domain the change reaches, who pays its costs, and where objections can be handled.

If natural-disaster designation is used to damage someone's reputation, exclude them from a group, or withdraw necessary support, it is no longer confined to personal retreat. As a judgment altering someone else's life, it needs different grounds and procedures. Conversely, where Business limits its scope, receives demands for correction from affected people, and can alter operations accordingly, abstraction itself need not be a reason to reject it.

This distinction avoids casting Business as the villain while exempting only the withdrawing self. The question is not whether abstraction occurs, but **how far that abstraction moves other people, and at what point I undertake the costs of my own judgment**.

### 20. Ethics does not follow automatically from bodily finitude

This essay has emphasized bodily finitude, but the fact of being finite does not uniquely determine which values should take priority. Some people want rest because life is finite; others want to attempt something difficult for precisely that reason. Choosing to undertake a burden for another person is not thereby invalidated.

The essay therefore adopts a normative position: the conditions under which people can maintain their bodies and lives and reconsider how much burden to undertake should not be exhausted merely to suit someone else's optimization. That is not the discovery of a natural law. It is a value judgment for which this essay takes responsibility.

Adopting it does not eliminate every conflict. Who undertakes necessary work, how the burdens of those who exit are distributed among those who remain, and how far public responsibilities extend remain open questions. Providing a return path does not guarantee the outcome everyone wants.

Even so, we can ask whether the costs of keeping a structure running should be fixed onto those unable to decide whether it continues. When decision-makers do not receive the consequences and those receiving the consequences cannot change the decision, increasing competence or goodwill may leave the asymmetry intact.

Nor does stating provenance and purpose make every structure legitimate. Where an actor knowingly pursues profit through others' exhaustion, the problem is not forgetting but the selected purpose and distribution of burdens themselves. A return path is a condition for examining validity, not a sufficient condition of validity. It cannot become a procedural license under which everything is permitted once an objection has been heard and rejected.

I also want to use "the body is capital" only within this limit. I do not mean to reduce the body to an income-producing asset. A person may protect their body even when it produces nothing. If resting requires proof of reduced productivity, bodily protection has itself been subordinated to Business indicators.

### 21. Nor can the answer simply be "add more return paths"

Return paths have costs. If every objection must be considered, every past event reread, and every purpose decided again, nothing can be carried out. Adding audits may only increase the burden on the workplace. A mechanism intended to support judgment can exhaust judgment instead.

This essay therefore does not demand constant self-reflection or unlimited response. A range may be left to routine procedures, with re-examination triggered only by relevant changes. What individuals cannot handle may be entrusted to records or third parties. What matters is not keeping every question continually open, but retaining the reasons for closure and the conditions under which it can be changed when necessary.

In personal withdrawal, a policy of not resuming contact can coexist with respecting a boundary by not circulating one's image of the other person as settled fact. By contrast, an institution affecting many people cannot easily justify continuing to externalize long-term disadvantages solely by saying it lacks time for review. Responsibility for return is not imposed in the same measure on individuals and institutions.

Optimization reappears here. We must decide what to review, what to hold for now, and where to allocate resources. Its sharing a form with Business does not break the argument. I am not trying to prohibit optimization; I am trying to prevent its purposes and allocation of costs from becoming uncorrectable.

**Create stopping conditions under which people can live, between endlessly questioning everything and being unable to question anything again.** That is this essay's practical task.

### 22. From where can this essay be challenged?

At this point there are naturally places to object. I may have grouped Business too broadly into one tendency. The judgment that coordination is impossible may excuse inadequate explanations on my part. I may have underestimated the burden of preserving provenance relative to its usefulness.

But simply saying "there are exceptions," "it is only a metaphor," or "that is subjective too" does not yet show which claim has failed. The existence of companies that pursue profit while developing people does not refute the observation that profit fails to exhaust social losses. Our inability to understand others completely does not remove the need to choose modes of contact under finite resources.

Yet adding qualifications does not make the argument invulnerable. If saying "in some cases" makes every objection impossible, it also becomes unclear what has been explained. In an actual application, the conditions used to judge should be stated in advance, together with what was expected under those conditions.

For example, if I judged a relationship difficult to coordinate, but the other person was in fact respecting my request and I ignored their change, the application judgment is mistaken. If I criticized an indicator for leaving out an important burden but that burden was already handled elsewhere, the criticism must change too. If preserving history was expected to improve judgment but instead entrenched preconceptions that obstructed needed correction, the way of preserving it needs re-examination.

A challenge to the center of this essay could ask in which range preserving origins and paths of correction works better than alternatives. If equivalent protection can be obtained at lower cost, I lose my reason to insist on a complicated mechanism. Conversely, if the compression or withdrawal recommended here broadly destroys opportunities for coordination that could have been retained, the method harms what it seeks to protect.

Someone who thinks seriously may be able to kill this argument. That does not mean only someone who has thought seriously is entitled to object. One short counterexample can be fatal if it severs a central connection. What decides is the content of the objection, not the critic's credentials or time invested.

I want the writing to be dangerous in the sense that a careless objection may also expose the objector's premises. I do not certify it as so powerful that every critic must be injured. I answer for the claims I put forward. This essay has no privilege beyond that.

> Note 12: "Kill" and "fatal" here refer to rejecting arguments or hypotheses. The force of the metaphor is not intended to intimidate critics or to treat objection itself as evidence of malice or incapacity.

### 23. Aren't you the natural disaster?

And finally this criticism comes:

"If you demand premises and grounds for everything and keep turning other people's explanations back into structural problems, won't people around you treat you as the natural disaster?"

That is possible. Even a question important to me need not be undertaken by the other person at that moment. If I demand explanations until I am satisfied and judge someone incapable of updating when no answer comes, I am making the burden of mutual understanding unlimited for them.

A person who has a theory has a distinctive escape route. If the other person objects, I can explain that their premises differ. If they are silent, I can say they severed the connection. If they become angry, I can call it defense of their position. If every possible response confirms the theory, that theory has lost a route by which reality can correct it.

This cannot be solved merely by adopting a gentler manner. I must retain conditions under which my interpretation counts as mistaken, and the other person's wishes and disadvantages must be considered even if they do not adopt my explanation. Agreement with the theory must not become an admission ticket to treatment as an equal.

On the other hand, someone's withdrawing from dialogue with me does not by itself make this essay's propositions false. Their allocation of time and the validity of my argument are separate matters. I undertake responses to criticism to the extent needed, without imposing on either myself or the other person an obligation to persuade everyone completely.

If I use natural-disaster designation, I accept that it may be applied to me as well. I do not turn that into a self-image of being special because I am not understood. Unless I can respect even the freedom not to read this argument, my claim to protect others' boundaries stops where it suits me.

### 24. Final consideration: Connecting stopping to the next judgment

Across the three questions, a problem has come into view: people not only make structures but have their ways of judging changed by them. An indicator used in Business eventually narrows what counts as reality. An image of another person made for self-protection eventually becomes a permanent verdict. A lesson taken from experience eventually becomes an immovable rule for reading experience.

None of these needs to begin with a bad purpose. They remain because they worked under limited conditions, become obvious because they remain, and lose sight of their formative circumstances because they are obvious. When new reality no longer fits, reality is judged inappropriate instead of the structure being revised.

That is the state I want to call "correctness on autopilot." Automation itself is not the target. Entrusting part of judgment to procedures is necessary. The danger is taking the continuing operation of a procedure as evidence that the reasons for continuing it have already been examined.

Business therefore needs a path returning the costs omitted from its success to the way decisions are made. Withdrawal from a relationship needs a distinction through which one can protect oneself without settling the other person's essence. Inheriting thought needs provenance not for believing conclusions unchanged, but for discarding them again when necessary. The tools differ, but each prevents present viability from being promoted without permission into ultimate legitimacy.

Nor may this work be pushed back onto individual endurance alone. If the person who sees a structural problem must also pay alone for changing it, understanding becomes another entrance to exploitation. Allowing withdrawal and abandoning improvement are not the same. Even after an individual leaves, records or other actors may sometimes carry the problem onward.

But a person need not use up their body to leave that record. Mental effects remaining in others do not mean there is a replacement for the present self. Writing left behind does not restore the lost person's life as it was.

What I want to leave is not a society in which everyone questions forever. I want a society with more areas where one can safely proceed without thinking, while retaining the ability to think again when something overlooked appears. Equally, I do not want a society in which those without the capacity for re-examination cannot escape until they prove their correctness.

This position includes both stopping and reopening. Withdraw from work whose reason for continuing has been lost, correct failed judgments, and let go of lessons that have served their purpose. If the reasons for reaching those points can be preserved to a feasible extent, the next person can not only follow, but also begin elsewhere.

**Stopping is not necessarily abandoning correctness. It can also mean retaining what was stopped so that judgment can begin again under other conditions.**

I want to place this essay as no more than that. Do not apply it to people solely because it is convenient. Do not erase the burdens it points toward solely because it contains dangerous words. If using it shows a difference, return where that difference lies. But no one need keep being depleted in order to provide that response.

Bodies are finite, judgments have conditions, and words left behind act beyond their authors' intentions. Making mechanisms that work is therefore not enough. **We must also pass on the conditions under which those mechanisms may be stopped.**
