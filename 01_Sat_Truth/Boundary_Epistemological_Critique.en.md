# Boundary Epistemological Critique
## Conditions for the Formation of Knowledge through Operational Correspondence, Collation, and Residual Retention

> Status: Draft
> Scope: boundary-epistemology
> Language: en
> Claim strength: S2/S3

This text redefines the conditions under which knowledge forms through boundary, history, internal and external heuristics, operational correspondence, collation, residual retention, and re-collatability.

It is not an attempt to authorize the system through philosopher names. Existing traditions are handled in the annotation file as acts of collation, not as authority claims.

For general translation and terminology policy, see [`../Translation_Note.md`](../90_Repository_Governance/Translation_Note.md), [`../Publication_and_Commensuration_Policy.md`](../90_Repository_Governance/Publication_and_Commensuration_Policy.md), and the term collision registries.

---

## 0. Position of This Text

0.1 This text does not reduce knowledge either to cognitive forms internal to the subject or to simple correspondence with external facts.

0.2 Knowledge is formed when a history-bearing entity receives input from the outside, processes it internally, enters into operational correspondence with the outside, collates its correspondences and non-correspondences, and maintains the next capacity for judgment while retaining what cannot yet be closed.

0.2.1 Therefore, cognition is not the act of copying the world as it is.

0.2.2 Nor is cognition the arbitrary construction of the subject alone.

0.3 Cognition is the process in which, at a boundary, internal heuristics and external heuristics come into contact, enter into operational correspondence, are collated, and are updated while leaving residuals.

0.3.1 This text asks after the conditions of formation, scope of application, and failure conditions of that process.

0.3.1.1 In this sense, this text is a Boundary Epistemological Critique.

0.3.2 Boundary Epistemological Critique is not an observational theory whose aim is to make everything observable.

0.3.3 Boundary Epistemological Critique asks how, within finite observational systems, what leaks out of observation, classification, operational correspondence, and collation can be retained as residuals while preserving the capacity for re-collation.

## 1. The World Appears Not as a Collection of Isolated Objects, but as Contact between History-Bearing Fields

1.1 Existence is not a mere thing.

1.2 Existence bears history.

1.2.1 History is not mere pastness; it is what changes the next reception, reaction, judgment, and meaning-attribution.

1.3 Existence comes into contact with the outside as a history-bearing field.

1.4 Contact occurs at a boundary.

1.4.1 A boundary is not a defensive wall.

1.4.2 A boundary is the surface where external input and internal history come into contact, and where passage, suspension, blocking, re-encoding, reflection, and updating occur.

1.5 Therefore, cognition cannot form without a boundary.

1.5.1 Cognition without a boundary is swallowed by the outside.

1.5.2 Cognition with a boundary that is too weak cannot distinguish external input from internal history.

1.5.3 Cognition with a boundary that is too strong rejects external updating and turns internal heuristics into doctrine.

1.5.4 Cognition with a boundary that is too rigid closes itself inward.

1.6 Boundary Epistemological Critique does not ask how to erase boundaries. It asks about the position, hardness, opening and closing, and residual-retention conditions of boundaries.

## 2. Sensibility Is the Receptive Heuristic of a History-Field

2.1 Sensibility is the receptive structure that determines what rises as signal when external input reaches a history-field.

2.2 What becomes visible changes according to history.

2.3 What becomes audible also changes according to history.

2.4 What remains as discomfort also changes according to history.

2.5 Therefore, sensibility is not a merely passive vessel.

2.6 Sensibility is a receptive heuristic grounded in history.

2.6.1 Sensibility does not receive external input as it is; it cuts it into a receivable form according to internal history.

2.6.1.1 What is cut out here becomes the material of experience.

2.6.1.2 Yet what is not cut out does not thereby disappear.

2.6.1.3 It may remain as noise, discomfort, residual, or unprocessed history.

2.7 The study of sensibility is not sufficient if it asks only what appears.

2.8 The study of sensibility must also ask what continues to operate without appearing.

2.9 The failure of sensibility consists in mistaking what cannot be received for what does not exist.

2.10 The excess of sensibility consists in receiving every noise as meaning and overheating the boundary.

## 3. Understanding Is the Classificatory Heuristic of a History-Field

3.1 Understanding is the classificatory structure that converts input into an object capable of judgment.

3.2 Classification is not mere naming.

3.3 Classification determines what is treated as object, what as background, what as relation, what as cause, what as responsibility, and what as value.

3.4 The way classification occurs depends on historical structure.

3.4.1 The same input can form as a different object in different history-fields.

3.4.2 Therefore, categories are reconstructed not as a fixed table, but as classificatory operators used by a history-field for external operational correspondence.

3.5 Understanding converts input cut out by sensibility into a structure capable of judgment.

3.5.1 However, understanding is not omnipotent.

3.5.2 When what cannot be classified is forcibly classified, misrecognition occurs.

3.5.3 When what cannot be classified is treated as non-existent, historical cutoff occurs.

3.6 The study of understanding is not sufficient if it asks only about successful classification.

3.7 The study of understanding must ask what residuals are generated by non-classifiability.

3.8 Non-classifiability may be a failure.

3.9 Non-classifiability may demand a new form of classification.

3.10 The runaway condition of understanding consists in mistaking only what can be classified for existence.

## 4. Sensibility and Understanding Are Studied as Formal Spatial Theory

4.1 Sensibility and understanding are not merely psychological faculties.

4.2 Sensibility asks in what formal space external input rises as signal.

4.3 Understanding asks in what formal space the signal that has risen is classified as object, relation, causality, quantity, quality, and meaning.

4.4 Therefore, the theory of sensibility and the theory of understanding require a theory of formal spaces.

4.5 The formal spaces in question include Hilbert space, rigged Hilbert space, entropy space, and the corresponding spaces of observation, classification, and meaning-generation.

4.5.1 Hilbert space is a formal space for thinking state, projection, and observational cross-section.

4.5.1.1 The interest of Boundary Epistemological Critique in Hilbert space is not the recalculation of physical states, but what becomes state-like and what leaks from projection.

4.5.2 Rigged Hilbert space is an extended space for handling generalized states, boundary states, and continuous objects that are difficult to close within an ordinary state space.

4.5.2.1 The interest of Boundary Epistemological Critique in rigged Hilbert space lies in how to handle what is difficult to close as a normalized state, or what does not stably fit within an observational cross-section.

4.5.3 Entropy space is a space for handling distribution, dissipation, compression, noise, and unintegrated residuals.

4.5.3.1 The interest of Boundary Epistemological Critique in entropy space is not to erase noise, but to read what boundary mismatch the noise indicates.

4.6 What matters for Boundary Epistemological Critique is not how these spaces handle energy.

4.6.1 That is the work of physics.

4.6.2 What Boundary Epistemological Critique should trace is what each formal space makes state-like, what it makes observable, what it makes non-classifiable, and what it lets leak as noise, residual, or semantic pressure.

4.7 Therefore, Scientific Ontology does not deal with spacetime alone.

4.7.1 Scientific Ontology deals with external spacetime, intrinsic time, entropy time, and the logical-depth axis.

4.7.1.1 External spacetime is the physical and social order of shared time and space.

4.7.1.2 Intrinsic time is the order in which meaning, reaction, and residual are updated within a history-field.

4.7.1.3 Entropy time is the processing direction in which differences, noise, and unintegrated residuals move toward closure, suspension, re-encoding, or cutoff.

4.7.1.4 The logical-depth axis is not the before and after of external time, but the depth direction in which unresolved problems, contradictions, branches, residuals, and semantic pressure can be retained and reconstructed.

4.8 The main object of inquiry in Scientific Ontology is how cognition, meaning, observation, noise, and residuals arise at the intersection of these formal spaces and temporal axes.

4.9 This chapter is a detailed research branch extending from Boundary Epistemological Critique, and will be developed separately as spatial theory, temporal theory, and boundary-noise theory.

## 5. Reason Is the Integrative Heuristic That Closes Internal Loops

5.1 Reason is the force that attempts to integrate unclosed input, classification, and judgment into a larger internal loop.

5.2 Reason seeks coherent explanation.

5.3 Reason seeks grounds.

5.4 Reason seeks a worldview.

5.5 Reason wants to close what remains unresolved.

5.6 Therefore, reason is useful.

5.7 However, reason is also dangerous.

5.8 When reason tries to close an internal loop beyond the range of external operational correspondence, false closure occurs.

5.8.1 False closure is the operation of treating what remains unresolved as if it had been resolved.

5.8.2 The runaway condition of reason is the state in which the desire for internal loop-closure has lost external operational correspondence and collation.

5.9 Critique does not mean the negation of reason.

5.10 Critique means distinguishing the range in which reason may legitimately close from the range in which it must not close.

5.11 The maturity of reason consists in possessing the power to close while being able to retain residuals that must not be closed.

5.12 The immaturity of reason consists in disliking what remains unclosed and falsely closing residuals.

## 6. Law Is a Highly Stable Heuristic Capable of External Operational Correspondence

6.1 A law is not an absolute command given from the outside.

6.2 Nor is a law a merely subjective assumption.

6.3 A law is a highly stable heuristic system that possesses the capacity for operational correspondence with external experience.

6.4 A heuristic is a closed understanding that compresses experience into a form usable for the next judgment.

6.4.1 A private heuristic is closed within an individual's experience.

6.4.2 A communal heuristic stabilizes within a community.

6.4.3 A scientific heuristic increases its capacity for correspondence with the outside through observation, measurement, reproduction, falsification, and updating.

6.4.3.1 Physical law is, among these, a legal system with extremely high correspondence precision within external spacetime.

6.4.3.2 Therefore, science need not be denied.

6.4.3.3 Nor must science be turned into a god.

6.5 Laws have scope conditions.

6.5.1 A law that has lost its scope conditions becomes doctrine.

6.5.2 The critique of a system of laws asks after the conditions of formation, correspondence conditions, scope conditions, and update conditions of those laws.

6.6 Boundary Epistemological Critique does not weaken laws.

6.7 Boundary Epistemological Critique distinguishes the range in which laws work legitimately from the range in which laws render residuals invisible.

## 7. Operational Correspondence Is the Practical Alignment between Internal and External Heuristics

7.1 An internal heuristic is the internal structure by which a history-field receives, classifies, and judges the outside.

7.2 An external heuristic is a judgment structure externalized as observation, experiment, language, mathematics, institution, literature, AI, or communal verification.

7.3 Operational correspondence is the condition in which internal heuristics and external heuristics stably correspond in practice.

7.3.1 Operational correspondence is a condition for function.

7.3.2 What corresponds operationally can be used.

7.3.3 What corresponds operationally can be predicted.

7.3.4 What corresponds operationally is easier to share with others.

7.3.5 However, operational correspondence alone does not complete knowledge.

7.3.6 Operational correspondence does not explain the meaning of agreement.

7.3.7 Operational correspondence does not explain the meaning of disagreement.

7.3.8 Operational correspondence does not explain what residuals indicate.

7.4 Therefore, operational correspondence requires collation.

7.5 If only operational correspondence is emphasized, only what functions appears to be true.

7.6 If only operational correspondence is emphasized, what does not function, what has not yet entered a functional form, and what has been pushed outside function are discarded as residuals.

## 8. Collation Is the Operation That Gives Meaning to Correspondence, Non-Correspondence, and Residuals

8.1 Collation is the operation of reading not merely whether operational correspondence succeeds, but what its agreements, disagreements, and residuals mean.

8.2 Collation is not mere comparison.

8.3 Collation asks in which history-field, with which external heuristic, and under what conditions something corresponds.

8.4 Collation does not immediately discard disagreement as simple error.

8.4.1 Disagreement may be error.

8.4.2 Disagreement may indicate that something lies outside the scope of application.

8.4.3 Disagreement may demand a new classification.

8.4.4 Disagreement may demand an update of internal heuristics.

8.4.5 Disagreement may indicate the limit of an external heuristic.

8.5 A residual is not a failure.

8.5.1 A residual is unprocessed meaning.

8.5.2 A residual is the source of the next question.

8.6 Collation retains residuals as resources of meaning.

8.7 If collation alone is absolutized, it attempts to close every residual as some kind of meaning.

8.8 The excess of collation is not residual retention, but the coercion of residuals into meaning.

## 9. Residual Retention and Residue Formation Are Hidden Themes of Boundary Epistemological Critique

9.1 Knowledge is not closed by operational correspondence and collation alone.

9.2 Operational correspondence gives a practical alignment between internal and external heuristics.

9.3 Collation gives meaning to that correspondence, non-correspondence, and residual.

9.4 However, not everything collated can be closed.

9.4.1 What cannot be closed remains as residual.

9.5 A residual is not a mere unresolved problem.

9.5.1 A residual is a still re-collatable difference that appears in the process of operational correspondence, collation, classification, and observation.

9.5.2 A residue is a historical sediment left after processing, classification, institutionalization, exchange, or explanation, often made invisible while still continuing to act upon the field.

9.5.3 A residual is retained as a question.

9.5.4 A residue accumulates as debt, discomfort, pollution, distortion, and semantic pressure.

9.6 A residual may continue to act upon a field while being unprocessed, or while being treated as if it had been processed, in the course of observation, meaning-formation, classification, institutionalization, recording, exchange, and explanation.

9.6.1 A residual may be an oversight.

9.6.2 A residual may be repression.

9.6.3 A residual may be externalized debt.

9.6.4 A residual may be the source of a future question.

9.6.5 A residual may be semantic pressure that cannot yet be collated within the current correspondence system.

9.7 Therefore, residuals must not be immediately discarded.

9.8 However, not all residuals should be retained.

9.9 To retain all residuals is to return cognition to chaos.

9.10 Residual retention is a boundary operation for neither closing nor discarding residuals, while also preventing them from flooding without limit.

9.11 New heuristics often arise as a result of residual retention and re-collation.

9.11.1 Emergence is not appearance from nothing; it is the demand for a new form of classification by residuals that could not be closed within an existing classificatory system.

9.12 A lie is not a mere false proposition.

9.12.1 A lie is an operation that cuts off the path of collation and blocks access to residuals.

9.13 Exploitation is an operation that maintains institutional correspondence while externalizing load, responsibility, pain, and future opportunity as residuals.

9.14 Environmental problems are problems in which the residues of nature, bodies, and future generations, excluded from economic correspondence, return as reactions of the field.

9.15 False closure by AI is the act of making unresolved residuals appear closed in answer-form.

9.15.1 Useful AI does not erase residuals; it preserves access paths to residuals.

9.16 The center of Boundary Epistemological Critique is not to observe everything.

9.16.1 The center of Boundary Epistemological Critique is to ask under what conditions the unobservable, the unobserved, what changes when observed, and what leaks from observational systems can be handled as residuals.

9.17 To know is not to eliminate residuals.

9.17.1 To know is to retain residuals while preserving the capacity for judgment.

## 10. Truth-Bearing Propositions Have Semantics

10.1 A proposition does not end with being simply true or false.

10.2 A proposition has a field in which it is true.

10.2.1 A proposition has conditions under which it is true.

10.2.2 A proposition has an external heuristic with which it corresponds.

10.2.3 A proposition has an internal heuristic that it activates.

10.3 A proposition has what it leaves as residual.

10.4 Therefore, truth-bearing propositions have semantics.

10.5 Truth is not only the simple correspondence of a proposition to a fact.

10.5.1 Truth is the state in which a proposition is collated, within a specific history-field, with a specific external heuristic, while preserving the capacity for judgment.

10.6 Falsity is not a mere disagreement.

10.6.1 Falsity appears as failure of operational correspondence, failure of collation, scope transgression, classificatory failure, or false closure.

10.7 The unresolved is not false.

10.7.1 The unresolved is a state in which collation has not closed.

10.8 What matters for knowledge is not only truth and falsity, but the arrangement of truth, falsity, meaning, residual, and updateability.

10.9 When access paths to residuals are cut off from truth-evaluation, a proposition may function institutionally while deteriorating semantically.

## 11. Scholarship Is the Practice of Articulating Operational Correspondence and Collation and Making Them Re-Collatable

11.1 Scholarship is not a repository of knowledge.

11.2 Scholarship is the practice of attempting operational correspondence between internal and external heuristics.

11.2.1 However, operational correspondence alone does not become scholarship.

11.2.1.1 Scholarship collates agreements, disagreements, and residuals within operational correspondence.

11.2.1.2 Scholarship articulates the procedure of that collation in language.

11.2.1.3 Scholarship gives symbolic form to the procedure of that collation.

11.2.1.4 Scholarship methodizes the procedure of that collation.

11.2.1.5 Scholarship preserves the procedure of that collation in a form that others can re-collate.

11.3 Therefore, scholarship is an inheritable form of operational correspondence and collation.

11.3.1 Science is a scholarly form with high strength of external operational correspondence and re-collation.

11.3.2 Philosophy is a scholarly form that asks about the very conditions under which operational correspondence and collation form.

11.3.3 Metaphysics is a scholarly form that asks about the conditions before input, object, meaning, judgment, and existence arise.

11.3.4 The humanities are scholarly forms that handle the collation of meaning, interpretation, language games, and value within history-fields.

11.4 Scholarship in the age of AI is asked whether external memory can be brought into correspondence with internal understanding, and whether humans can retain the process of collation.

11.5 Scholarship is not an institution for eliminating residuals.

11.5.1 Scholarship is an institution for preserving residuals in a re-collatable form and passing them to future questions.

## 12. A Language Game Is the Stabilization of Collation Rules within a Community

12.1 Language does not merely carry meaning.

12.2 Language synchronizes classification.

12.3 Language synchronizes reaction.

12.4 Language synchronizes responsibility.

12.5 Language synchronizes what is called a problem, what is called an explanation, what is called proof, what is called a joke, and what is called an insult.

12.6 A language game is a set of collation rules stabilized within a community.

12.7 When language games differ, the same proposition activates different internal heuristics.

12.8 Therefore, misunderstanding is not a mere lack of information.

12.8.1 Misunderstanding occurs as a mismatch between collation rules.

12.9 Commensuration is the creation of a collatable interface between different language games.

12.9.1 Complete translation is unnecessary.

12.9.2 What is needed is to distinguish the core that must be preserved, the layer that can be transformed, and the residual that remains undecided.

12.9.3 Commensuration does not erase difference; it constructs a boundary surface capable of collation.

12.10 The failure of a language game consists in disposing of residuals as nonsense, irrationality, or non-existence, thereby destroying the capacity for collation.

12.10.1 When residuals are processed as humor, or handled as misunderstanding or error, the possibility of collation remains.

## 13. AI Is an Amplification Device for External Heuristics

13.1 AI accelerates external memory.

13.2 AI increases candidates.

13.3 AI summarizes, classifies, compares, and generates.

13.4 AI is not internal understanding itself.

13.5 AI is an amplification device for external heuristics.

13.6 If AI is used as a substitute for internal understanding, the judging subject becomes thinner.

13.7 If AI is used as an external heuristic and collated with internal understanding, the capacity for judgment increases.

13.8 The danger of AI is not only that it makes mistakes.

13.8.1 The danger of AI is that it makes the unresolved appear closed.

13.9 The usefulness of AI is not only that it gives answers.

13.9.1 The usefulness of AI lies in preserving the capacity for judgment.

13.10 Critical philosophy in the age of AI does not mean rejecting AI.

13.10.1 Critical philosophy in the age of AI asks how externally amplified heuristics produced by AI should be brought into operational correspondence and collation with internal understanding.

13.11 AI may also become an aid to residual retention.

13.11.1 AI may also become a device for false closure of residuals.

13.11.2 Useful AI is not AI that erases residuals, but AI that preserves access paths to residuals.

## 14. Boundary Epistemological Critique Asks after the Runaway Conditions of Knowledge

14.1 Knowledge can run away.

14.2 Sensibility may mistake only what can be received for the world.

14.3 Understanding may mistake only what can be classified for an object.

14.4 Reason may mistake only what can be closed for truth.

14.5 Science may mistake only what can be measured for reality.

14.6 Institutions may mistake only what can be recorded for responsibility.

14.7 Economics may mistake only what can be priced for value.

14.8 AI may mistake only what can be output for an answer.

14.9 These are all failures of boundary.

14.10 Boundary Epistemological Critique asks what is received, what is classified, what is closed, and what is retained as residual.

14.11 Boundary Epistemological Critique is not for weakening knowledge.

14.12 Boundary Epistemological Critique is for fixing the conditions under which knowledge works legitimately.

14.13 The attitude that operational correspondence alone is sufficient discards residuals.

14.14 The attitude that absolutizes collation forcibly closes residuals.

14.15 The attitude that discards both operational correspondence and collation lets residuals flood.

14.16 Chaos cut off from history is a state in which residuals have lost their points of connection.

14.17 Therefore, the runaway conditions of knowledge can be redescribed as failures in residual processing.

## 15. Conclusion

15.1 Knowledge cannot form from either the inside alone or the outside alone.

15.1.1 Knowledge functions through operational correspondence between internal and external heuristics.

15.2 Knowledge has meaning by collating the agreements, disagreements, and residuals of that correspondence.

15.3 Knowledge has updateability by retaining residuals and preserving the capacity for re-collation.

15.4 Experience is constituted at the boundary.

15.5 Sensibility and understanding are receptive and classificatory heuristics of a history-field.

15.6 The detailed study of sensibility and understanding develops as formal spatial theory, temporal theory, and boundary-noise theory.

15.7 Law is a highly stable heuristic capable of external operational correspondence.

15.8 Truth-bearing propositions have semantics.

15.9 Scholarship is the practice of preserving operational correspondence and collation in a re-collatable form.

15.10 A language game is a set of collation rules within a community.

15.11 AI amplifies external heuristics.

15.11.1 Therefore, in the age of AI, Boundary Epistemological Critique is necessary.

15.12 To know is not to possess the world.

15.12.1 To know is to come into contact at a boundary, enter into operational correspondence, collate, retain residuals, and open the next capacity for judgment.

## Appendix B. Translation Notes

### B.1 Boundary Epistemological Critique / 境界認識批判

Preferred term: **Boundary Epistemological Critique**
Short candidate: **Boundary Critique**
Explanatory phrase: **Critique of Boundary Cognition**

Note: `Boundary Critique` is short and strong, but weakens the epistemological context. The formal title should be `Boundary Epistemological Critique`. `Boundary Epistemic Critique` is intelligible, but in English it is stiffer and less stable as a system title than `Epistemological`.

---

### B.2 Operational Correspondence / 同期

Preferred term: **operational correspondence**
Secondary term: **synchronization**

Note: Synchronization here is not mere simultaneity. It includes physical synchronization, communicative alignment, social adjustment, and institutional correspondence. At first occurrence, use `operational correspondence`, and add `synchronization` where necessary.

---

### B.3 Collation / 照合

Preferred term: **collation**
Explanatory phrases: **semantic collation / cross-checking / semantic comparison**

Note: `Collation` works well as a system term, but may sound stiff to general readers. At first occurrence, explain it as semantic collation: the operation of reading what agreements, disagreements, and residuals in operational correspondence mean. Avoid translating it as `verification`, which narrows the meaning too much toward validation.

---

### B.4 Residual / 残差

Preferred term: **residual**
Explanatory phrases: **re-collatable residual / unresolved residual**

Note: A residual is a still re-collatable difference that appears through operational correspondence, collation, classification, and observation. Use `residual` when the remainder can still be handled as an object of operation.

---

### B.5 Residue / 残渣

Preferred term: **residue**
Emphatic phrase: **sedimented residue**

Note: A residue is a historical sediment left after processing, classification, institutionalization, exchange, or explanation, often made invisible while still acting upon the field. Use `residue` or `sedimented residue` for environmental load, extra-institutional debt, embodied discomfort, or the remains of exploitation.

Additional note: Even when treated as a pure virtual or imaginary component, do not immediately translate it as `pure imaginary component` in the main English text. Until mathematical definition is given, use a phrase such as `residue outside the current operational frame`.

---

### B.6 History / 履歴

Preferred term: **history**
Emphatic phrases: **accumulated history / history-bearing**

Note: History here is not mere pastness, but accumulation that changes the next reception, reaction, judgment, and meaning-attribution. Use phrases such as `history-bearing entity` or `history-bearing field` where needed.

---

### B.7 History-Field / 履歴場

Preferred term: **history-field**
Explanatory phrase: **history-bearing field**

Note: `Field` may be confused with a physical field. At first occurrence, explain it as `history-field, or history-bearing field`, and state that it is not identical to a field in standard physics.

---

### B.8 Internal Heuristic / External Heuristic / 内部ヒューリスティック / 外部ヒューリスティック

Preferred terms: **internal heuristic / external heuristic**

Note: A heuristic here is not a mere rule of thumb. It is a structure, sourced in history, that enables reception, classification, judgment, closure, and re-collation.

Additional note: False closure is an operation that borrows a structure resembling an existing closure and makes an unresolved gap appear filled. Because that completion ignores the history-field, it blocks access paths to residuals or residues.

---

### B.9 Capacity for Judgment / 判断可能性

Preferred term: **capacity for judgment**
Contextual candidates: **room for judgment / conditions for judgment**

Note: `Judgment-possibility` is too stiff. Use `capacity for judgment` when the ability of the judging subject is emphasized. Use `room for judgment` or `conditions for judgment` when the space, field, or conditions for judgment are emphasized.

---

### B.10 False Closure / 偽閉鎖

Preferred term: **false closure**

Note: False closure is the operation of treating what remains unresolved as if it had been resolved. It is a core term applicable to AI, reason, institutions, and language games.

---

### B.11 Semantic Pressure / 意味圧

Preferred term: **semantic pressure**
Explanatory phrase: **meaning pressure**

Note: `Semantic pressure` is preferred. It must be clearly stated that this is not physical pressure.

Additional note: Semantic pressure is the pressure by which an unclosed gap or residual demands a structure resembling an existing closure, prompting interpretation, classification, completion, or re-collation. If this pressure is processed while ignoring the history-field, false closure is likely to occur.

---

### B.12 Logical-Depth Axis / 論理-深度軸

Preferred term: **logical-depth axis**
Supplementary phrase: **logical-depth axis in the SO sense**

Note: Avoid identifying this with `logical depth` in computational theory. In Scientific Ontology, logical depth is the depth direction in which unresolved problems, contradictions, branches, residuals, and semantic pressure are retained and reconstructed.

Additional note: Where needed, it can be described as a direction distinct from external time: a quasi-temporal depth axis in which residuals, residues, and unclosed gaps accumulate. Until mathematically defined, avoid directly defining it as an imaginary axis.

---

### B.13 Commensuration / 通約

Preferred term: **commensuration**
Explanatory phrases: **construction of a comparable interface / making different frameworks comparable**

Note: This connects to the Kuhnian problem of commensurability. Here, however, commensuration does not mean complete translation. It means constructing a collatable interface between different language games, paradigms, or history-fields.

Additional note: `Bridge` may be used in some contexts, but it gives too strong an impression of successful connection. When emphasizing incomplete connection with residuals, `comparable interface` is preferable.

---

### B.14 Capacity for Re-Collation / 再照合可能性

Preferred term: **capacity for re-collation**
Candidates: **re-examinability / capacity for re-examination**

Note: `Re-collation` works well as a system term, but is stiff. In contexts of meaning, judgment, and scholarly institutions, use `re-examinability` or `capacity for re-examination` as needed.

Additional note: Capacity for re-collation is the condition under which once-closed propositions, judgments, classifications, or institutional processes can be collated again while preserving access paths to residuals and residues.
