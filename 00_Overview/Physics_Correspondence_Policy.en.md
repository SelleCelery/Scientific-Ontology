# Physics Correspondence Policy
### Why Scientific Ontology Approaches Physical Terminology

> Status: Policy  
> Scope: physical correspondence, physical-adjacent terminology, boundary realism, observation conditions  
> Language: en
> Claim strength: S2/S3; conceptual policy, not an empirical physics claim  
---

## 0. Positioning

This document defines the public policy for how Scientific Ontology treats terminology that approaches physics.

It is not merely a disclaimer.

The purpose of this policy is not only to state that Scientific Ontology is not physics. Its purpose is also to explain why Scientific Ontology cannot avoid approaching terms such as particle, field, time, interaction, path, entropy, entanglement, black hole, cosmic outer shell, and qualia.

Scientific Ontology does not approach physical vocabulary in order to replace contemporary physics.

Scientific Ontology addresses the conditions under which an object becomes available as an object: observation, history, boundary, meaning, communication, disconnection, and the possibility of collation. For that reason, it must structurally come into contact with terms in which observability, reachability, communicability, collatability, disconnectability, return paths, history, and residue are concentrated.

Within contemporary physics, some of these terms belong to measurement, calculation, conservation laws, symmetry, spacetime structure, and interaction. From the standpoint of Scientific Ontology, however, they also name boundary surfaces through which something appears as an object.

Accordingly, this policy defines why Scientific Ontology approaches physical terminology, what restrictions apply to that approach, how it remains non-identical with standard physics, and how strong hypotheses should be placed in Research Notes.

---

## 1. Central Thesis

The central thesis of this policy is as follows.

> Scientific Ontology does not replace standard physics.  
> However, because it addresses the conditions under which observation becomes possible, it inevitably comes into contact with physical terminology that names boundary phenomena.

More compactly:

> Contemporary physics and Scientific Ontology begin from different questions, yet they partially arrive at common boundary phenomena.

This thesis states two things at once.

First, Scientific Ontology is not an alternative theory to contemporary physics.

Contemporary physics precisely describes observable phenomena, measured values, conservation laws, symmetries, spacetime structures, and interactions. Scientific Ontology does not deny these achievements. Nor does it overwrite standard definitions in physics.

Second, Scientific Ontology questions the event of observation itself.

Science begins from observation. Scientific Ontology observes that observation: what becomes available as an observable object, where a boundary arises, what history remains, what communication is established, what disconnection occurs, and what form of collation becomes possible.

For this reason, Scientific Ontology cannot be unrelated to physics.

This does not mean that it enters physics in order to replace existing physical theories. Rather, once the conditions of observation are at issue, Scientific Ontology must structurally come into contact with the boundary surfaces of the observable world that physics investigates.

## 2. Not Science Itself, but Conditions that Include Scientific Observation

Scientific Ontology values methodological scientificity in the sense that it preserves observation, experiment, objection, collation, and reconsideration.

Yet Scientific Ontology is not science itself.

Nor is it a system whose purpose is simply to explain existing philosophy of science.

Its core character is metaphysical. More precisely, it is a metaphysics centered on ontology, while connecting epistemology, semantics, ethics, communication, history, and boundary.

Accordingly, the word “scientific” in Scientific Ontology does not mean institutional membership in science. It refers to a procedural orientation that does not abandon observability, refutability, collatability, residue preservation, and the possibility of re-examination.

Scientific Ontology does not attempt to explain the world from the same starting point as science.

Science describes the world through observable objects, measured values, reproducibility, and mathematical formulation.

Scientific Ontology asks under what conditions an object becomes observable, under what conditions something is extracted as a measured value, under what conditions reproducibility holds, and what is preserved or cut off by mathematization.

In this sense, Scientific Ontology does not deny science from outside. It collates, from another layer, the conditions of observation that include science itself.

## 3. Contact in the Process of Thought, Not Definition

When Scientific Ontology uses physical vocabulary, it is not attempting to take over standard definitions in physics.

Nor is it casually borrowing existing physical terms as mere metaphors.

Scientific Ontology must come into contact with physical vocabulary because it addresses conditions of observation, boundary, history, communication, disconnection, return paths, and collatability. Physical terms are not only calculative terms inside standard physics; they also mark boundary surfaces through which something appears as an object.

Thus, the use of physical vocabulary in Scientific Ontology should first be understood not as definition, but as contact within the process of thought.

This contact includes the following operations.

- Respecting standard definitions while reading the boundary surfaces that appear through them.
- Treating structural proximity rather than mathematically established identity.
- Preserving existing uses of a term while making SO-specific use explicit as a different layer.
- Observing what is preserved and what is cut off by definition.
- Refusing to eliminate in advance meanings that exceed the definition.

Definition is necessary.

However, definition is always also a boundary operation.

To define something is to make it available for treatment. At the same time, to define is to separate certain possibilities from the object.

Scientific Ontology makes explicit how a system is divided by definition. It also does not deny that unprocessed meanings, residues, and other ranges may remain outside the defined scope.

## 4. Treating Sentences, Not Bits

Scientific Ontology does not treat the world only as bits.

What Scientific Ontology treats is the sentence with history.

Here, “sentence” does not mean merely a linguistic sentence. It means a boundary-formed unit that includes history, contact, reaction, suspension, return path, disconnection, meaning generation, and unresolved residue.

A bit extracts difference.

A sentence includes the history from which a difference arises, the boundary at which it reacts, the return path it preserves, and the indeterminacy it leaves open.

To treat a sentence as a sentence sacrifices a considerable degree of simplification and computability achieved through quantitative reduction.

In exchange, it becomes possible to preserve history, meaning, reaction systems, and unresolved residues without prematurely excluding possible ranges.

This policy also applies to how Scientific Ontology handles physical vocabulary.

Terms such as particle, field, time, interaction, path, entropy, entanglement, black hole, cosmic outer shell, and qualia have definitions in their standard domains. At the same time, within SO, they may be reread in their range as sentences: as configurations of boundary, history, communication, disconnection, return path, and collatability.

This rereading does not overwrite standard definitions.

It is an operation of recollating, within SO, the boundary conditions that remain around an object after that object has been extracted by a standard definition.

## 5. Two Starting Points

Contemporary physics and Scientific Ontology do not begin from the same point.

Contemporary physics describes the world with high precision on the basis of measurable phenomena, observed values, mathematical formulation, conservation laws, symmetries, interactions, and spacetime structures.

Scientific Ontology does not take for granted that observable objects are already given. It attends to the conditions under which something rises as an object.

Those conditions include at least the following.

- Boundary
- Contact
- History
- Return
- Subsequent conditions
- Communicability
- Reachability
- Collatability
- Disconnectability
- Meaning generation
- Residue preservation
- Avoidance of false closure

This difference must not be read as a hierarchy.

Scientific Ontology does not claim to have superior authority over contemporary physics. Nor is contemporary physics a subfield of Scientific Ontology.

They have different questions.

Contemporary physics asks what lawful structures the observable world has.

Scientific Ontology asks what rises as an observable world and through what kinds of boundaries history, communication, meaning, and ethics arise.

Because of this difference, Scientific Ontology does not merely compete with physics. It collates, from another layer, concepts presupposed by physics: observation, object, boundary, measurement, interaction, and reachability.

## 6. Relation to Boundary Realism

Scientific Ontology is not a system that directly possesses being itself.

Nor is it a system that fully defines what lies behind the world as a completed substance.

What this system treats as real is boundary.

More precisely, it treats boundary, contact, history, return, and subsequent conditions.

What lies behind a boundary is not denied. However, it is not directly owned as an object within this system; it is retained in a thing-in-itself-like reserve.

Therefore, Scientific Ontology is not an axiomatic system that exhaustively describes being itself. It is a system for describing, collating, and operating contact, history, communication, meaning, and ethics as they appear at boundaries.

The same principle applies to physical vocabulary.

When Scientific Ontology uses terms such as particle, field, time, interaction, entropy, entanglement, and black hole, it does not thereby claim to possess or finally define the physical entities themselves.

What this system treats are the boundary phenomena that appear through those terms: observability, reachability, communicability, collatability, disconnectability, return paths, history, and residue.

For this reason, the use of physical vocabulary in Scientific Ontology does not erase standard definitions in physics.

It is an operation of rereading, from the side of Scientific Ontology, the boundary surfaces indicated by physical vocabulary.

## 7. Terminology Operation Principles

When Scientific Ontology treats physical-adjacent terminology, it follows these principles.

1. Terms in standard physics must be treated with respect for their standard definitions.
2. Each SO use must identify whether it belongs to standard definition, ontological rereading, structural analogy, conceptual model, projection, or working hypothesis.
3. It must not be presented as an empirically established proposition in standard physics.
4. Possible physical correspondence must not be confused with structural analogy.
5. Similarity must not be confused with identity.
6. Unresolved issues must not be treated as resolved.
7. SO use must not be treated as the full possible range of the term.
8. Strong connections to physics, cosmology, or consciousness theory should be placed in Research Notes, not in the Foundation layer.
9. Terms likely to collide with existing usage should be recorded in the Term Collision Registry.
10. Non-claim boundaries should be stated so that readers can collate objections, counterexamples, and limits of application.
11. Public restraint should be treated not as a weakening of theory, but as a sheath that preserves collatability.

The final point is important.

Scientific Ontology places a sheath on physical vocabulary not to flatter existing fields, and not because it requires institutional approval from them.

The sheath exists so that readers can collate what they are reading: what is a standard definition, what is an SO rereading, what is analogy, and what is a strong hypothesis.

Claim strength is managed not to weaken the theory, but to increase its public readability and criticizability.

## 8. Vocabulary Categories

Physical-adjacent terminology should be handled through at least the following categories.

| Category | Meaning | Public handling |
|---|---|---|
| Standard definition | A definition in contemporary physics or another established field | Respect it and do not overwrite it |
| Structural analogy | A use based on structural resemblance | Do not identify it with the original term |
| Ontological rereading | A use that rereads the term through boundary, history, communication, meaning, and disconnection | State that it is an SO use |
| Projection | A correspondence that appears when a broader history-field topology is restricted to spacetime, interaction, and observational sections | Do not confuse it with mathematical isomorphism or physical proof |
| Conceptual model | An explanatory scaffold inside SO | Do not present it as an empirically established theory |
| Formalization candidate | A research issue that may later be formalized | Place it in Research Notes |
| High-strength hypothesis | A hypothesis strongly connected to physics, cosmology, or consciousness theory | Restrict it to Research Notes and require non-claim boundaries |
| Sentence-like reading | A use that treats a defined term as a unit containing history, reaction, possibility, and residue | Preserve its irreducible range, but do not confuse it with the standard definition |

This classification is not meant to bind the expressive capacity of Scientific Ontology.

It is a boundary operation that keeps public text collatable for readers.

## 9. Treatment of Major Physical-Adjacent Terms

The descriptions in this section do not exhaust how these terms may be treated within Scientific Ontology.

Particle, field, time, interaction, path, entropy, entanglement, black hole, cosmic outer shell, and qualia each have established definitions and accumulated debates in their standard domains. At the same time, they may have multiple layers of reading within Scientific Ontology.

Therefore, the following descriptions are minimum public distinctions, not total SO definitions.

---

### 9.1 Particle

In Scientific Ontology, the term “particle” is not used to replace the particle concept in standard physics.

When Scientific Ontology approaches particles, it primarily treats reachability of action, observational section, closure of history, mediation of communication, and the presence or absence of a return path.

An SO particle may be read not merely as a point in external space, but as a model unit that carries boundary, history, and communicability.

Further, within SO, a particle can be read less as an isolated point-like entity and more as a communicative accumulation field.

In this sense, an SO particle may be read as a soliton-like structure rather than a particle in the narrow sense: a structure in which history, field, communication, reaction, and boundary conditions temporarily or stably accumulate and appear particle-like on an observational section.

This does not erase the standard physical definition of particle.

It is an SO-side rereading of boundary conditions concentrated in the term “particle.”

---

### 9.2 Field

In Scientific Ontology, the term “field” may not mean the physical field in the standard sense.

An SO field is a conceptual field that preserves history, reacts to input, synchronizes, desynchronizes, disconnects, and reconnects.

A field is not merely a background in which objects exist in isolation.

A field is a boundary configuration in which contact, history, communication, reaction, suspension, disconnection, and reconnection occur.

When the term `boson field` is used, it does not necessarily mean a boson field in the Standard Model.

Scientific Ontology may approach this term in order to treat mediation, transmission, synchronizability, communicability, and the field-like character of meaning generation.

A boson field in standard physics and a boson field in Scientific Ontology must always be distinguished.

---

### 9.3 Time and Intrinsic Time

Intrinsic time in Scientific Ontology is not clock time.

Nor is it identical with proper time in standard physics.

Intrinsic time is an internal order of updating: the order in which history is updated, suspended, folded back, and recollated.

When intrinsic time is connected to particles or cosmology in physical-adjacent documents, it should be treated as a high-strength conceptual model or a formalization candidate in Research Notes.

---

### 9.4 Interaction and Path

When Scientific Ontology uses the terms “interaction” and “path,” it does not replace interaction terms or path integrals in standard physics.

An SO interaction is a boundary process in which fields with history come into contact: what passes, what is reflected, what is suspended, what is disconnected, and what closes as meaning.

An SO path is not merely a spatial route.

It is a conceptual course through which action, history, communication, return path, and collatability are connected.

A path is not treated only as a single already-determined line.

Within SO, a path is treated as a sentence that includes possibility, suspension, branching, reconnection, disconnection, and subsequent conditions.

---

### 9.5 Entropy

Scientific Ontology does not arbitrarily detach the term “entropy” from its standard thermodynamic or information-theoretic definitions.

However, SO may read entropy as a condition of difference, diffusion, unresolved communication, residue formation, disconnection, and recoding.

In such cases, entropy in standard physics and entropic communication in Scientific Ontology must be distinguished.

Entropic communication in SO is not merely a quantity of information.

It concerns what remains unresolved, where difference diffuses, at which boundary recoding occurs, which return path is lost, and which residue acts as a subsequent condition.

---

### 9.6 Entanglement

Entanglement in standard quantum theory has its own definitions and mathematical structure.

Scientific Ontology does not replace them.

However, there is a clear reason why Scientific Ontology approaches the term entanglement.

History fields may retain correspondence after contact in a way that cannot be adequately described by external spacetime order or simple distance. A history may act upon distant fields of judgment, institution, memory, or meaning. Even when direct physical contact seems absent, correspondence may remain at the level of historical structure.

Such a state can be read within Scientific Ontology as a historical state in which communicability is retained in suspension.

This reading does not claim to have empirically explained quantum entanglement itself. It does not claim superluminal communication. It does not claim a violation of causality.

It is an SO-side reading of boundary conditions: non-separability, preservation of history, suspended communicability, and persistence of collatability.

---

### 9.7 Black Hole

A black hole in standard astrophysics has standard definitions and observational research.

When Scientific Ontology approaches the term black hole, it does not do so in order to replace the astrophysical black hole.

Within SO, black hole may be referenced as a boundary model for reachability, return paths, communicative disconnection, observational limits from outside, closure of history, and the relation to nothingness.

Here again, a black hole in standard astrophysics must not be confused with a black hole as a boundary model in Scientific Ontology.

---

### 9.8 Cosmic Outer Shell

The cosmic outer shell in Scientific Ontology or the PINGER Hypothesis does not mean a simple spatial wall.

It is a concept concerning reachability, folding-back, return paths, and boundary phase within the same history field.

This term may come into contact with the boundary of the observable universe or horizon concepts in standard cosmology, but it does not directly replace them.

The cosmic outer shell should be treated as a high-strength concept in Research Notes.

---

## 10. Qualia and the Treatment of Quality

Qualia is a term that may collide with multiple domains: philosophy of mind, cognitive science, neuroscience, AI, literature, religion, and everyday theories of experience.

Scientific Ontology uses the term qualia.

However, Scientific Ontology does not treat qualia simply as “quality.”

Quality in SO is not an independently given property.

Quality is a sign that appears at a boundary through the compression of a history-dependent reaction system.

When external input comes into contact with internal history, reacts, produces friction, is suspended, and reconnects, texture appears as the compressed form of that history-dependent reaction system.

Therefore, qualia in Scientific Ontology does not replace standard debates in philosophy of mind.

At the same time, the fact that philosophy of mind or cognitive science has used the term does not mean that any one field owns the full range of qualia.

Undetermined terms, boundary terms, and cross-domain terms are not the property of a single field.

Each system may use such terms if it states its own conditions of definition, range, and non-claim boundaries.

The problem is not the use of the term itself.

The problem is using a term while hiding what definition is being used, how far the claim extends, and what is not being claimed.

Accordingly, Scientific Ontology does not need to avoid the term qualia.

However, public use should state the limited SO usage.

Where necessary, qualia may be paraphrased as follows.

> Texture phenomena at a boundary  
> Contact friction between external input and internal history  
> Experiential energy phenomena arising at a boundary  
> A sign in which a history-dependent reaction system rises as quality at a contact surface  
> An experiential unit as sentence before reduction into bits

These paraphrases do not weaken the theoretical content.

They are boundary operations used when readers are likely to be pulled too quickly into existing qualia debates and thereby lose sight of the SO object.

## 11. Relation to Feynman Diagrams

When Scientific Ontology approaches Feynman diagrams, it does not do so in order to replace a calculative method in standard physics.

Feynman diagrams have a strict role in standard physics. They are notations for interaction, particle processes, perturbative expansion, and calculative correspondence. Scientific Ontology does not overwrite that role.

However, from the perspective of Scientific Ontology, the Feynman-diagrammatic idea contains structures that are highly important for boundary reading: where interactions occur, which paths are considered, what mediates, what correspondences hold, and what results appear on an observational section.

Scientific Ontology does not restrict this idea only to the calculative domain or to the moment of physical interaction.

Within SO, it approaches Feynman-diagrammatic thinking in order to treat paths, interactions, mediation, branching, suspension, disconnection, return paths, history, and observational sections as sentences.

What matters here is not reusing individual symbols as calculative notation in physics.

What matters is reading interaction not as an isolated event, but as an arrangement of path, mediation, possibility, history, and result.

Therefore, Feynman-diagrammatic reading in Scientific Ontology is not identical with Feynman diagrams in standard physics.

It is a conceptual contact for treating interaction and path as sentences within history-field topology.

## 12. Collation, Not Approval

Scientific Ontology does not depend on approval from existing fields in order to exist.

At the same time, it does not refuse dialogue with existing fields.

The purpose of publication is not to seek approval.

The purpose of publication is to present concepts in a collatable form so that counterexamples, criticism, misreadings, term collisions, and limits of application can later be reopened.

Criticism of Scientific Ontology is therefore welcome.

However, effective criticism must at least distinguish the following.

- Standard definitions in physics
- Definitions within Scientific Ontology
- Structural analogy
- Ontological rereading
- Projection
- Working hypothesis
- High-strength hypothesis in Research Notes
- Non-claim boundary
- Sentence-like reading

Merely pointing out that “this is not standard physics” is not sufficient as criticism of Scientific Ontology.

Scientific Ontology is not presented as standard physics in the first place.

Effective criticism must address the boundary conditions, claim strength, terminology management, and collatability that Scientific Ontology itself has specified.

## 13. Items to Place in Research Notes

The following should be placed not in the Foundation or README base layer, but in Research Notes.

- Hypotheses that strongly contact standard physical vocabulary
- Conceptual models approaching cosmology
- High-strength rereadings of particle, field, time, interaction, entropy, entanglement, and black hole
- Trials that treat particles as communicative accumulation fields or soliton-like structures
- Trials that extend Feynman-diagrammatic thinking into history-field topology
- Possible physical correspondences
- Mathematical formalization candidates
- Trials connecting consciousness theory or qualia theory with physical vocabulary
- Documents likely to be misread by standard-science readers as alternative physics claims

Even when placed in Research Notes, such documents should state the following.

1. Why the document belongs in Research Notes rather than in the ordinary public base layer.
2. What claim strength it carries.
3. What it does not claim.
4. Whether it is standard definition, SO definition, structural analogy, ontological rereading, projection, working hypothesis, or sentence-like reading.
5. What criticism, formalization, and collation are needed in the future.

This operation allows Scientific Ontology to preserve strong hypotheses without deleting them.

At the same time, it avoids overasserting them in the public base layer.

Research Notes are not a place to remove the fangs.

They are a place to sheath the fangs and indicate from which angles they should be examined.

## 14. Non-Claim Boundaries

This document does not claim the following.

- That Scientific Ontology replaces standard physics.
- That Scientific Ontology is an empirical theory superior to contemporary physics.
- That Scientific Ontology is science itself.
- That Scientific Ontology is completed as a subfield of existing philosophy of science.
- That existing definitions in physics should be overwritten by definitions in Scientific Ontology.
- That an SO boson field is identical with a boson field in the Standard Model.
- That SO intrinsic time is identical with proper time in standard physics.
- That an SO particle-as-soliton reading replaces the standard physical definition of particle.
- That SO entanglement has empirically explained quantum entanglement exhaustively.
- That an SO black-hole model replaces black holes in standard astrophysics.
- That the cosmic outer shell directly replaces the boundary of the observable universe in standard cosmology.
- That an SO reading of qualia has solved the qualia problem as empirical science.
- That a Feynman-diagrammatic reading in SO is identical with Feynman diagrams in standard physics.
- That scientific verification, mathematical formalization, or experimental collation are unnecessary.
- That criticism from existing fields is rejected.

This document does claim the following.

- Scientific Ontology treats the conditions under which observation becomes possible.
- Scientific Ontology values methodological scientificity, but is not science itself; it is a metaphysics centered on ontology.
- Because it treats conditions of observation, it inevitably comes into contact with physical vocabulary.
- This contact is managed not as replacement of standard physics, but as ontological rereading of boundary phenomena.
- SO terminology does not merely close meaning through definition; it also preserves history, reaction, possibility, and residue as sentence.
- Strong connections should be placed in Research Notes with stated claim strength and non-claim boundaries.
- Term collisions should be managed not by hiding them, but as public boundaries.

## 15. Public Conclusion

Scientific Ontology approaches physical vocabulary not in order to replace physics.

Scientific Ontology treats the conditions under which observation becomes possible.

To treat such conditions is to ask where an object becomes an object, at which boundary it comes into contact, what history it leaves, what communication it establishes, what disconnection it undergoes, and what collatability it preserves.

Some terms in physics are located precisely on this boundary surface.

For this reason, Scientific Ontology does not avoid physical vocabulary.

However, it does not overwrite standard definitions.

Scientific Ontology treats physical vocabulary through categories such as standard definition, structural analogy, ontological rereading, projection, conceptual model, formalization candidate, high-strength hypothesis, and sentence-like reading.

This policy is not a retreat of the theory.

It is a boundary operation for making Scientific Ontology public, criticizable, collatable, and capable of preserving unresolved residue.

Scientific Ontology does not approach physical vocabulary in order to seek approval.

Scientific Ontology approaches the boundary surface of physical vocabulary in order to observe observation.

## 17. Operational Notes

When adding new physical-adjacent terminology to a public document, confirm the following.

1. Does it respect the standard definition?
2. Does it make the SO use explicit?
3. Does it distinguish structural analogy, ontological rereading, projection, working hypothesis, and sentence-like reading?
4. Could it be overread as physical proof or empirical verification?
5. Is its claim strength such that it should belong in Research Notes?
6. Should it be registered in the Term Collision Registry?
7. Does it require a non-claim boundary?
8. Can readers collate criticism, counterexamples, and limits of application?
9. Does it make clear what is preserved and what is cut off by definition?
10. Does it avoid processing the term too much as a bit, while treating it as a sentence where necessary?

This check does not weaken the theory.

It is the minimum boundary management required for the concept to remain recollatable after publication.
