## 0. The shortest form

> **Events happen at boundaries.**
> **Neither simply subjective nor simply objective, but where different modes of description come into contact.**

Scientific Ontology does not end by explaining only an external world, nor by explaining only an interior self.

Something arrives from outside. A receiving history takes it in. A response is returned. That response acts on the outside. Further consequences return. Through this circulation, the receiving side, the external field, and the conditions of what may happen next are all altered.

The event is operationally located at that contact surface.

The central question is therefore not simply whether "the world is at fault" or "the self is at fault."

> **At which boundary did what occur, how far did an action reach, what returned, and where should responsibility be partitioned again?**

This is the shortest operational entrance used by this outline.

## 1. Do not explain everything from outside窶俳r from the self

When an unwelcome external event reaches a person, it can expose what that person fears, expects, rejects, lacks, or can no longer tolerate. But this does not mean that everything is the person's responsibility.

The reverse error is also possible. External conditions, institutions, other agents, chance, resource asymmetry, and bodily constraints cannot be erased by returning every problem to individual psychology.

Scientific Ontology therefore rejects both totalizations:

- collapsing external burdens into self-responsibility;
- exporting every modifiable part of the self into external responsibility.

Responsibility partition is not always knowable in advance. Action and return may be needed before the boundary can be redrawn.

> I acted this far.
> This is what returned.
> This far was within my modifiable range.
> Beyond this point, the result is not mine alone to own.

Responsibility can then be treated as an updateable boundary formed from contact history rather than as guilt or a wish for exemption.

---

## 2. Present-state affirmation and inertia

Human beings do not always enact what they understand. They can also construct explanations that protect a state in which they do not wish to move.

This outline does not reduce that problem to moral condemnation of laziness.

> **The critical problem is not inertia itself, but the point at which inertia acquires a theory of present-state preservation and uses that theory to close the possibility of update.**

The aim is not to demand a virtuous subject who can always exert effort. The design problem is to construct a terrain in which lower-cost movement does not automatically lead to contraction, but can lead toward mobility, learning, and renewed contact with the unknown.

---

## 3. Distinguishing endurance from training by contraction and expansion

Endurance is not treated as a virtue in itself.

> **Endurance may appear as a direction in which a boundary is pushed inward and the available range of movement contracts.**
> **Training may appear as a direction in which load expands usable range, options, resilience, or precision.**

Pain alone cannot distinguish the two. The relevant question is what remains after the load:

- Did the range of movement expand?
- Did options increase?
- Did judgment become more precise?
- Did recoverability remain?
- Does the next contact with the same boundary permit greater freedom of action?

Anger at imposed contraction and self-development are not mutually exclusive operations.

---

## 4. Use least-action language as a design heuristic, not a personality judgment

Human beings often move toward lower-cost routes.

The phrase "least action" is not used here as a claim that the physical principle of least action directly governs psychology or society. It is an operational heuristic: among available routes, agents often tend toward those with lower cognitive, bodily, or social cost.

The question is therefore not whether easy paths should be prohibited.

> **The design question is which paths are made easy.**

Recording can be made easier than concealment. Revision can be made easier than defensive closure. Saying "I do not know" can be made easier than inventing certainty. Early return can be made easier than hiding damage.

In this sense, sustained thinking may be understood as an activity that rewrites the future cost terrain of action.

---

## 5. Justice rewrites the terrain of available action

Justice is not treated merely as moral energy or proof of virtue.

It changes which actions are permitted, rewarded, condemned, difficult, or practically available. Justice therefore rewrites the terrain through which action moves.

The question is not only whether a justice exists, but:

> **Which paths does this justice make easier, and which does it make harder?**

Does it make objection easier? Does it make burden transfer easier? Does it preserve routes for relearning? Does it impose endurance only on the weaker side? Does it also make stronger actors receive return?

A justice can therefore be examined not only through its declared ideal but through the pathways it actually generates.

---

## 6. Theory is material for inquiry, not possession of correctness

Theories weighted toward subjective experience, theories weighted toward objective description, cross-domain research, science, religion, literature, ethics, and institutions can all be brought into inquiry as materials.

But bringing them together does not mean mixing them into one language.

Each language game has its own conditions of validity, observational range, claim strength, non-claims, and inherited vocabulary. Commensuration preserves those conditions while bringing domains into boundary contact.

> **Introduce the descriptive surfaces needed to see what occurs at the boundary.**

Commensuration is not identification. It makes traceable what is preserved, what is lost, how far a correspondence holds, where it stops, and where analogy or hypothesis begins.

This matters because a term borrowed from one field does not automatically carry the evidential authority of that field into another. Scientific vocabulary used in religious or spiritual discourse, for example, becomes problematic when resemblance is silently presented as scientific proof. The research task is not to prohibit the contact, but to expose the translation rule, claim boundary, and residual.

The value of a theory is therefore not measured only by whether it claims final possession of reality. It can also be examined by whether it reveals previously invisible differences, expands options, sharpens responsibility partition, permits non-destructive boundary operations, remains revisable when wrong, and stays open to unknown return.

> **Theory is not property that owns truth; it is material that can increase mobility.**

This does not mean that truth is irrelevant. It means that a theory should not close the very conditions required for observation, correction, and renewed collation.

---

## 7. The operational core of Scientific Ontology

The public operational core can be compressed into one question:

> **What should be introduced as material, how should it be received and acted upon, and how should the resulting dynamics be governed so that events at boundaries can become rich without becoming fatal?**

"Rich" does not merely mean pleasant or stimulating. It includes the capacity to encounter new differences, learn, rebuild relations, revise meaning, expand capability, and remain in contact with otherness without erasing it.

"Fatal" refers to conditions such as irreversible destruction, permanent loss of return paths, elimination of the subject of judgment, erasure of history, or destruction of the possibility of learning and correction.

The goal is not a frictionless world. Contact, difference, refusal, resistance, and change remain necessary. The design problem is to preserve routes for return, correction, and possible reconnection.

---

## 8. Ethics under an undetermined endpoint

An agent does not ordinarily possess the endpoint of the future.

A present state may look final and later reopen. A loss may be irreversible, or it may reveal another path. A theory may survive, fail, or become material for a later theory.

This outline therefore treats an undetermined endpoint as an ethical constraint: where no final endpoint can be owned with certainty, unnecessary irreversible closure deserves special caution.

This is not empirical proof of literal eternity, immortality, or a cosmological infinite future. It is an operational stance toward incomplete knowledge of what later conditions remain possible.

Before reassurance comes revisability. Before final correctness comes re-collatability.

Scientific Ontology can therefore be read as a system for asking how beings that cannot own the endpoint may handle boundaries while continuing to tolerate and investigate the unknown.

---

## 9. An "easier world" is a secondary aim

Reducing unnecessary suffering and making life easier are legitimate outcomes, but they are not the deepest criterion of this outline.

A perfectly comfortable world could become a closed world if it eliminated difference, experiment, learning, refusal, risk, and the possibility of transformation.

The stronger aim is to preserve conditions under which agents can remain mobile, can meet difference without being needlessly destroyed by it, and can return from error.

Ease matters, but as part of a larger terrain of sustainable contact and continued inquiry.

## 10. Carry the framework outward and receive what returns

The operational core should not remain sealed inside the theory.

> **Events happen at boundaries. Neither simply subjective nor simply objective, but where different modes of description come into contact.**

This orientation can be carried into language, literature, institutions, accounting, AI, interfaces, creative work, and other domains.

The purpose is not only to prove that Scientific Ontology was already correct. The outward movement asks:

- What became visible?
- What remained invisible?
- Where did judgment close too early?
- What broke?
- What expanded?
- Where did return arrive from?
- What should be sent back into the theory?

Implementation and generalization increase this circulation.

The framework goes out, is used, is allowed to fail, receives return, and窶背here necessary窶廃ermits the theory itself to change.

This metabolism belongs to the operation of Scientific Ontology itself.

## 11. Non-claim boundaries

This document does not claim that:

1. every harm or misfortune is the responsibility of the person who experiences it;
2. social structures, power asymmetries, bodily conditions, chance, or other external conditions are secondary;
3. pain should be endured because suffering is necessary for growth;
4. inertia is a moral defect of personality;
5. the physical principle of least action directly governs human psychology or society;
6. one universal definition of justice has been settled;
7. the difference between subjective and objective description should be erased;
8. "eternity" has been established here as an empirical scientific fact;
9. mobility should always be maximized窶敗afety, rest, protection, and temporary fixation may be necessary;
10. usefulness alone determines whether a theory is true.

The document proposes an operational line for bringing such different conditions into boundary collation and updating judgment through action and return.

## 12. Provisional conclusion

Scientific Ontology does not need to be held only as a large-scale explanation of the world.

It can also be compressed into an operational stance:

> Events occur at boundaries.
> Therefore do not explain them only from outside or only from the self.
> Act where action is possible.
> Observe what returns.
> Redraw responsibility boundaries.
> Design the terrain so that lower-cost movement can still lead toward expansion.
> Examine not only the word "justice," but which paths a justice makes easier or harder.
> Use theory as material for mobility rather than as property that possesses correctness.
> And because the endpoint cannot be owned in advance, avoid unnecessary irreversible closure and preserve places from which learning, reconnection, and return remain possible.

Scientific Ontology is not a theory for avoiding thought.

**It is a theory for making continued thought less likely to destroy the conditions that make further thought possible.**
