## 0. What This Document Does

Scientific Ontology is not a closed system intended to explain the whole world exhaustively.

Rather, it is a framework concerned with where the world does not close, where residuals arise, and at which boundaries collation and revision become necessary.

This document presents Scientific Ontology not as a list of concepts, but as a scholarly method. It does more than place summaries of individual documents side by side. It maps the reciprocal relations among ontology, epistemology, realism, ethics, organizational theory, AI, social design, and physical-near rendering.

Its central proposition can be stated in one sentence:

> **Scientific Ontology is a discipline that protects the limit of reality that humans can responsibly handle through Boundary Realism, explores the aporias beyond that limit through Applied Metaphysics, and repeatedly tests both through the history returned from boundary operation and implementation.**

In this sense, Scientific Ontology is not a closed system.

**Scientific Ontology does not abandon non-closure; it is a system for managing non-closure.**

For that reason, Scientific Ontology does not begin by choosing theories weighted toward subjectivity, theories weighted toward objectivity, cross-domain research, religion, literature, institutions, science, or any other language game as the final language. It attempts to maintain a field in which these can be brought to a boundary while preserving their own conditions of validity, observational scope, claim strength, and limits.

Commensuration here is not an identification of domains. It makes traceable what is preserved, what is transformed, where structural comparison becomes metaphor, what remains as a residual, and which return paths remain available. Science is not used as evidence for religion; religion is not used as a substitute for science. Yet the possibility that different language games touch the world from different angles is not discarded in advance.

To manage non-closure does not mean legitimizing ambiguity simply by leaving it ambiguous. It means stating what remains unresolved, where a description ceased to apply, what remained as a residual, and which collation is required next, then retaining these matters in a condition that remains open to inquiry.

## 2. Why Begin from “Boundary” Rather Than “Existence”?

When asking what truly exists, we often try to identify directly some ultimate substance behind the world.

What humans can actually confirm, however, is less often the ultimate substance itself than the trace of one thing acting upon another.

A person's words arrive and change another person's judgment.  
An institution narrows what a person can do.  
A corporate purpose changes how time is distributed at the operational site.  
An AI output enters decision-making and destabilizes the location of responsibility.  
Past experience changes what will later be felt as danger, beauty, rightness, or loss.  
A burden pushed into the natural environment returns from another place and time.

These are not merely subjective impressions or words alone.

Contact has occurred, history remains, and that history changes the next conditions.

Scientific Ontology treats this difficult-to-deny surface of operation as a **boundary**.

A boundary does not mean only a wall. It is a surface at which something may pass, reflect, be retained, be cut off, be transformed, or be recoded. When a boundary is formed, history remains. When history remains, the next mode of reception changes. When reception changes, return arises.

```text
contact
  ↓
boundary
  ↓
history
  ↓
change in downstream conditions
  ↓
return
  ↓
re-collation
```

“Return” here is not limited to retaliation. It includes reaction, exhaustion, residuals, residues, demands for repair, demands for explanation, loss of trust, institutional fatigue, learning, updates of meaning, and responses from nature.

This minimum structure crosses individuals, organizations, AI, society, history, narrative, and physical-near models.

## 3. Boundary Realism and Applied Metaphysics

Scientific Ontology contains two movements oriented in different directions.

### 3.1 Boundary Realism

Boundary Realism determines the range that humans may responsibly treat as real.

Humans do not possess the thing-in-itself as such. Nature itself, mind itself, matter itself, and the ultimate origin of the cosmos are not exhaustively fixed as the first propositions of the public framework.

Instead, boundaries, contact, history, return, and downstream conditions are treated as real.

This does not assert that nothing exists except boundaries. It does not deny what may lie behind boundaries. It withholds such things from treatment as directly settled objects.

Boundary Realism is not a wall erected to prohibit metaphysics.

It is a responsibility boundary that prevents metaphysics from confusing its own conjectures with what humans can confirm.

### 3.2 Applied Metaphysics

Applied Metaphysics extends inquiry beyond what Boundary Realism has reserved.

It describes, as hypotheses, the origin of existence, what precedes time, possibility, observation, meaning, matter, fields, non-existence, cutoff, and cosmic totality. AMP and ITS belong to the high-strength research layer that advances in this direction.

High strength does not mean empirical establishment.

It means extending hypotheses farther than the ordinary claims of the public framework while examining internal coherence, explanatory capacity, candidates for formalization, counterexamples, and residuals.

### 3.3 Scientific Ontology Operates the Reciprocity Between Them

Boundary Realism alone keeps inquiry within a responsible and confirmable range, but questions may stop short of the limit.

Applied Metaphysics alone may extend questions far outward, but risks losing testability and responsibility boundaries.

Scientific Ontology does not choose only one of them.

```text
Boundary Realism
  ├─ confirmability
  ├─ public communicability
  ├─ non-claim boundaries
  └─ responsible reservation
          ↑↓
Scientific Ontology
  ├─ collation
  ├─ historical retention
  ├─ counterexample processing
  ├─ implementation testing
  └─ aporia management
          ↑↓
Applied Metaphysics
  ├─ origin hypotheses
  ├─ thing-in-itself-side descriptions
  ├─ formalization candidates
  └─ high-strength inquiry
```

Metaphysics passes questions to boundary operation.  
Boundary operation returns failures, counterexamples, and residuals to metaphysics.

Through this reciprocity, the two correct and develop one another.

## 4. Why Is a Non-Closed System Necessary?

When a system attempts completeness, matters appear that cannot be decided from within that system alone.

Exceptions that do not fit a classification.  
Differences that remain after explanation.  
Premises that cannot be proven but whose removal would destroy the operating conditions of the system.  
Aporias whose meaning cannot be closed.  
Residues that remain in operational sites, bodies, and nature even after institutions declare processing complete.  
An exterior that theory cannot see, but whose effects return.

Scientific Ontology does not erase these as mere failures.

At the same time, it does not conclude that anything may be said merely because a matter is unresolved.

Managing non-closure requires at least the following:

1. State how far closure has been achieved.  
2. State from where closure has not been achieved.  
3. Do not retreat unresolved questions into unfalsifiable mystery.  
4. Distinguish residuals from residues and preserve a route for subsequent collation.  
5. Do not confuse public claims, research hypotheses, structural analogies, and implementation candidates.  
6. Retain the reactions produced by implementation as history and return them to the theoretical side.  

### 4.1 How Gödelian Incompleteness Is Read Here

Scientific Ontology does not claim that Gödel's incompleteness theorems prove Scientific Ontology.

Nor does it directly generalize theorems from the foundations of mathematics to society, consciousness, or the cosmos.

What it references is the configuration in which a sufficiently strong formal system, when it seeks complete self-containment, may expose propositions that cannot be proved or refuted within the system and dependencies upon what lies outside the system.

Scientific Ontology reads this configuration as a methodological guide.

```text
seek a complete system
  ↓
propositions, residuals, and aporias appear that cannot close internally
  ↓
the temptation arises to delete the exterior as “meaningless”
  ↓
the deleted exterior returns as damage, reaction, or residue
  ↓
a field of aporia opens in which missing links can be explored
  ↓
Boundary Realism and Applied Metaphysics enter into reciprocity again
```

For Scientific Ontology, the non-closure of a system is not a defect.

It is an exposed surface indicating where inquiry should proceed next and which boundary should be reconstructed.

Theory in Scientific Ontology does not exist to judge reality unilaterally.

It exists so that contact with reality may also judge the theory itself.

### 5.1 Planar Logic and the Logical-Depth Axis

This network cannot be read as a single straight line.

Within planar logic, one can trace an order in which a difference is recognized, made explicit as an axiom, purposified, implemented through organizations and institutions, and followed by the return of its consequences.

```text
difference
  → cognitive formation
  → articulation and commensuration of cognitive forms
  → purpose setting
  → sharing
  → world formation
  → organization and implementation
  → return from boundaries
```

Some matters, however, cannot be processed within a planar cycle alone.

Different time horizons.  
Purposes that cannot hold simultaneously.  
Non-commensurable residuals.  
Experiences that do not fit the current vocabulary.  
Residues that continue to act even after institutional processing is declared complete.  
Aporias that cannot yet be formalized.

The logical-depth axis is a direction in which these matters are retained as unresolved questions, contradictions, branches, residuals, and semantic pressure without crushing them into the same plane, so that they may be reconstructed at another time and in another form.

```text
Planar logic
  the order that can be connected, judged, and implemented at a given point

Logical-depth axis
  the direction that retains and re-collates differences that do not close on the plane
```

Scientific Ontology does not attempt to fill logical space exhaustively with concepts.

It distributes primary document responsibilities and gives each document depth ports and return paths.

- **returns to**: returns residuals arising from implementation or collation.
- **depth interface**: retains questions that cannot close within planar logic.

Through this division of responsibility, documents do not fill one another in. They connect only their blind spots through return paths.

## 8. Epistemology Connects the Fields

Scientific Ontology does not treat differences among fields as mere differences in specialist vocabulary.

Each field brings a different world into view according to what it attends to, what it abstracts away, what it retains as value, and what it processes as cost.

### 8.1 [Cognitive-Axis Formation](../02_Raj_Beauty/Entropy_Attributed_Difference_and_Cognitive_Axis_Formation.en.md)

The cognitive-axis formation model proposes the following cycle:

```text
entropy-attributed difference
  ↓
attention / abstraction
  ↓
sensibility / understanding
  ↓
value / cost
        ↓
      residual
        ↓
    repetition
  ↓
cognitive axis
```

The same institution may appear as order to one person and as historical cutoff to another.

The same AI may appear as an efficiency device to one person and as a device that diminishes the field of judgment to another.

The same history may appear as progress to one person and as the externalization of burden to another.

Differences in cognition are not merely differences in conclusion.

They are differences in what arises as an object, what becomes perceptible, and what acquires a form that can be understood.

### 8.2 [Optional Axiom Modules](../03_Tam_Goodness/Optional_Axiom_Modules_as_Cognitive_Bridge.en.md)

The Optional Axiom Module is a cognitive bridge that connects this model of cognitive-axis formation to philosophy, ethics, aesthetics, politics, economics, organization, AI, and metaphysics. It is also a boundary protocol for contact between different cognitive forms.

Each axiom is not a label for ranking systems of thought.

It describes, for a given worldview:

- what it attends to;
- what it abstracts away;
- what it retains as value;
- what it processes as cost;
- which historical background and narrative it carries;
- at which boundary it collides with another axiom.

This allows comparison among systems of thought to become not only a question of “which is correct,” but an epistemological comparison of which differences each makes visible and which it makes invisible.

This module is not a diagnostic table for fixing people into ideological or personality types. Nor is it a classification tool for education, recruitment, governance, or persuasion.

Its purpose is to collate cross-disciplinary commensurability, collision points, and abstracted regions.

### 8.3 Meaning Generation Model

Meaning does not arise merely because a difference has become visible.

Information moves toward meaning when external input connects with internal history, emotion, bodily sensation, memory, thought, and action, and forms a cycle with a return path.

The Meaning Generation Model connects realism and epistemology.

It connects the realist aspect—in which something acts upon a boundary—with the epistemic aspect—in which that action connects to a particular history and is experienced as a particular world, through a meaning loop.

## 9. [Truth Management](./Truth_Management_and_Boundary_PDCA.en.md) — From Cognition to Purpose

Once a cognitive axis has formed, the next question is what should be placed as purpose.

Truth Management in Scientific Ontology does not limit truth to the correctness or incorrectness of propositions. It also treats truth as a process in which a purpose is shared, forms a world, and receives corrective pressure from the boundaries of that world.

### First Purpose — Purpose

What is to be protected?  
What is to be increased?  
What is to be treated as something that must not be lost?  
For what purpose do we organize, work, build institutions, and use technology?

### Shared Purpose — Sharing

A purpose that remains only within one person remains a belief or wish.

It descends into reality when it is passed into language, institutions, roles, education, evaluation, and design, and thereby becomes shareable with others.

### World Formation — The World Formed by Sharing

When a purpose is shared, what counts as achievement, what counts as loss, who counts as a contributor, who is treated as a cost, and what is treated as an exception are rearranged.

Purpose draws a world.

### Boundary CA — Inspection and Revision

When a shared purpose descends into operational practice, who becomes exhausted?  
Where has burden moved?  
What has become invisible?  
Which fields of judgment have become impoverished?  
Which return paths have been lost?

Check and Act are not completed inside a meeting room.

They occur at the boundaries of the world drawn by purpose.

```text
cognitive axis
  ↓
First Purpose: purpose
  ↓
Shared Purpose: sharing
  ↓
World Formation
  ↓
Boundary CA
  ↓
re-collation of First Purpose
```

Within this operational extension, what remains qualified as purpose after developing from First Purpose through World Formation and passing through corrective pressure from boundaries is called truth.

This does not reject ordinary theories of truth.

It is an extension for operationally testing truth in domains—such as organizations, institutions, AI, states, and history—where the setting of purposes itself changes reality.

History can also be read through this framework.

What did a civilization, religion, state, or corporation place as its First Purpose? How was it shared? What world did it form? From which boundaries did corrective pressure return? History can be reread as the history of purpose operation.

## 10. [Organizational Boundary and Port Model](../05_Research_Notes/Cognitive_Dynamics_Communication_Studies/Organizational_Boundary_and_Port_Model.ja.md)

When a purpose is shared, an organization arises.

In Scientific Ontology, an organization is not merely a collection of people. It is a boundary that retains responsibility, history, culture, decision-making, communication, and processing capacity.

In this sense, human consciousness—grounded in the body and operating history, sensibility, understanding, attention, abstraction, and meaning loops—may also be read as a local organizational boundary.

This does not reduce a human being to a corporation or machine.

It means that systems at different scales may contain comparable structures of boundary, communication, history, and responsibility.

### 10.1 Responsibility Boundary

Functions can be delegated.

Responsibility cannot be delegated away.

A public administration may contract out front-desk operations, but it cannot erase administrative responsibility.  
Medical practice may externalize tests or systems, but it cannot erase the duty to explain.  
Open-source software may distribute work, but it still requires a responsibility boundary for release decisions.  
An organization may delegate classification, summarization, and proposals to AI, but it cannot cast responsibility for the results onto AI and make that responsibility disappear.

Scientific Ontology also holds that the historical record does not permit responsibility to be obscured.

Who authorized the action?  
Who benefited?  
Who received the burden?  
Who could not object?  
Which return paths were closed?

These remain as history that changes downstream conditions.

### 10.2 Port

Organizations require different communication openings.

Research, sales, legal affairs, diplomacy, education, recruitment, finance, and crisis management do not operate under identical communication conditions.

A port is a boundary opening that admits, transforms, processes, and returns a specific kind of communication.

Without appropriate ports, complaints, exceptions, contradictions, and unresolved cases from outside may flow into the organization without limit, making processing impossible.

Conversely, when an organization pushes burdens it cannot process outward onto customers, operational staff, society, or nature without explanation, residuals and residues accumulate outside it.

### 10.3 Negentropy Definition

An organization defines itself by what it receives as entropy and what it returns as negentropy.

A hospital may receive pain, diagnostic uncertainty, and insufficient care, and return recovery, explanation, and care.  
A school may receive ignorance, isolation, and an inability to learn, and return understanding, learning circuits, and shared inquiry.  
A court may receive conflict, rights violations, and unclear responsibility, and return legal stability, procedural fairness, and responsibility processing.

An organization's purpose appears not in its slogan, but in what it receives, what it transforms that input into, and what it returns to society.

## 11. Implementation in AI, Institutions, and Peace

Scientific Ontology does not fix technology as either good or evil.

Technology usually depends on how it is used.

The phrase “it depends on how it is used,” however, cannot erase the reactions and responsibility produced by technological use.

### 11.1 AI Adaptation

AI Adaptation does not mean only making AI resemble a human being.

It means adjusting the boundaries to which AI is connected, the inputs it receives, the history it retains, and the responsibility boundaries to which it returns its outputs and effects.

The usefulness of AI cannot be measured only by the speed or volume of its answers.

Usefulness also includes preserving a field in which users retain options, grounds, uncertainty, and the possibility of objection, and do not lose the capacity to judge for themselves.

### 11.3 Peace Specification

Peace is not a condition in which competition has disappeared completely.

It is a condition in which different modes of existence can continue non-destructive interaction while retaining their respective histories and responsibility boundaries.

When everything is compressed into one evaluative axis, education, art, research, family life, care, labor, play, and faith are forced into a single competitive space.

Designing peace does not mean erasing differences.

It means creating ports, protocols, diplomacy, and return paths so that differences are not immediately converted into exclusion or total competition.

## 12. Physical-Near Rendering and the High-Strength Research Layer

Scientific Ontology approaches physical-near vocabulary such as field, time, particle, entropy, entanglement, Feynman diagram, and `BlackHole`.

The term `entanglement` here is not identified with entanglement in standard quantum theory.

Nor is using these terms equivalent to replacing standard physics.

The purpose of physical-near rendering is to explore what becomes visible when history, boundary, communication, return path, cutoff, and non-separable correspondence are constrained toward the descriptive surface of physical diagrams.

For the handling of physical-near vocabulary, claim scope, and non-claim boundaries, see the [`Physics Correspondence Policy`](./Physics_Correspondence_Policy.en.md).

### 12.1 History-Field Topology

History is not a warehouse of past records.

It is a structure that changes later reception, reaction, observation, path selection, and communicability.

History-field topology reads an object not as an isolated point, but as a structure with contacts, junctions, return paths, cutoffs, and residuals.

### 12.2 PINGER Hypothesis

The PINGER Hypothesis is a high-strength Research Note that projects history-field topology into physical-near vocabulary.

It is not a completed physical theory that redefines particles as point-like entities. It examines a conceptual model in which communication pathways or history-field solitons appear particle-like at an observational section.

Before reading it, one must distinguish standard definitions, structural analogies, ontological reinterpretations, and formalization candidates.

### 12.3 AMP / ITS

AMP and ITS are non-public or high-strength core layers that deliberately describe what Boundary Realism reserves.

They are not withheld because they are unrelated to the framework.

They are withheld because their claim strength and interpretive density are high, and because the risk of their being read as scientific or pseudoscientific claims is substantial. At the present stage, they are therefore not allowed to govern the ordinary public layer directly.

Their role is not to confer authority upon public documents.

Their role is to supply where the public framework remains open, which aporia should be explored next, and which candidates for formalization remain available.

## 14. What Becomes Visible in the Current Public System?

Through the v4 series, the public framework presented Boundary Realism, history-fields, meaning generation, boundary ethics, AI boundary functions, and the Physics Correspondence Policy with increasing clarity and connected them through research dynamics.

At the opening of the v5 series, that circulation is carried beyond the framework's internal vocabulary into language, literature, consent, institutions, AI, and interfaces. Criticism, failure, residuals, and unresolved conditions returned from those contacts are explicitly allowed to revise upstream documents.

Two additional features therefore come into the foreground:

1. a field for collating different language games while preserving their conditions and claim boundaries; and
2. a living-canonical operation in which research documents may be revised by what returns from public use, implementation, and criticism.

The following connections become especially visible:

```text
cognitive formation
  ↓
collation through language, meaning, and communication phase
  ↓
purpose setting
  ↓
purpose sharing
  ↓
world formation
  ↓
organizational boundaries and responsibility boundaries
  ↓
implementation in AI, institutions, and society
  ↓
Boundary CA
  ↓
history and aporia
  ↓
re-examination of epistemology, ontology, and metaphysics
```

This change does not merely mean that the number of documents has increased.

It means that Scientific Ontology has reached a stage at which it can present, as one methodological cycle:

- how the world is described;
- how humans cognize it;
- how purposes become institutions;
- where technology is connected;
- who receives responsibility for resulting reactions;
- how the non-closure of theory is returned to the next inquiry.

The concept network in the current public system is not an additional index.

It is an entrance through which the public framework becomes able to explain its own research method.

## 15. What This Framework Does Not Promise

Scientific Ontology does not promise:

- to declare possession of the ultimate substance of the world;
- to replace standard science, philosophy, psychology, medicine, economics, or law;
- to present physical-near vocabulary as empirically established physical theory;
- to use cognitive axes or optional axioms for human diagnosis, personality classification, recruitment, or governance;
- to assert that AI possesses the same consciousness or personality as humans;
- to absorb all conflict into harmony and make cutoff or isolation unnecessary;
- to treat unresolved questions as closed by means of mystical language.

“To exclude no one” does not mean admitting everything unconditionally into the interior.

It means that, even when cutoff, suspension, isolation, or transformation is necessary, what has been placed outside a boundary is not erased as completely meaningless.

Safety does not mean the absence of danger.

It means that when a dangerous difference makes contact, suspension, warning, collation, cutoff, retention of return paths, and responsibility processing remain possible.

Non-closure does not mean avoiding conclusions.

It means closing what can responsibly be closed at a given stage and leaving precisely identified what does not close.

## 16. Conclusion

Scientific Ontology is not a system for possessing the world completely.

It is a system for responsibly handling what remains, what returns, what does not close, and where the next question arises after contact with the world.

Boundary Realism protects the limits of human handling.  
Applied Metaphysics extends questions beyond those limits.  
Epistemology examines what arises as a world.  
Truth Management follows the process through which purposes form worlds.  
Organizational-boundary theory implements responsibility and communication.  
Ethics takes up return and destruction.  
Implementation tests whether theoretical language functions at real boundaries.  
High-strength research extends the next hypotheses toward aporias that remain open.

No layer becomes the final authority.

They pass questions to one another, return history, and require revision.

What Scientific Ontology manages is not a completed world.

**It manages boundaries that allow inquiry and responsibility to continue without pretending to completion.**
