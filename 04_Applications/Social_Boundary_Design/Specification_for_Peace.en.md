This document owns the following question:

> What specification for peace is required for existences with finite cognitive and processing capacity to continue interacting through necessary restriction and diplomacy, without erasing different existence phases or destructively discharging meaning pressure?

This document takes the Logical-Depth Axis as its principal axis.

Peace is not treated as a condition in which every difference is immediately converted into agreement.

An important condition of peace is the capacity to retain differences that do not close on the current logical plane, send them toward other concepts, experiences, language games, institutions, and time horizons, and make them available for re-collation.

## 1. Problem Setting

The struggle for survival is often understood as competition for material resources.

Food, land, money, power, and status affect survival.

They are not, however, existence itself.

They are exchange surfaces that support existence.

In a society that treats external metrics as the sole standard of existential value, the struggle for survival becomes zero-sum.

Moreover, when cognition and meaning experience weaken, people become unable to handle multiple existence phases and attempt to decide everything through a single axis.

Different existence phases then appear not as existences serving different roles, but as competitors struggling for the same metric.

This document organizes peace not as the disappearance of competition, but as a boundary specification that prevents the competitive space from being made singular.

---

## 2. What Is Non-Essential to the Struggle for Survival

Material resources matter.

They are not, however, existence itself.

Possessing money is not the same as living. Acquiring status is not personality itself. A qualification indicates part of a history, but does not represent an existence as a whole.

External metrics are comparable.

Where comparison is possible, ranking emerges.

When ranking becomes the sole evaluation of existence, different existence phases are compressed into one competitive space.

This compression turns the struggle for survival into a contest of deprivation.

This document recognizes competition over material resources, status, and authority as one surface of the struggle for survival, but not as its essence.

---

## 3. What Is Essential to the Struggle for Survival

This document understands the struggle for survival as the formation, maintenance, and renewal of **Existence Strength**.

Existence Strength is the capacity of an existence to continue interacting with the world and to keep forming new histories, meanings, relations, and structures.

Existence Strength is formed through:

- cognition;
- creation;
- learning;
- trust;
- technology;
- culture;
- cooperation;
- responsible processing;
- recovery; and
- commensuration.

Material resources may be one condition or result of Existence Strength, but they are not its substitute.

Existence Strength is not maximized by erasing others.

It grows by deepening connections with others, nature, institutions, and history; restoring broken return paths; and becoming able to form more diverse existence phases.

The fundamental competition can therefore be reread not as a competition to destroy one another, but as a creative competition over who can form a richer world.

---

## 4. Self-Definition, Objective Definition, and Existence Phases

Existence Strength has both self-defining and objectively defined aspects.

Self-definition is formed internally through experience, understanding, responsibility, creation, belief, and meaning loops.

Objective definition is formed socially through qualifications, titles, evaluations, income, authority, and affiliation.

Objective definition is necessary for social operation.

However, when objective definition alone becomes the measure of existence, competition becomes biased toward scarce external metrics.

A person has multiple existence phases, including education, research, family, friendship, culture, embodiment, labor, play, and community.

Peace requires these phases to be retained at their appropriate boundaries rather than collapsed into a single evaluative axis.

## 5. The Meaning-Experience Field Model

This document treats the cycle through which an existence receives differences, transforms them into meaning, and returns a response as a **Meaning-Experience Field**.

```text
external world / others / nature / institutions
        ↓
boundary contact
        ↓
sensibility / cognitive ports
        ↓
meaning field
        ↓
history network
        ↓
judgment / action / response
        ↓
return to the external world
```

The Meaning-Experience Field is not a static container.

Whenever a new experience enters, it interacts with existing history and reconstructs prior meanings, responsibilities, values, and the conditions of future response.

Vision is a powerful cognitive port that organizes differences as arrangements of objects along three-dimensional spatial axes.

The Meaning-Experience Field, however, does not close within three-dimensional space alone.

History, Intrinsic Time, responsibility, relations, meaning pressure, and distance between language games require axes other than spatial arrangement.

This document recognizes the visual-spatial axis as an important port while refusing to erase other cognitive ports.

---

## 6. The Logical-Depth Axis

The Logical-Depth Axis does not indicate before and after in external time.

Nor is it a planar logic concerned only with the truth or falsity and consistency of propositions.

It is a direction for retaining unresolved matters, contradictions, branches, residuals, residues, and meaning pressure, and for reconstructing them at another depth.

What peace requires is not the capacity to solve every problem immediately.

It is the capacity to send differences that do not close on the current plane toward another port and another time, without discarding them as enemies, noise, incompetence, or violations.

The Logical-Depth Axis can also be read as the distance required to reach the core concepts of different language games.

Merely replacing another person's words with one's own vocabulary does not constitute commensuration.

It is necessary to read what the other retains as Core and what histories must be traversed before that meaning becomes reachable.

Peace is also a condition in which unintelligible differences are not destroyed merely because they remain unintelligible.

## 7. iMass and Meaning Capacity

This document does not treat `iMass` as a publicly established physical quantity.

Within this application model, `iMass` is read through one of its operational manifestations: the cohesion of history networks, language games, and paradigms, together with the inertia of internal processing that prevents them from immediately losing form under external input.

A large `iMass` does not simply mean stubbornness.

It means that past histories, connections among concepts, responsibilities, and meaning loops are sufficiently cohesive to allow the whole to be maintained and reconstructed when a new difference is received.

High cohesion without a pathway for re-collation, however, becomes rigidity.

This application-level reading does not exhaust the generative structure of `iMass` as a high-depth identifier.

In this document, internal cohesion inertia is treated as one factor that regulates the effective Meaning Capacity of the structure modeled as a capacitor for meaning and experience.

### 7.1 Meaning Capacity

**Meaning Capacity** is the capacity to retain differences, contradictions, and meaning pressure without immediately cutting them off or discharging them destructively.

Meaning Capacity is not determined by the quantity of knowledge alone.

It varies with:

- the cohesion of history networks;
- pathways along the Logical-Depth direction;
- available cognitive ports;
- holding capacity;
- commensuration capacity;
- return paths; and
- bodily and practical reserves.

`iMass` is not identified with capacity itself. It is treated as the inertia and cohesion that maintain the structure of the capacitor.

Meaning Capacity may be read as a function of `iMass`, the number of ports, depth pathways, and available processing reserves.

## 8. Meaning-Experience Capacitor

The **Meaning-Experience Capacitor** is an application model of a boundary function that receives differences, retains them, sends them toward depth, reconstructs them, and releases them into return paths.

The term does not claim physical identity with an electrical circuit.

The correspondences can be organized as follows.

| Capacitor-like element | Reading within the Meaning-Experience Field |
|---|---|
| Terminals | Cognitive and communication ports |
| Capacity | Effective Meaning Capacity for retaining meaning pressure |
| Structural retention | History-cohesion inertia read through `iMass` |
| Difference analogous to potential difference | Unprocessed differences and meaning pressure |
| Charging | Holding differences and connecting them to history |
| Mediation analogous to a dielectric | Language games, culture, embodiment, and institutions |
| Discharge | Response, creation, explanation, apology, compensation, and action |
| Short circuit | Immediate hostility, violence, false closure, and destructive expulsion |
| Leakage | Forgetting, exhaustion, dissipation of attention, and unconscious externalization |

The role of the Meaning-Experience Capacitor is not to accumulate differences indefinitely.

It is to route them toward appropriate ports, languages, responsibilities, and times, and to return them in non-destructive forms.

Capacity overload may appear as bodily symptoms, anger, silence, institutional distrust, organizational exit, environmental burden, or preparation for war.

## 9. An Operational Definition of Peace

This document does not define peace as the disappearance of competition.

Peace is a condition in which different existence phases can continue interacting while retaining their respective boundaries and histories, without falling into destructive single-axis reduction.

Its minimum conditions are as follows.

1. Existence phases are not completely compressed into a single evaluative axis.
2. Multiple ports are retained as potential pathways.
3. Necessary Scoped Port Operation is permitted.
4. The scope, duration, and responsibility of the restriction are made explicit.
5. Closed ports can be reopened.
6. Untranslatable residuals are not erased.
7. Unresolved matters can be routed along the Logical-Depth direction.
8. Return paths exist for appeal, explanation, compensation, and renegotiation.
9. Externalized burdens are returned to Boundary CA.

Peace is not universal connection.

It is scoped operation that does not lose the possibility of connection.

## 10. Boundary Diplomacy

Efforts toward peace lie in diplomacy among organizations, persons, and cognitive axes.

Diplomacy does not mean internalizing the other or achieving complete synchronization.

It means maintaining communicability while retaining the other's boundary, history, purpose, and language game.

Diplomacy includes detection, port selection, commensuration, holding, response, residual retention, and renegotiation.

In communication between conscious beings, each party can return language and judgment.

Cognitive capacity, authority, embodiment, time, and language are nevertheless asymmetric.

Diplomacy does not conceal this asymmetry. It places greater cognitive responsibility on the stronger side.

## 11. An I-Thou Boundary Attitude

This document refers to Buber's "I-Thou" as a conceptual guide for an attitude that does not completely reduce the other to one's own use.

This does not claim that every object possesses human-like consciousness.

Nature, bodies, institutions, devices, and environments do not respond in the same language as humans.

They nevertheless exhibit resistance, damage, limits, returns, and subsequent conditions.

The side possessing cognition must not process these merely as silence, but has a responsibility to read them as boundary conditions.

- Do not reduce nature to an unlimited resource.
- Do not reduce the body to labor power.
- Do not reduce institutions to commands.
- Do not disguise AI as a responsible subject.
- Do not treat non-response from the other as consent.

An I-Thou boundary attitude means refusing to close the other as an entirely ownable "It."

## 12. Dynamics through Which Peace Collapses

The collapse of peace does not begin only with sudden hostility.

It begins with the contraction of meaning experience and the reduction of available ports.

```text
decline in bodily, practical, and organizational reserves
  ↓
decline in Meaning Capacity
  ↓
reduction of available ports
  ↓
contraction of the range of concern
  ↓
expansion of abstraction
  ↓
contraction of meaning experience
  ↓
compression into a single evaluative axis
  ↓
processing different existence phases as enemies, noise, or exceptions
  ↓
externalization of residuals
  ↓
forced synchronization / exclusion / destructive discharge
```

Here, the weakening of cognition does not mean a decline in intelligence.

It means a reduction in the differences, depths, ports, and time horizons that can be handled.

Simple answers provide a strong sense of closure at low processing cost.

Because they continue sending residuals outside, however, they cannot sustain peace over the long term.

## 13. Scoped Port Management and Peace

Scoped Port Management is necessary.

Not everything can be received at the same time.

During a crisis, a safety port may need priority; in medicine, a treatment port; and in a court, a procedural port.

The problem is the absolutization of restriction.

Scoped operation requires:

- a purpose;
- a scope of application;
- a duration;
- a responsible actor;
- a holding destination for differences outside the scope;
- stop conditions;
- reopening conditions; and
- observation of externalized residuals.

Existence phases outside the restricted port must not be treated as without value or as enemies.

---

## 14. The Power of Diplomatic Interfaces

A diplomatic interface possesses considerable power because it knows multiple ports, languages, and histories.

A sound intermediary supports communication between parties, explains translation criteria, retains residuals, and permits exit and appeal.

An unsound intermediary monopolizes connection, gives different explanations to different parties, and profits from disagreement.

This document calls this failure **Gateway Capture**.

In literary terms, it may be called the **Gray Witch Strategy**.

The Gray Witch is not dangerous because she stands between good and evil.

She becomes dangerous when she possesses the power to commensurate multiple boundaries while remaining beyond collation herself.

What is required is audit of the intermediary, records of translation criteria, direct pathways between the parties, appeal, exit, and reconnection.

## 15. Organizational Implementation

Principles alone are insufficient to implement a specification for peace within organizations.

The following are required:

- organizational boundaries;
- responsibility boundaries;
- diplomatic ports;
- depth ports;
- appeal ports;
- audit ports;
- history retention;
- stopping and reopening; and
- boundary updates.

Even when an organization declares that it "values dialogue," peace is not implemented unless it specifies who receives appeals, who holds unresolved matters, who reconsiders decisions, and who audits intermediaries.

Peace between organizations does not mean integrating them into one entity. It means preserving diplomatic ports, translation, stopping, and return paths for renegotiation.

## 16. Application to AI

AI can function as an external support node for the Meaning-Experience Field.

AI can connect multiple sources, languages, and perspectives, and temporarily organize differences that a human cannot currently retain.

At the same time, AI can amplify cognitive axes and accelerate single-axis reduction through objective functions, training data, and evaluation metrics.

When AI is used as a Depth Port or diplomatic interface, the following must be examined:

- Does it display unresolved matters as unresolved?
- Does it preserve untranslatable residuals?
- Does it avoid falsely closing different sources into one conclusion?
- Can a human switch to another port?
- Does AI replace human Meaning Capacity and weaken internal circuits?
- Do judgment, appeal, stopping authority, and responsibility return to humans?

Contact between AI and social institutions should be collated through [`AI_Adoption_Collation_Checklist.md`](./AI_Adoption_Collation_Checklist.md).

DSSI can be read as an implementation candidate that supports boundary sovereignty in digital environments through pre-input warnings, holding, transmission control, and history retention.

## 17. Checklist for a Specification for Peace

1. Are multiple existence phases recognized?
2. Has a single metric substituted for existence as a whole?
3. Are multiple ports retained as potential pathways?
4. Can closed ports be reopened?
5. Can differences that do not close on the current plane be sent to a Depth Port?
6. Is there sufficient Meaning Capacity to hold meaning pressure?
7. Are untranslatable residuals being preserved?
8. Are there return paths for appeal and renegotiation?
9. Can intermediaries and gateways be audited?
10. Are externalized burdens being traced?
11. Are nature, bodies, and future generations included within Boundary CA?
12. Does AI maintain the field of judgment?

## 18. Application Examples

### 18.1 Interpersonal Dialogue

Do not immediately judge another person's statement only within one's own language game. Retain meaning pressure, history, and holding as relevant dimensions.

### 18.2 Diplomacy between Organizations

Retain different purposes and responsibility boundaries while making shared ports and non-shared domains explicit.

### 18.3 Public Administration and Residents

Route differences in lived experience that do not enter institutional ports toward appeal, exception, and learning ports.

### 18.4 Businesses and Local Communities

Collate not only the profit port, but also employment, technology, environment, culture, and responsibility after withdrawal.

### 18.5 Humans and Nature

Do not treat nature as a silent resource. Read recovery speed, damage, returns, and irreversibility as boundary conditions.

### 18.6 AI and Users

Do not treat AI output as judgment itself. Preserve grounds, uncertainty, appeal, stopping, and re-collation.

### 18.7 Relations between States

Do not compress the other party's existence phases into a single metric of hostility. Separate ports for security, economy, culture, history, residents, and environment.

## 21. Provisional Conclusion

Peace is not a static condition in which conflict has disappeared.

It is a dynamic condition in which different existence phases can continue interacting through necessary restriction and diplomacy while retaining their respective boundaries and histories.

Neither humans nor organizations can process everything at once.

Compression and Scoped Port Operation are therefore necessary.

However, when currently unused existence phases are erased and a restricted axis is transformed into the essence of the world as a whole, meaning experience contracts, residuals are externalized, and hostility increases.

Peace requires the capacity not to destroy unintelligible differences immediately, but to hold them within the Meaning-Experience Capacitor, send them along the Logical-Depth direction, and return them through appropriate ports and responsibility boundaries.

Peace is not universal connection.

It is boundary control that does not lose the possibility of reconnection.
