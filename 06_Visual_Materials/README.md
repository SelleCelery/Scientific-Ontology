# Visual Materials

> Status: Public Companion Materials  
> Scope: conceptual posters, visual system maps, research companion figures  
> Language: Japanese / English  
> Note: These materials are conceptual research companions. They are not empirical data figures, formal proofs, physical cosmology models, financial models, policy proposals, or AI system specifications.

This folder contains conceptual posters and visual companion figures for **Scientific Ontology (SO)**.

These materials are intended to provide visual orientation for the public edition of SO. They summarize structural relations among operational correspondence, collation, residuals, residues, logical-depth axis, meaning generation, heuristic operation, boundary-based interpretation, and residue assessment.

They should be read together with the main public documents of the repository.

本フォルダには、**Scientific Ontology (SO) / 存在境界論** の概念ポスターおよび視覚的補助図を収録します。

これらの図版は、SO公開版の概念構造を俯瞰するための研究補助資料です。同期、照合、残差、残渣、論理-深度軸、意味生成、ヒューリスティック運用、境界に基づく解釈、残渣査定の関係を視覚的に整理します。

本文資料と併読することを前提とします。

## How to read these materials

These posters are not intended to replace the written theory.

They function as visual entry points into the conceptual structure of SO. Some elements are symbolic, metaphorical, or compressed for visual presentation.

For formal definitions and claim boundaries, please refer to the relevant text documents in the repository.

These visual materials should be read as conceptual maps, not as direct empirical claims.

これらのポスターは、本文理論の代替ではありません。

SOの概念構造へ入るための視覚的入口です。一部の要素は、視覚表現のために象徴化・比喩化・圧縮されています。

正式な定義、主張強度、適用範囲については、リポジトリ内の本文資料を参照してください。

## Conceptual status

These visual materials are:

- conceptual posters    
- visual system maps    
- research companion figures    
- orientation aids for readers    

They are not:

- empirical diagrams    
- mathematical proofs    
- physical cosmology models    
- financial models    
- policy proposals    
- AI system specifications    
- legal or accounting standards    

The diagrams may use cosmological, economic, linguistic, computational, and institutional motifs as conceptual analogies. Such motifs should be read as part of the theoretical mapping, not as direct empirical or institutional claims.
