## 1. Authoritative text / 正本

Unless otherwise stated, the Japanese text is the authoritative draft for the present stage of Scientific Ontology and its related documents.

English versions are provided as translation drafts, working equivalents, and international-facing reference texts.

Where the Japanese and English versions diverge in nuance, the Japanese text should be consulted as the primary source.

ただし、英訳は単なる機械的置換ではない。英語圏で読解可能な体系語として成立させるため、必要に応じて語順、説明句、補助語を調整する。

## 2. Translation method / 翻訳方法

English translations may be prepared with the assistance of AI translation tools and large language models.

The author reviews and adjusts key terms, conceptual structure, and major claims. However, due to the scale and conceptual density of the materials, the author does not guarantee that every English sentence fully preserves all nuance of the Japanese authoritative draft.

The English version should therefore be read as a checked translation draft, not as an independently authoritative original.

## 3. Terminological consistency / 用語一貫性

Core terms should follow the established translation policy defined in the relevant glossary or appendix.

Examples include:

- 境界認識批判: Boundary Epistemological Critique
- 同期: operational correspondence
- 照合: collation
- 残差: residual
- 残渣: residue / sedimented residue
- 履歴場: history-field / history-bearing field
- 偽閉鎖: false closure
- 意味圧: semantic pressure
- 論理-深度軸: logical-depth axis
- 通約: commensuration
- 再照合可能性: capacity for re-collation / re-examinability

Where a new document introduces domain-specific usage, the new usage should be defined explicitly rather than silently replacing an existing core term.

## 4. On “closure” / 「閉じる」について

The Japanese term 「閉じる」 is context-dependent and should not always be translated mechanically as “to close.”

In epistemological and semantic contexts, it may refer to closing an internal or semantic loop.

In economic, accounting, contractual, or institutional contexts, it may refer to treating a matter as resolved, settled, accounted for, cleared, or responsibility-settled.

Therefore, translations should distinguish at least the following:

- 閉鎖: closure
- 内部ループを閉じる: to close an internal loop
- 意味ループを閉じる: to close a semantic loop
- 解決済みとする: to treat as resolved
- 決算済みとする: to treat as accounted for
- 清算済みとする: to treat as settled / cleared
- 責任処理済みとする: to treat responsibility as settled
- 偽閉鎖: false closure

False closure refers to treating something as resolved, settled, accounted for, or responsibility-settled while residuals or residues remain active.

## 5. Translation limits / 翻訳上の限界

Some terms are intentionally provisional. This is especially true where the theory touches formal space theory, logical-depth axis, quasi-temporal depth, residue assessment, imaginary-direction tokens, or negentropy economy.

Where mathematical, computational, or legal meanings are not yet formally defined, translations should avoid implying full formalization.

For example:

- 論理-深度軸 should not be identified directly with computational logical depth.
- 虚方向 should not be treated as a fully defined mathematical imaginary axis unless explicitly formalized.
- トークン should not be confused with cryptocurrency, securities, or LLM tokens unless the context explicitly defines it.
- 残渣査定 should not be treated as a legal audit, accounting standard, or certification regime unless the document explicitly says so.

## 6. Translation priority / 翻訳優先順位

When translating, priority should be given in the following order:

1. Preservation of conceptual boundary
2. Preservation of claim strength
3. Preservation of relation between terms
4. Readability in English
5. Stylistic smoothness

A smooth translation that erases residuals, overstates formalization, or converts a provisional concept into a settled claim should be avoided.

## 7. Correction policy / 補正方針

If an English translation is found to deviate from the Japanese authoritative draft, the correction should be made by one of the following methods:

1. Correcting the English text directly.
2. Adding a translator’s note.
3. Adding a glossary entry.
4. Marking the expression as provisional.
5. Returning to the Japanese source text for re-collation.

Translation correction is not merely linguistic correction. It is part of maintaining re-collatability across language games.

## 8. Japanese Authority and English Commensuration Policy

For theory documents, policy documents, research notes, and system maps, Japanese is treated as the authoritative text. English is provided as commensuration, not as a word-for-word translation or an independent authoritative version.

In such documents, Japanese and English should normally be separated into full blocks:
1. Japanese authoritative text
2. English commensurated rendering

README files may use a more accessible bilingual layout because their primary function is navigation, not theoretical authority. In README files, short Japanese and English explanations may be placed near each other for reader orientation.
