## 0. Positioning

This document is a boundary protocol for comparing different systems of thought, worldviews, disciplines, institutions, technological designs, and mythic systems as stable patterns of attention, abstraction, value, and cost, and for collating their commensurability and untranslatable residuals.

It is not a table for ranking which system of thought is correct.

Nor is it a diagnostic table for classifying people into ideological types.

This document owns one question:

> When different cognitive forms come into contact, what should be translated, suspended, connected, or retained as a residual without destroying the core of either form?

This document does not generate cognitive axes themselves.

The generation, stabilization, hardening, drift, branching, and reconnection of cognitive axes are treated primarily in [`Entropy_Attributed_Difference_and_Cognitive_Axis_Formation.en.md`](../02_Raj_Beauty/Entropy_Attributed_Difference_and_Cognitive_Axis_Formation.en.md).

This document does not determine purposes.

The work of setting something as a First Purpose and examining at the boundary what kind of world its sharing has formed is treated primarily in [`Truth_Management_and_Boundary_PDCA.en.md`](../00_Overview/Truth_Management_and_Boundary_PDCA.en.md).

This document stands between them.

It receives from cognitive-axis formation articulable patterns of attention, abstraction, value, cost, and residual handling; organizes them as modules that can be compared across domains; and passes candidate purposes, hidden costs, boundary conditions, and non-commensurated residuals to Truth Management.

### 0.1 Why This Document Belongs in Tam / Goodness

This document is placed in Tam / Goodness not in order to select a morally good system of thought.

Whenever a cognitive form is adopted, something is foregrounded, something is abstracted away, something is preserved as value, and something is processed as cost.

That cost does not necessarily remain within the observer.

It may be externalized to other persons, organizations, society, nature, future generations, or beings that remain unrepresented.

What Goodness takes responsibility for is not the moral ranking of systems of thought, but the reaction and responsibility generated by selection.

> What becomes visible through this cognitive form, what becomes invisible, who receives the cost, and which return paths remain open?

This document requires every module to answer that question.

## 1. Why Call Them “Optional Axioms”?

The axioms in this document are not strict axioms in mathematics or formal logic.

They are not foundational propositions guaranteed to be complete, consistent, or independent.

An Optional Axiom here is a cognitive point of selection that determines, when reading the world, what is placed first, what counts as evidence, what is treated as real, what is sent into the background, and which value is protected.

Examples include:

- placing material structure first;
- placing subjective experience first;
- placing relations or events first;
- placing measurability at the center of knowledge;
- placing responsibility, freedom, utility, beauty, community, or salvation at the center of value.

These are not single propositions that determine the whole world at once.

When multiple points of selection are combined and stabilized within a particular history and institution, they operate as a worldview, method, ethic, or organizational principle.

This is why this document calls them modules.

Modules can be conditionally connected or separated. One observer or organization may use multiple modules in different contexts. If the core is lost, however, the result is a different system even when the same name is retained.

An Optional Axiom Module is not a declaration of absolute belief.

It is a unit for making the points of selection that compose cognition explicit in a form that can be compared, commensurated, and criticized.

## 2. Role as a Boundary Protocol

Different systems of thought do not necessarily see the same thing even when they use the same words.

Terms such as “truth,” “freedom,” “nature,” “information,” “responsibility,” “nothingness,” “meaning,” “human,” and “safety” carry different structures of attention and abstraction in different systems.

Conversely, systems that use different terms may have closely related cognitive structures.

Connecting systems solely because their vocabularies match produces false commensuration.

Cutting them off solely because their vocabularies differ discards structures that may be connectable.

This document treats communication between systems of thought through the following boundary operations:

- **Pass-through:** The core of meaning can be translated without substantial loss.
- **Transformation:** The content must be rendered into another vocabulary or form.
- **Suspension:** The conditions required for translation are currently insufficient.
- **Cutoff:** Connection would damage the core or the responsibility boundary.
- **Reflection:** The receiving conditions do not hold within the other system.
- **Residual retention:** An untranslatable difference is not deleted as meaningless.
- **Re-collation:** The conditions of connection are re-examined through another history, case, or formalization.

In this sense, Optional Axiom Modules are not an inventory of ideologies. They are a communication protocol at cognitive boundaries.

## 4. Basic Axes of Reading

Each module is read, at minimum, through four axes: attention, abstraction, value, and cost.

### 4.1 Attention

Attention is what the system strongly sees as reality.

Physical law.  
Subjective experience.  
Institution.  
Power.  
Community.  
Suffering.  
Beauty.  
God.  
Symbol.  
Efficiency.  
Responsibility.

Attention is not merely preference.

It gives resolution to difference and determines what emerges within the system as a problem, evidence, value, or object.

### 4.2 Abstraction

Abstraction is what the system does not treat, backgrounds, cuts off, or sends to another process.

Abstraction is not necessarily a defect.

Cognition cannot take form without abstracting something away.

Abstraction nevertheless creates the blind spots of a system.

A system that sees only matter tends to abstract away qualia and meaning.  
A system that sees only inwardness tends to abstract away institutions and external responsibility.  
A system that sees only order tends to abstract away exceptions and suffering.  
A system that sees only salvation tends to abstract away real-world procedures and verification.

### 4.3 Value

Value is a difference that the system explicitly treats as worthy of preservation.

Truth.  
Freedom.  
Order.  
Community.  
Objectivity.  
Beauty.  
Efficiency.  
Responsibility.  
Body.  
Soul.  
Experience.  
Institution.  
Relation.  
Connection.  
Non-destructiveness.

In cognitive-axis formation, value operates as a pre-reflective weighting before cognition is fully articulated.

This document treats the stage at which that weighting becomes explicit as a system of thought, method, institution, or technological design.

### 4.4 Cost

Cost is a difference processed as a burden in order to preserve a value.

To protect freedom, instability of order may be accepted.  
To protect order, exceptions and particularity may be cut away.  
To protect objectivity, first-person texture may be suspended.  
To protect salvation, attachment to the actual world may be treated as a cost.  
To protect efficiency, margin, suspension, and redundancy may be reduced.

Cost is not merely a defect.

It shows what a system distributes as burden in order to protect what it values.

Cost analysis therefore asks not only how much cost exists, but who receives it, where, and when.

## 5. Modulation Axes

Even when attention, abstraction, value, and cost are similar, systems may differ in their dynamics.

This document uses the following four supplementary axes.

| Axis | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Variability | Almost fixed | Varies by context | Capable of major transformation |
| Hardness | Soft | Conditional | Hard; prone to doctrinal fixation |
| Cohesion | Dispersive | Moderate | Strongly concentrated around a center |
| Connectivity | Tends toward isolation | Limited connection | Readily commensurated across domains |

These numbers are not objective measurements.

Nor are they psychological scores assigned to systems of thought or institutions.

They are provisional annotations for collating where different readers evaluate a system differently.

### 5.1 Variability

Variability indicates how far operation may change with history, culture, individual, or institution.

### 5.2 Hardness

Hardness indicates how strongly the core proposition is fixed and how readily exceptions or dissenting interpretations are admitted.

Hard systems are strong in institutionalization and education but tend to cut residuals away.  
Soft systems are strong in translation and creation but tend to lose their core.

### 5.3 Cohesion

Cohesion indicates how strongly diverse differences are gathered into one center.

Cohesion produces a strong image of the world.  
Dispersion opens possibilities in multiple directions.

### 5.4 Connectivity

Connectivity indicates how readily a system can construct common interfaces with other systems.

High connectivity does not imply correctness or superiority.

A highly connectable system may have made its core ambiguous.  
A system that is difficult to connect may be strongly preserving an important difference.

## 6. Core Invariant and Operational Drift

If every system of thought or worldview is treated as wholly fluid, everything begins to look the same.

If every system is treated as wholly fixed, historical transformation, cultural difference, institutional variation, and individual difference disappear.

This document divides each module into two layers:

- **Core Invariant:** The core without which the system would no longer be that system.
- **Operational Drift:** The operational layer that changes through era, culture, institution, medium, and practice.

Even where the name of a system is retained, it may no longer be the same system if its central attention, abstraction, value, and cost have been reversed.

Conversely, even where the vocabulary changes, commensuration may be possible if the core cognitive structure is preserved.

Core Invariant is not a term for absolutizing doctrine.

It is a responsibility boundary for determining what, if lost in translation, would turn the system into something else.

Operational Drift is not a term for condemning deviation.

It is a historical layer for examining how far a system can transform under changed operating conditions while retaining its core.

## 7. Logical Space L0-L6

This document provisionally places modules across seven logical layers.

This is not a hierarchy of value.

Higher layers are not superior to lower layers.

The purpose is to avoid producing false conflict by placing different questions on the same plane.

| Layer | Name | Primary question |
|---|---|---|
| L0 | Metaphysical Ground | How are origin, totality, nothingness, and the furthest upstream condition of the world placed? |
| L1 | Ontological Composition | How are substance, information, elements, relations, time, and death placed? |
| L2 | Cognition and Method | What counts as evidence, knowledge, and collatability? |
| L3 | Value, Ethics, and Beauty | What is retained as good, beautiful, or worthy of protection? |
| L4 | Society, Institutions, Economy, and Technology | When purposes are shared, what boundaries and responsibilities are formed? |
| L5 | Interface between Self and Society | How is truth expressed, withdrawn, and modulated? |
| L6 | Operative and Symbolic Systems | How are naming, symbols, ritual, arrangement, and correspondence treated? |

The same system may appear across multiple layers.

Kant, for example, connects to the thing-in-itself at L0, phenomena and things-in-themselves at L1, forms of cognition at L2, and deontology at L3.

In such cases, the system should not be forced into one box. The module should specify which question it is being used to address.

## 9. Commensuration Protocol

When connecting different modules, use the following sequence.

### Step 1. Confirm the Layer and Scope of Application

Do not directly oppose ontology and ethics, empirical science and metaphysics, or institutional theory and individual practice as though they were claims of the same kind.

First determine what each system is attempting to explain.

### Step 2. Identify the Core Invariant

Determine what the system would have to lose before it became a different system.

Look not only at vocabulary, but at the combination of attention, abstraction, value, and cost.

### Step 3. Compare Attention and Abstraction

Are both modules looking at the same object?  
Is one deliberately abstracting away something that the other sees?  
Or are their objects different from the outset because they occupy different layers?

### Step 4. Compare Value and Cost

Determine what each module protects and what it processes as burden in order to do so.

In particular, make explicit who receives the cost.

### Step 5. Compare Procedures of Collation

What counts as evidence?  
What counts as falsification?  
What is treated as experience, tradition, logic, measurement, narrative, institutional history, or log data?

Where procedures of collation differ, do not compare conclusions directly.

### Step 6. Construct a Common Interface

Search for the minimum vocabulary, case, form, or boundary condition that both modules can share.

A common interface does not make the modules identical.

It is a limited port created to make communication possible.

### Step 7. Retain Untranslatable Residuals

Do not immediately delete what cannot be translated as error or meaninglessness.

Record what conditions are missing and why translation cannot yet occur.

### Step 8. Determine the Connection Type

Determine whether the relation is direct translation, conditional translation, complementarity, tension, orthogonality, incompatibility, or safety-based cutoff.

### Step 9. Preserve History and Return Paths

Record what transformations were performed, what was lost, and what was suspended.

Preserve a return path to the originating system.

## 10. Connection Types

### 10.1 Direct Translation

The cores, objects, and procedures of collation are close, and loss through vocabulary conversion is small.

### 10.2 Conditional Translation

Connection is possible if an appropriate scope or non-claim boundary is specified.

The relation between a field in physics and a history-field in Scientific Ontology would normally belong to this type.

### 10.3 Complementarity

The modules illuminate different blind spots of the same problem.

Resolution increases by retaining multiple perspectives rather than integrating them into one.

### 10.4 Tension

The distributions of value, cost, evidence, or responsibility conflict.

Tension should not be resolved immediately. It should show at which boundary a selection becomes necessary.

### 10.5 Orthogonality

The questions or layers differ. The modules may appear to conflict without directly opposing one another.

### 10.6 Incompatibility

Within the same scope, the modules hold core propositions that cannot both be maintained.

Even where modules are incompatible, it remains possible to collate what each one makes visible.

### 10.7 Safety-Based Cutoff

Connection or implementation carries a high risk of damaging another person's judgment-field, body, rights, or responsibility boundary.

In such cases, an operational port may be closed while intelligibility is retained.

## 11. Types of Collision

Collisions between systems can be divided at least as follows.

### 11.1 Same Word, Different Core

The same term, such as “freedom,” “nature,” “information,” or “truth,” is used while the Core Invariant differs.

### 11.2 Same Core, Different Operation

The value being protected is similar, but institutions, rituals, evidence, or procedures differ.

### 11.3 Same Value, Different Cost Distribution

The same purpose is declared, but the burden is returned to different agents or boundaries.

### 11.4 Same Attention, Different Procedures of Collation

The same phenomenon is addressed, but measurement, argument, experience, transmission, symbol, or narrative is treated as the relevant ground in different ways.

### 11.5 Layer Error

A metaphysical hypothesis is treated as a conclusion of empirical science.  
A model of empirical science is treated as an ethical command.  
An institutional convenience is treated as ontological truth.

This type of collision can often be reduced by separating the layers.

### 11.6 Destruction of the Core through Translation

Too much of the core is removed in order to make a system easier for another system to receive.

Even where commensuration appears successful, the connection is false if the originating system has been lost.

## 13. Cross-Module Treatment of Nothingness and Residuals

“Nothingness” does not have the same meaning in every module.

- In physicalism, it may be treated as the end or absence of a process.
- In a Kantian position, it may be reserved as something that cannot be positively described.
- In the philosophy of emptiness, it is treated as a denial of fixed intrinsic essence.
- In relation-grounded ontology, it may appear as relational cutoff or loss of a return path.
- In conflictual dualism, it may be fixed as enemy, evil, or darkness.
- In symbolic systems, it may be treated as the unnamed, the unplaced, or latent possibility.

This document therefore does not impose “nothingness” on every system as one independent, uniform category.

Instead, each module records under `residual_handling` how it processes the unconnected, unprocessed, unobserved, unknowable, cut off, annihilated, or latent.

Residual handling strongly reveals the ethical character of a module.

Does it suspend what it cannot process?  
Does it send it to another port?  
Does it exclude it as an enemy?  
Does it delete it as meaningless?  
Does it wait for a new form of classification?

Commensuration must examine these differences of processing before comparing conclusions.

## 14. Standard Science and Systems Not Usually Treated Together with It

This document does not place science, philosophy, religion, mythology, magic, institutions, and fictional worlds on the same scale of empirical truth.

Standard science has its own procedures of collation: measurement, reproducibility, falsification, mathematical formalization, and examination by specialist communities.

Its definitions and achievements are respected.

When quantum mechanics, relativity, thermodynamics, or other sciences are placed within Optional Axiom Modules, they must not be reduced to mere worldviews.

What is compared is their cognitive operation: what they make observable, what they formalize, and at which boundary their standard definitions hold.

Mythology, magic, ritual, divination, symbolic systems, and fictional cosmologies may also be treated where necessary.

This does not recognize them as empirical facts.

It collates how they foreground naming, symbol, correspondence, ritual, arrangement, bodily texture, and communal history that ordinary science or philosophy may tend to abstract away.

The important operation here is not adoption, but collation.

What does the system make visible?  
What does it make invisible?  
Which values does it preserve?  
Which costs does it externalize?  
Which boundaries does it open, and which boundaries does it tend to damage?

So long as these questions can be answered, systems that are not usually treated in the same framework may also become objects of the cognitive bridge.

## 15. Connection to Truth Management

Optional Axiom Modules do not determine the First Purpose of truth.

They show from which cognitive form a candidate First Purpose has emerged.

```text
Cognitive-Axis Formation
  What becomes visible and invisible
        ↓
Optional Axiom Modules
  How it is articulated as a worldview, value, or method
        ↓
Truth Management
  What is set as a First Purpose, shared, and examined
```

When passing a candidate purpose to Truth Management, attach not only its name, but also:

- the Core Invariant on which the purpose depends;
- the differences foregrounded by the purpose;
- the differences the purpose tends to abstract away;
- the costs required to preserve the value;
- the recipients of those costs;
- Operational Drift that may emerge during sharing;
- residuals and residues to be examined through Boundary CA;
- conditions under which the First Purpose should be stopped or revised.

This prevents a purpose from being operated through attractive language alone.

The world it forms and the burdens it returns outward become available for examination.

## 17. Non-Claim Boundaries and Prohibited Uses

This document does not claim that:

- one system of thought is superior to another;
- module placement can diagnose a person's character, ability, ideology, pathology, or bodily tendencies;
- one name for a system of thought represents all historical interpretations of that system;
- this document replaces standard accounts in the history of philosophy, religious studies, history of science, or political thought;
- magic, mythology, religion, or occult systems are empirically proven;
- scientific verification is unnecessary;
- manipulating attention, abstraction, value, and cost allows another person to be guided into any desired ideology.

This document must not be used for:

- hiring, labor evaluation, or educational selection;
- medicine, psychological diagnosis, treatment, or correction;
- religious instruction, recruitment, or persuasive manipulation;
- governance, surveillance, or ideological screening;
- fixed labeling of persons or groups.

The purpose of this document is not to erase differences between cognitive forms.

Nor is it to classify and manage other people.

Its purpose is to make explicit what can and cannot be commensurated, what should remain as residual, and how communication can be created while different boundaries remain distinct.

## 19. Conclusion

Optional Axiom Modules are not a ranking table for systems of thought.

They are a boundary protocol for constructing communication ports between different systems when cognitive axes become explicit as worldviews, methods, ethics, institutions, and technological designs.

Each module has a pattern of what it attends to, abstracts away, preserves as value, and processes as cost.

It also has a core that must not be lost and an operational layer that varies historically.

Commensuration does not mean integrating everything into one system.

It means identifying the core, comparing procedures of collation, constructing a shareable interface, retaining untranslatable residuals, and preserving a return path to the originating system.

Selecting a cognitive form entails responsibility.

One must take responsibility not only for what becomes visible, but also for what becomes invisible and to whom the cost is returned.

Only after that responsibility is made explicit are candidate purposes and images of the world passed to Truth Management.

This is the role of Optional Axiom Modules as a Cognitive Bridge.
