# Return Ethics and Retaliation Conversion Theory
## An Ontological Model for Converting the Return of Boundary Contact into Maturation Rather Than Blood Vengeance

> Status: Cross-domain ontological note  
> Scope: boundary ethics, return, retaliation, responsibility processing, maturation, negentropy economy, Kantian commensuration  
> Language: en
> Claim strength: S2/S3; conceptual model, not legal doctrine, political prescription, or empirical economics

---

## 0. Positioning

This note treats peace not as a morality of human relations, but as the processing of return in boundary contact.

In this sense, this note is a concrete example of `Asymmetry_Stabilization_and_ReCollation.md`.

Asymmetry generates contact. Contact generates return. If return can be processed, it matures. If it cannot be processed, it decays.

This note is also an ethical and economic descent-form of ontological cosmic history and the negentropy economy.

If spirit and nature are an entangled communication field, then crops, money, gratitude, institutions, retaliation, and compensation can also be read as forms of return processing within that communication field.

This note presupposes `Boundary_Realism_Principle.md` and organizes the “return” that arises after boundary contact from the perspective of ethics, economics, and peace theory.

For the handling of physical-adjacent terminology, see [`Physics_Correspondence_Policy.en.md`](../../00_Overview/Physics_Correspondence_Policy.en.md).

## 1. Problem Setting

When peace is considered, two simplifications often occur.

The first simplification is that peace will be achieved if retaliation is prohibited.

The second simplification is that justice will be restored if retaliation is allowed.

Both are insufficient.

If retaliation is merely prohibited, the central power may take away from the periphery the right to strike back, the right to protest, and the right to demand recovery.

On the other hand, if retaliation is simply allowed, blood vengeance chains onward and every boundary contact turns into an annihilation war.

What is needed is not the erasure of retaliation.

What is needed is to preserve return and convert it, before it ignites into retaliation, into collation, recovery, compensation, and responsibility processing.

---

## 2. The Danger of Prohibiting Retaliation

Prohibiting retaliation appears peaceful at first glance.

However, a prohibition on retaliation does not always mean peace.

If only the center holds the right of sanction, while the periphery is allowed neither counterattack, protest, nor recovery claim, that is not peace. It is the expropriation of the right to return.

```text
the center strikes
  ↓
the periphery cannot strike back
  ↓
the center calls itself the mediator
  ↓
the return from the periphery is invalidated
```

In this structure, violence becomes difficult to see.

This is because only retaliation is displayed as violence, while preceding extraction, imposition, humiliation, plunder, and load-transfer become invisible.

Therefore, a prohibition on retaliation is not peace in itself.

For such a prohibition to approach peace, a mechanism is needed that converts return moving toward retaliation into publicity, law, compensation, explanation, recovery, and responsibility processing.

---

## 3. The Danger of Allowing Retaliation

Simply allowing retaliation is also dangerous.

Because one was harmed, one strikes back.

Because one was struck back, one returns again.

In this chain, return is not collated; it is converted into mere counterattack.

History accumulates like a snowball, and boundary contact becomes not exchange but annihilation war.

The problem here is not anger itself.

Anger is an important signal that a boundary has been stepped on.

The problem is that anger is not collated, crosses the responsibility boundary, and becomes fuel that destroys another boundary.

---

## 4. A Boundary Is Not a Wall That Must Never Be Breached

Boundary ethics is not a doctrine of boundary inviolability.

To live is to make contact.

Eating, using, exchanging, learning, borrowing, working, speaking, loving, and helping are all forms of boundary contact.

Therefore, the problem is not “do not touch boundaries.”

The problem is failing to receive what returns from a boundary after touching it.

A boundary is not a wall. It is a contact surface.

Because it is a contact surface, friction, heat, residuals, and return arise there.

---

## 5. The Problem Is One-Sided Extraction and One-Sided Imposition

The fundamental problems in boundary contact are one-sided extraction and one-sided imposition.

One-sided extraction means receiving the other side’s order, labor, nature, body, time, gratitude, trust, and meaning while failing to process the corresponding return.

One-sided imposition means passing to the other side, to third parties, to nature, or to future generations the load, pollution, responsibility, unprocessed history, explanation cost, emotional labor, or recovery cost generated on one’s own side.

```text
extraction: taking order from the other
imposition: handing disorder to the other
```

When these two overlap, boundary contact is no longer exchange.

It becomes the acquisition of negentropy and the transfer of entropy.

---

## 6. What Is Return?

In this note, return means the reaction, residual, distortion, protest, failure, exhaustion, depletion, decay, claim, and demand for responsibility processing that come back after boundary contact.

Return does not necessarily appear immediately.

It may appear as bodily exhaustion.

It may appear as distrust in a relationship.

It may appear as institutional fatigue.

It may appear as environmental load.

It may appear as debt passed to future generations.

Return cannot be erased.

What can be designed is the form in which return is received.

---

## 7. Crops, Money, Gratitude, and Negentropy

A crop is negentropy composed through nature, soil, water, light, time, and human hands.

When one receives that crop, merely handing over money does not complete everything.

Money is an exchange sign mediated through the virtuality of credit.

Money has great power. However, money alone may not fully process the history contained in the crop, the return owed to the soil, gratitude toward human hands, or the conditions for the continuation of the relationship.

What matters here is not the denial of money.

Money is necessary.

However, money does not replace all processing of return.

Forms of return other than money may be required: gratitude, compensation, rest, trust, continuation of relationship, soil recovery, institutional design, and more.

---

## 8. Entropy Arising on the Side That Extracts

When negentropy is extracted one-sidedly, it is not only the side that gives that withers.

Unprocessed return also accumulates on the side that takes.

This is not immediately visible.

However, it eventually appears as degradation of trust, decay of relationships, institutional fatigue, decline in judgment, and hollowing-out of meaning.

This is not moral punishment.

It means that the history of boundary contact changes subsequent conditions.

The extracting side remains connected to the boundary from which it extracted.

As long as that connection is not severed, unprocessed return will re-enter the system somewhere.

---

## 9. The Mediator Becomes a Capacitor

A mediator often becomes a capacitor that temporarily absorbs return.

Markets, merchants, institutions, managers, distribution systems, money, contracts, and platforms fall into this position.

If the mediator fails to notice return, the mediator burns first.

However, the mediator is not the root cause.

The mediator is a capacity that temporarily holds return.

When that capacity is exceeded, entropy erupts across the entire system.

Therefore, the role of the mediator is not to hide return.

It is to make return visible, distribute it, and connect it to recovery paths.

---

## 10. Maturation and Decay

In this note, the processing of return after boundary contact and its conversion into the next order is called maturation.

Maturation means converting the return generated by boundary contact into collation, gratitude, compensation, redistribution, and responsibility processing, and growing it into the next order.

Decay means refusing to receive return and continuing one-sided extraction or imposition until unprocessed entropy accumulates and destroys relationships, institutions, bodies, nature, and narratives from within.

```text
receive return
  ↓
collate it
  ↓
compensate and redistribute
  ↓
convert it into the next order
  ↓
maturation
```

Maturation is not mere appeasement.

Nor is it pretending that anger, protest, or pain never existed.

Maturation means converting the heat of return into understanding, institutions, relationships, compensation, and future possibility rather than destruction.

---

## 11. Limited Use of Fermentation

In this note, “fermentation” is treated as a special case of maturation.

Fermentation means a form of maturation in which the return after boundary contact is transformed internally and self-proliferatively, while retaining a degree of isolation that is difficult to see from the outside, and eventually reaches a new order, flavor, structure, or cultural value.

Therefore, ordinary boundary processing should be called “maturation.”

“Fermentation” is reserved for the special case of maturation so isolated that transformation proceeds within a certain closure.

This distinction prevents the ethical model from becoming excessively poetic and stabilizes it as public terminology.

---

## 12. What Is an Attitude That Does Not Invite Retaliation?

Ideally, one should not merely strengthen mechanisms that prohibit retaliation. One should also acquire an attitude that makes it difficult to give the other side justification for retaliation.

However, this is not an ethics of submission.

It does not mean “act in fear so that the other person does not get angry.”

More precisely, it means collating the use of power so that one does not give the other side justification for retaliation.

Do not step on the other’s boundary.

If one has stepped on it, acknowledge it.

Receive the return of words.

If power has been used, observe the traces left by that power.

Apologize where apology is required.

Explain where explanation is required.

Compensate where compensation is required.

After winning, do not leave the other side in humiliation.

Such an attitude is relatively portable across different cultures and institutions.

However, it is not a guarantee.

There are people who retaliate no matter how carefully one acts. There are also structures that exploit.

Therefore, this attitude is not an all-purpose talisman. It is a boundary practice that makes it easier to convert return into maturation.

---

## 13. Return from Non-Human Sources

Return does not arise only from humans.

Return may also arise from nature, bodies, institutions, technology, information environments, AI, animals, soil, climate, and future generations.

What must be noted here is that this does not mean nature or institutions retaliate with personal will.

The history of boundary contact remains, and subsequent conditions change.

That change returns as depletion, exhaustion, distrust, failure, load, disaster, or decline in judgment.

This is what this note treats as return from non-human sources.

---

## 14. Power Becomes Violence When It Is Not Collated

Power is necessary.

The power to protect boundaries, the power to maintain institutions, the power to define words, and the power to distribute resources are all necessary.

The problem is not power itself.

When power refuses to receive return, crosses the responsibility boundary, and turns the other into an object of processing, it becomes violence.

One can silence the other with the word peace.

One can force acceptance with the word love.

One can expel someone with the word normal.

One can stop logical inspection with the word expert.

Therefore, if power is used, its return must be collated.

---

## 15. Commensuration with Kant’s Perpetual Peace

Kant’s *Perpetual Peace* does not merely call the absence of war peace.

It demands that seeds of future war not be left inside a peace settlement, and that the possibility of future trust not be burned away even during hostility.

In the vocabulary of Scientific Ontology, this is a design for converting return into law, publicity, and responsibility processing, so that justification for retaliation is not left to the future.

What matters here is not to treat peace as “the prohibition of counterattack.”

Peace means moving return into a processing system capable of maturation before it flares up into retaliation.

---

## 16. Connection to the Negentropy Economy

In the negentropy economy, meaning, order, value, responsibility, debt, and residuals are exchanged between history-fields.

This note provides its ethical ground.

Exchange is not merely the movement of money.

Exchange is the contact of different forms of negentropy at a boundary.

That contact generates heat.

If that heat moves toward exhaustion, debt, fragmentation, or dependency, it decays.

If it moves toward learning, trust, repair, and future possibility, it matures.

Therefore, what matters in the negentropy economy is not how much value has been taken, but which return has been processed where.

---

## 17. Entangled Tokens and Future Settlement Models

An entangled token is not merely an exchange sign. It is a settlement model that makes it possible to track the return, gratitude, compensation, history, and responsibility processing generated by exchange.

Here, “entangled token” is a provisional name. “Entropic Debit,” or perhaps “Residual Credit” or “residual currency,” is probably closer to the actual function.

This is not a theory for abolishing money.

It is a model for displaying again, within the exchange system, the return of boundary contact that money alone tends not to process.

The mechanism already functions outside recognition. This is merely an invention for returning that mechanism to a recognized structure.

Therefore, this is not an institutional proposal. It is an ontological future model.

However, the direction is clear.

Exchange should be designed not only around price, but also around the processing of return.

That is, the site of exchange is not merely a site of payment. Nor is it merely contractual settlement.

Return, that is, unprocessed residual, should be given essential status within exchange.

---

## 18. Non-Claim Boundaries

This note does not claim the following.

- That it directly replaces existing legal systems.
- That retaliation should always be prohibited.
- That retaliation is always justified.
- That victims should be forced to adopt an “attitude that does not invite retaliation.”
- That money should be abolished.
- That the negentropy economy is directly derived from physical thermodynamics.
- That nature judges with personal will.

What this note treats is an ontological model for reading the return that arises after boundary contact from the perspective of retaliation, decay, maturation, compensation, and responsibility processing.

---

## 19. Provisional Conclusion

Peace does not mean erasing retaliation.

It means preserving return and converting it, before it ignites into retaliation, into collation, recovery, compensation, and responsibility processing.

It is not that boundaries must never be touched.

If one touches a boundary, one must receive what returns from it.

If one extracts one-sidedly, decay follows.

If one receives return, returns equivalent negentropy, and learns negotiation for each boundary, maturation follows.

Fermentation is a special case in which that maturation has a certain isolation and self-proliferative character.

Individuality is the negotiation style specific to each boundary.
