## 0. Purpose

This paper does not replace the Standard Model of particle physics.

It does not deny existing mathematical formalisms, experimental values, predictive accuracy, gauge theory, or the effectiveness of the Standard Model. What this paper attempts is to organize how particle attributes, interactions, symmetries, and boundary information appearing in the Standard Model may be reread in the vocabulary of history-field topology when the minimal additional assumption of **intrinsic time** is introduced.

Therefore, this paper is not a completed physical theory. It is a correspondence table.

It is not an assertion, but a proposal.

It is not a proof, but a map for research.

## 1. Minimal Additional Assumption: Intrinsic Time

This paper adds only one assumption.

**Intrinsic time.**

Here, intrinsic time does not mean external clock time.

It refers to the update order, phase, capacity for operational correspondence, and mode of historical folding that a history unit or field carries internally.

If external time indicates order within spacetime, intrinsic time is the condition that determines what kind of historical updates an entity carries internally, with which others it can enter operational correspondence, and at which boundaries it can interact.

When this assumption is introduced, particle attributes can be read not as fixed labels, but as attributes stabilized at an observational cross-section by a history-field that carries intrinsic time.

## 3. Bosons: Histories That Reach and Histories That Return

In ordinary spectral observation, what can be read is not simply what was emitted.

What can be read is a history that was emitted, propagated, passed through absorption, scattering, and re-encoding, and still reached the boundary of the observer.

On the other hand, some histories are emitted but quickly absorbed.

Some histories are excited but do not remain as externally readable histories, instead closing within the local field.

History-field topology reads this difference as follows.

**Bosonic history that reaches:** a history that reaches the boundary of an external observer and can be read as spectrum or interaction.

**Bosonic history that returns:** a history that is reabsorbed and re-established in operational correspondence within a local field, and does not appear as an externally readable history.

This branching between reaching and returning is not merely a metaphor. It may be treatable as a question of which fields a history unit with intrinsic time can enter operational correspondence with.

## 4. Rereading Spin, Charge, and Generation

When intrinsic time is introduced, spin can be read not merely as a metaphor of rotation, but as internal phase updating or as a solitonic vortex.

Charge can be read as a boundary attribute that determines which fields a structure can interact with.

In other words, charge is the connectability of historical operational correspondence.

Generation may be read as the possibility that particles of the same type possess different depths of intrinsic time.

In that case, generational difference is not a mere duplication. It is the stabilization of the same type of structure at different historical depths.

The purpose here is not to deny the particle classifications of the Standard Model.

Rather, the purpose is to reread from the side of historical operational correspondence and intrinsic time why those attributes appear as stable classifications at the observational cross-section.

## 5. Scope Toward Black Holes, Holograms, and Wormholes

This correspondence table has an intuitive scope toward the black hole information problem, the holographic principle, and wormhole-like connections.

However, it does not claim a mathematical solution here.

In history-field topology, the event horizon of a black hole can be read as the limiting boundary of historical readability from the perspective of an external observer.

Histories that proceed inward and histories that can be transmitted outward branch near the horizon.

To an external observer, the internal history does not arrive directly as a volumetric description. Rather, it appears to be re-encoded as information, entropy, radiation, or area-like structure on the boundary surface.

From this view, a black hole is not a place where history is lost, but a boundary where the readable form of history is transformed.

Wormholes can be read similarly as mappings that indicate the possibility that distant history-fields may be connected in a way different from distance in external spacetime.

However, this does not replace wormhole solutions in general relativity. It is a metaphorical and formalization-candidate reading of connection structures within history-field topology.
