## 1. Why Scientific Ontology Approaches Physical Vocabulary

Scientific Ontology does not attempt to explain the world from the same starting point as contemporary physics.

Contemporary physics precisely describes observable phenomena, measured values, conservation laws, symmetries, spacetime structures, and interactions.  
Scientific Ontology, by contrast, attends to conditions that precede or intersect with the formation of an object as an object: observation, history, boundary, meaning, communication, disconnection, return path, and collatability.

For this reason, Scientific Ontology does not replace standard physics.  
However, there are points where it inevitably approaches vocabulary such as particle, field, time, interaction, path, entropy, entanglement, Feynman diagram, black hole, and cosmic outer shell.

The reason is simple.  
Inside contemporary physics, these terms belong to measurement, calculation, and theoretical structure. From the perspective of Scientific Ontology, however, they are also boundary terms in which observability, reachability, communicability, mediation, collatability, and disconnectability appear in concentrated form.

For example, what is a particle?

In standard physics, a particle is treated as an excitation of a field, an object bearing quantum numbers, or a measured state.  
In Scientific Ontology, however, a particle is not treated as a point-like entity already existing independently in external space.

A particle in Scientific Ontology is a state in which a boson field, communication pathway, or history-field soliton appears as a local object at time zero or on a specific observational section.

Here, “time zero” does not mean a physical absolute origin.  
It refers to a cutoff point at which a communication pathway localizes on an observational section and appears as a particle-like object.

Therefore, reading a particle within Scientific Ontology does not mean looking only at where the point-like object is located.  
It means asking where the communication pathway that makes that appearance possible comes from, where it returns, where it knots, where it is cut off, and where it loses its return path.

The same applies to field.

A field in standard physics has standard physical definitions.  
From the perspective of Scientific Ontology, however, a field can also be read as a place where history is preserved, action is mediated, communication is established, and meaning is reconnected.

Therefore, this note does not avoid physical vocabulary.  
However, it does not overwrite the definitions of standard physics.

The terminology operation in this note follows these principles.

- Terms in standard physics are treated with respect for their standard definitions.
- Uses within Scientific Ontology are specified as conceptual models, ontological reinterpretations, structural analogies, or projections.
- They are not presented as empirically established propositions in standard physics.
- Possible physical correspondence is not confused with structural analogy.
- Similarity is not confused with identity.
- Unresolved issues are not treated as resolved.

Projection here does not, at this stage, mean a strict mathematical mapping.  
It means a conceptual correspondence that appears when a broader description containing history, boundary, communication, return path, and disconnection is restricted toward the side of physical diagrams.

Scientific Ontology is not simply borrowing physical terms.

In this sense, the cosmic outer shell is not a place too distant to reach.

In Scientific Ontology, null / void is not simple absolute nonexistence.

Null / void is read as a state in which communication is cut off in a history-field, the return path is lost, and collatability is closed.

Communication here does not mean mere signal transmission.

When communication is established, one possibility is selected as a reality section.  
At that point, possibilities that were not selected “cease to exist as possibilities” in that phase.

This does not mean that they are absolutely annihilated.  
It means that, in that history-field, observational phase, and communication pathway, they are no longer constituted in the same form.

Therefore, null / void does not mean simply that “there is nothing.”  
Null / void appears as a possibility that has lost its return path after communication has been established, or as a region cut off from collatability.

From this perspective, `BlackHole` can be read as a special boundary model of null / void.

## 9. BlackHole: A Boundary as an Accumulation of Cutoff Events

In this note, the conceptual model in Scientific Ontology is written as `BlackHole` in order to distinguish it from a black hole in standard astrophysics.

The `BlackHole` here does not deny black hole candidates, general relativity, gravitational lensing, accretion disks, gravitational waves, or event horizons in standard physics.

> `BlackHole` is an accumulation of events in which components of an approaching fermionic soliton cannot be maintained on the basis of that field and are cut off and decomposed.

The important point here is not to view `BlackHole` as merely a “place.”

`BlackHole` is not a container placed in space.  
It is read as an accumulation of boundary events in which components of an approaching fermionic soliton lose the ground of their own formation, cannot maintain their communication pathways, and are cut off.

A fermionic soliton is a state in which a boson field, communication pathway, or history-field soliton appears particle-like on an observational section.  
Therefore, approaching `BlackHole` does not mean that a point-like particle simply falls into a hole.

It means that the communication pathways, historical components, and boundary conditions supporting that particle-like appearance become unmaintainable at the cutoff boundary called `BlackHole` and are decomposed component by component.

In this sense, `BlackHole` is related to null / void.

However, null / void here is not simple emptiness.  
Null / void is a boundary state in which communication is cut off, the return path is lost, and collatability is closed.

In `BlackHole`, external communication that something “is there” is established.  
`BlackHole` affects the surrounding history-field.  
It changes the paths of what approaches it, generates heat around it, and leaves traces on external observational sections.

However, its inside is not constituted, from the external history-field, as a descriptive region with a return path available for collation.

Therefore, the inside of `BlackHole` is treated in Scientific Ontology not as a place where “something exists inside” in the ordinary sense, but as a boundary where an internal return path is no longer established through disconnection.

Heat here does not mean mere temperature. It means the pressure that unprocessable history, communication that has lost its return path, and components that have become non-constitutable leave upon the surrounding history-field.

Furthermore, because the internal return path is not established, information is read not as being stored inside, but as being returned to the surface.

This does not claim the information-preservation problem or holographic principle in standard physics as such.  
As a reading within Scientific Ontology, if the inside is not constituted as a descriptive region with a return path, then collatable information returns to the boundary surface, that is, to the surface side.

The physical projection of this state may appear as a structure close to the observational image of a physical black hole.

However, this note does not claim physical identity.

What this note treats is a boundary model in Scientific Ontology that reads `BlackHole` as communication cutoff, component decomposition, residual heat, surface return, and non-establishment of an internal return path.

| Relation to null / void | Not null / void, but a fold-back boundary | Generates null / void as communication cutoff |
