This note organizes why Scientific Ontology uses physical-adjacent vocabulary such as `boson field`, `intrinsic time`, `entropy communication`, `history-field`, `entanglement`, `Feynman diagram`, and `BlackHole`.

These terms are not used in order to replace the terminology of standard physics.
Nor does this note correct, deny, or replace completed theories in standard physics, quantum field theory, cosmology, general relativity, or standard quantum theory.

The purposes of this note are as follows.

1. To explain why history-field topology in Scientific Ontology can be read through a diagrammatic structure close to a Feynman diagram.

4. To reorganize the legacy PINGER hypothesis as a model of **communication pathways with intrinsic time** rather than as a particle-intrinsic-time model.
5. To state the generative hypothesis that communication transports **communication history itself** and simultaneously adds a new historical difference.
6. To distinguish pre-observational non-separable correspondence with the working term `sub-entanglement`, while managing its interference with standard quantum `entanglement`.
7. To reconsider bosonic and fermionic descriptions as candidate modes of transmission, mediation, localization, and retention of communication history rather than as SO replacements for standard particle classes.
8. To connect spontaneous symmetry breaking, as an SO/AMP generative hypothesis, with selected communication-relation density, intrinsic time, communication impedance, and a possible mass-like projection, while keeping the boundary to the Higgs/BEH mechanism explicit.
9. To read the cosmic outer shell not as a spatial wall, but as a boundary with a return path where communication pathways fold back.
10. To organize `BlackHole` not as the astrophysical black hole itself, but as a boundary model read through the relation between the communication of “being there” and `null / void` as communication cutoff.
11. To approach physical vocabulary while avoiding confusion between standard definition, ontological reinterpretation, structural analogy, conceptual model, and formalization candidate.

The object of argument in this note is not empirical verification.
What this note treats is internal consistency, explanatory connectivity, and preservation of unresolved residuals within Scientific Ontology.

Therefore, this note is not a “complete physical theory.”
It is a conceptual scaffold for using physical-adjacent vocabulary and an organization for later formalization and criticizability.

`PINGER` is retained as a lineage label, but the current working name of this note is **Intrinsic-Time (Sub-Entanglement) Communication Model**.

## 1. Why Scientific Ontology Approaches Physical Vocabulary

Scientific Ontology does not attempt to explain the world from the same starting point as contemporary physics.

Contemporary physics precisely describes observable phenomena, measured values, conservation laws, symmetries, spacetime structures, and interactions.
Scientific Ontology, by contrast, attends to conditions that precede or intersect with the formation of an object as an object: observation, history, boundary, meaning, communication, disconnection, return path, and collatability.

For this reason, Scientific Ontology does not replace standard physics.
However, there are points where it inevitably approaches vocabulary such as particle, field, time, interaction, path, entropy, entanglement, Feynman diagram, black hole, and cosmic outer shell.

The reason is simple.
Inside contemporary physics, these terms belong to measurement, calculation, and theoretical structure. From the perspective of Scientific Ontology, however, they are also boundary terms in which observability, reachability, communicability, mediation, collatability, and disconnectability appear in concentrated form.

For example, what is a particle?

In standard physics, a particle is treated as an excitation of a field, an object bearing quantum numbers, or a measured state.
In Scientific Ontology, however, a particle is not treated as a point-like entity already existing independently in external space.

A particle in Scientific Ontology is a state in which a boson field, communication pathway, or history-field soliton appears as a local object at time zero or on a specific observational section.

Here, “time zero” does not mean a physical absolute origin.
It refers to a cutoff point at which a communication pathway localizes on an observational section and appears as a particle-like object.

Therefore, reading a particle within Scientific Ontology does not mean looking only at where the point-like object is located.
It means asking where the communication pathway that makes that appearance possible comes from, where it returns, where it knots, where it is cut off, and where it loses its return path.

The same applies to field.

A field in standard physics has standard physical definitions.
From the perspective of Scientific Ontology, however, a field can also be read as a place where history is preserved, action is mediated, communication is established, and meaning is reconnected.

Therefore, this note does not avoid physical vocabulary.
However, it does not overwrite the definitions of standard physics.

The terminology operation in this note follows these principles.

- Terms in standard physics are treated with respect for their standard definitions.
- Uses within Scientific Ontology are specified as conceptual models, ontological reinterpretations, structural analogies, or projections.
- They are not presented as empirically established propositions in standard physics.
- Possible physical correspondence is not confused with structural analogy.
- Similarity is not confused with identity.
- Unresolved issues are not treated as resolved.

In Scientific Ontology, this diagrammatic character is treated not as the calculative system of standard QFT, but as a reading guide for communication pathways in history-field topology.

When history-field topology is restricted to spacetime coordinates, interaction points, propagation paths, and observational sections, it may appear as an interaction diagram close to a Feynman diagram.
However, this presupposes not treating spacetime as an absolute stage, but first treating diagrammaticity as a way to read communication pathways, knots, return paths, disconnections, and residuals.

Projection here does not, at this stage, mean a strict mathematical mapping.
It means a conceptual correspondence that appears when a broader description containing history, boundary, communication, return path, and disconnection is restricted toward the side of physical diagrams.

The same applies to the term `entanglement`.

In Scientific Ontology, history-fields may have correspondences that cannot easily be described by external spacetime order alone.

When this non-separable historical correspondence is restricted as a problem of separability in spacetime coordinates, it appears as a structure close to `entanglement`.

However, this does not refer to quantum entanglement itself in standard quantum theory.

The point is that a correspondence established on the side of history-field topology may, when projected toward the side of physical vocabulary, appear as a Feynman-diagrammatic interaction structure or as non-separability close to `entanglement`.

Scientific Ontology is not simply borrowing physical terms.

## 3. Basic Structure of the Intrinsic-Time (Sub-Entanglement) Communication Model

This model reframes the legacy PINGER hypothesis from a particle-centered model into a **communication-centered model**.

Its central question is:

> When communication is established, what is transported, what temporal structure arises, and how does the resulting history alter later communicability?

A provisional communication unit is written as

\[
\mathcal{C}=(\gamma,\tau_{\mathrm{int}},H,\Pi,O)
\]

where \(\gamma\) is a communication path, \(\tau_{\mathrm{int}}\) is intrinsic-time structure, \(H\) is carried communication history, \(\Pi\) is projection or mode of appearance on an observational section, and \(O\) is connection to observation or collation. This is a formalization candidate, not a completed physical definition.

### 3.1 Communication Is Broader Than Observation

The model does not identify communication with observation.

\[
\mathcal{C}\neq\varnothing
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
O(\mathcal{C})\neq\varnothing
\]

A communication path may therefore be defined in the model without a readable projection into the present observational system. Definability, however, is not empirical confirmation of existence.

### 3.2 Intrinsic Time and Sub-Entanglement

`Intrinsic time` is neither external clock time nor identical to proper time in relativity. It is the internal ordering of phase, folding, synchronizability, return-path possibility, and historical update within a communication path.

The working term `sub-entanglement` designates a pre-observational non-separable correspondence that supports communicability. It does not mean a weaker, lower, or hidden version of quantum entanglement, nor does it claim that standard quantum entanglement is itself a controllable communication channel.

Its limited meaning is:

> A relation structure in which two or more boundaries cannot be treated as completely independent histories before they are collated as one communicability or historical correspondence.

Whether restricted physical cases of this structure genuinely intersect standard quantum entanglement remains a research question.

### 3.3 Communication Transports Communication History

A core hypothesis is:

> **Communication transports not only content but communication history.**

Communication also adds the fact of its own occurrence to history:

\[
H_{n+1}=H_n\oplus\Delta H(\mathcal{C}_n)
\]

Repeated history can then be compressed into a pattern:

\[
h_n=\operatorname{Compress}(H_n)
\]

This compressed pattern acts as a heuristic that biases later path selection:

\[
h_n:\Gamma_n\rightarrow P(\gamma)
\]

The provisional generative sequence is therefore:

\[
Communication
\rightarrow History
\rightarrow Pattern
\rightarrow Compression
\rightarrow Heuristic
\rightarrow Next\ Communication
\]

Because a heuristic is a compression rather than history itself, the discarded differences remain candidates for residuals.

### 3.4 Bosonic and Fermionic Modes

This note does not appropriate the standard-model classifications of bosons and fermions. Within SO/AMP they are used as candidate comparative modes of how intrinsic-time-bearing communication history appears on an observational section.

Provisionally:

- **bosonic mode**: history appears as mediation, transmission, synchronization, or an active path;
- **fermionic mode**: history appears as localization, retention, stabilization, or a local state.

This is not fixed by a single time coordinate. In particular,

\[
\tau_{\mathrm{int}}=0
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
Fermionic
\]

A present section may still appear bosonically when it is functioning as communication, while a history that was bosonic in another phase may localize fermionically in the present projection. The relation to topology, boundary conditions, and spin/statistics remains unresolved.

### 3.5 Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking, Density, Time, Impedance, and Mass

The model explores whether “communication takes time” can be read not by presupposing an external clock but by saying that the mediating field acquires intrinsic-time structure.

A provisional generative chain is:

\[
Symmetry
\rightarrow
Selected\ Configuration
\rightarrow
\rho_{\mathcal C}>0
\rightarrow
\tau_{\mathrm{int}}
\rightarrow
Z_{\mathcal C}
\rightarrow
Inertial/Mass\text{-}like\ Projection
\]

\(\rho_{\mathcal C}\) is not defined as standard physical energy density. It is a candidate variable for communication-relation density: the degree to which communicable paths, correspondences, and junctions are established in a selected field configuration.

\(Z_{\mathcal C}\) is not dissipative friction. It is a candidate communication impedance: structural resistance to updating a path while retaining history.

Within SO/AMP, spontaneous symmetry breaking can therefore be reread as the generation of a selected communication density, intrinsic time, and finite update structure in a field. Saying that a mediating bosonic field, including light, has intrinsic time does not mean assigning rest mass to the photon; it means that the path is not a zero-length instantaneous relation but has internal phase and history.

If communication impedance projects observationally as inertia, a possible contact with mass-generation questions appears. A spin-0 scalar field is then structurally interesting as a field able to change background configuration without adding directional structure.

This has a strong interference surface with the Standard Model Higgs field and the Brout-Englert-Higgs mechanism. In standard physics, electroweak spontaneous symmetry breaking, the non-zero vacuum configuration of the Higgs field, and couplings associated with W/Z and fermion masses are part of the established framework. `Communication-relation density`, `intrinsic-time generation`, and `communication impedance` are not Standard Model terms.
In the Standard Model, electromagnetic \(U(1)_{em}\) remains unbroken and the photon remains massless. Therefore, reading intrinsic time into light in this model must not be identified with a claim that the Higgs field gives the photon mass.

The model does not require adding a literal faster-than-light tachyon particle. This does not establish that the negative quadratic term or so-called tachyonic mass parameter in standard Higgs-potential formulations has been derived away. The PINGER-side generative account and the Standard Model mathematical formalism remain different layers.

### 3.6 Compression Power and Falsification Surface

The value of the model is not that everything can be redescribed as communication. Its compression claim is meaningful only if the same intrinsic-time communication structure can reconstruct, without a separate auxiliary rule for each target, distinctions among observed and unobserved communication, historical update, heuristic formation, bosonic/fermionic appearance, symmetry breaking, mass-like projection, return boundaries, and cut boundaries.

If every target requires a new independent auxiliary rule, the model loses explanatory compression. If high-resolution constraints from different domains are preserved while the same history-bearing communication structure remains, the model becomes stronger within that scope.

`PINGER particle` is retained only as a historical label for one observational section of the model. The primary object is now a **communication pathway that transports history while adding history**.

## 8. Null / Void: Nonexistence as Communication Cutoff

In Scientific Ontology, null / void is not simple absolute nonexistence.

Null / void is read as a state in which communication is cut off in a history-field, the return path is lost, and collatability is closed.

Communication here does not mean mere signal transmission.
In Scientific Ontology, communication is the process through which an object is observed, given meaning, returned to the history-field, and changes later reaction or judgment.

When communication is established, one possibility is selected as a reality section.
At that point, possibilities that were not selected “cease to exist as possibilities” in that phase.

This does not mean that they are absolutely annihilated.
It means that, in that history-field, observational phase, and communication pathway, they are no longer constituted in the same form.

Therefore, null / void does not mean simply that “there is nothing.”
Null / void appears as a possibility that has lost its return path after communication has been established, or as a region cut off from collatability.

From this perspective, `BlackHole` can be read as a special boundary model of null / void.

## 9. BlackHole: A Boundary as an Accumulation of Cutoff Events

In this note, the conceptual model in Scientific Ontology is written as `BlackHole` in order to distinguish it from a black hole in standard astrophysics.

The `BlackHole` here does not deny black hole candidates, general relativity, gravitational lensing, accretion disks, gravitational waves, or event horizons in standard physics.

`BlackHole` is the following boundary model in Scientific Ontology.

> `BlackHole` is an accumulation of events in which components of an approaching fermionic soliton cannot be maintained on the basis of that field and are cut off and decomposed.

The important point here is not to view `BlackHole` as merely a “place.”

`BlackHole` is not a container placed in space.
It is read as an accumulation of boundary events in which components of an approaching fermionic soliton lose the ground of their own formation, cannot maintain their communication pathways, and are cut off.

A fermionic soliton is a state in which a boson field, communication pathway, or history-field soliton appears particle-like on an observational section.
Therefore, approaching `BlackHole` does not mean that a point-like particle simply falls into a hole.

It means that the communication pathways, historical components, and boundary conditions supporting that particle-like appearance become unmaintainable at the cutoff boundary called `BlackHole` and are decomposed component by component.

In this sense, `BlackHole` is related to null / void.

However, null / void here is not simple emptiness.
Null / void is a boundary state in which communication is cut off, the return path is lost, and collatability is closed.

In `BlackHole`, external communication that something “is there” is established.
`BlackHole` affects the surrounding history-field.
It changes the paths of what approaches it, generates heat around it, and leaves traces on external observational sections.

However, its inside is not constituted, from the external history-field, as a descriptive region with a return path available for collation.

Therefore, the inside of `BlackHole` is treated in Scientific Ontology not as a place where “something exists inside” in the ordinary sense, but as a boundary where an internal return path is no longer established through disconnection.

Through this cutoff, residual heat overflows into the surroundings.

Heat here does not mean mere temperature. It means the pressure that unprocessable history, communication that has lost its return path, and components that have become non-constitutable leave upon the surrounding history-field.

Furthermore, because the internal return path is not established, information is read not as being stored inside, but as being returned to the surface.

This does not claim the information-preservation problem or holographic principle in standard physics as such.
As a reading within Scientific Ontology, if the inside is not constituted as a descriptive region with a return path, then collatable information returns to the boundary surface, that is, to the surface side.

The physical projection of this state may appear as a structure close to the observational image of a physical black hole.

However, this note does not claim physical identity.

What this note treats is a boundary model in Scientific Ontology that reads `BlackHole` as communication cutoff, component decomposition, residual heat, surface return, and non-establishment of an internal return path.

## 10. Contrast Between the Cosmic Outer Shell and BlackHole

The cosmic outer shell and `BlackHole` are both boundary structures rather than ordinary objects.

The second is communication that returns to the surface through disconnection.

Through this difference, the cosmic outer shell and `BlackHole` have different ontological characters even though they are both boundary problems.

## 12. Provisional Conclusion

This note reframes the legacy PINGER hypothesis from a particle-centered model into the **Intrinsic-Time (Sub-Entanglement) Communication Model**.

Its present core is:

1. The primary objects are communication path, intrinsic time, communication history, observational section, and return path rather than a `PINGER particle`.
2. Communication is broader than observation; unobserved communication is definable in the model, but definability is not empirical confirmation.
3. `Sub-entanglement` is a working term for non-separable historical correspondence supporting communicability and is not identical to quantum entanglement.
4. Communication transports communication history and adds new history through its occurrence.
5. Repeated history forms patterns; compression of those patterns generates heuristics that bias later communication paths.
6. Bosonic and fermionic language is used internally as a candidate comparison of mediation/transmission and localization/retention, without replacing standard particle classifications.
7. Present-time zero does not by itself determine a fermionic mode; a present communication may appear bosonically, and a history that was bosonic in another phase may localize fermionically in a present projection.
8. Spontaneous symmetry breaking is explored internally as a generative transition toward selected communication-relation density, intrinsic time, and communication impedance.
9. This is not a rederivation of the Standard Model BEH mechanism. The Higgs connection remains a high-strength physics-adjacent hypothesis.
10. The model does not require a literal faster-than-light tachyon particle, but it also does not establish that the negative quadratic term of standard Higgs-potential formulations is unnecessary.
11. Feynman-diagrammatic paths, entanglement, the cosmic outer shell, and `BlackHole` remain interference surfaces and formalization candidates rather than replacements for standard physics.
12. The model is evaluated by whether one communication-history structure survives high-resolution constraints across domains, not by explanatory comfort alone.

This is therefore neither a replacement for standard physics nor merely a collection of metaphors. It is a high-strength research model for placing communication, intrinsic time, history, non-separability, symmetry breaking, mediation, localization, return, and cutoff on a shared generative coordinate and testing where the correspondence breaks.
