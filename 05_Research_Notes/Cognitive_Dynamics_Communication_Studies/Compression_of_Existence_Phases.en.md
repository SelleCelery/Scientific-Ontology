## 1. What Is an Existence Phase?

An existence phase is a configuration of roles, meanings, responsibilities, histories, evaluative spaces, and time horizons that an existence holds within a particular boundary relation.

Existence is not constituted through a single measure alone.

A person simultaneously holds multiple existence phases: worker, family member, friend, local resident, learner, consumer, creator, and embodied living being.

A company is not only a profit-seeking agent. It is also an employer, a bearer of technical succession, an educational agent, a safety agent, a regional agent, a bearer of environmental burden, and a cultural agent.

A state likewise holds multiple existence phases involving security, law, diplomacy, education, culture, history, welfare, environment, and intergenerational responsibility.

An existence phase is not a list of titles or attributes. Even for the same person, organization, or institution, the phase that becomes foregrounded changes when the boundaries it contacts, the responsibilities it assumes, or the histories it retains change.

An existence phase is therefore not a fixed component. It is a relational phase reallocated through each boundary contact.

---

## 2. Compression of Existence Phases

Compression of existence phases refers to the phenomenon in which multiple existing phases are aggregated into one or a small number of evaluative axes.

When a company is evaluated only by profit, a school only by standardized scores, research only by publication count, a state only by GDP, or a person only by income or title, multiple existence phases are compressed into a single metric.

Compression does not cause the object itself to disappear.

Compressed existence phases fall outside the visible surfaces of evaluation, institutions, budgets, and representation. They nevertheless remain as history in bodies, relationships, regions, cultures, environments, and future generations.

Compression of existence phases is therefore not merely information summarization. It is a boundary operation that determines what is counted as socially existent and what is sent into the background.

### 2.1 Distinction from Information Compression

Information compression aims to reduce the volume of representation while preserving necessary structure.

In existence-phase compression, what is regarded as structure worth preserving is itself determined by cognitive axes and power.

Even when the same operation takes the form of metricization, structure-preserving compression must therefore be distinguished from destructive compression that loses the Core.

### 2.2 Necessary Compression and Destructive Compression

Necessary compression limits what is processed while making explicit its scope, the differences it loses, and the conditions under which the excluded material may be reopened.

Destructive compression treats a limited metric as the essence of the whole object and regards the lost existence phases as irrelevant or without value.

The problem is not compression itself. The problem begins when compression forgets its own reach and losses.

---

## 3. Why Compression Occurs

Existence phases are multidimensional. Handling all of them at once requires time, knowledge, dialogue, responsibility, coordination, and a capacity to hold unresolved material.

Neither individuals nor organizations possess infinite processing capacity.

As social scale increases, common metrics, standard procedures, classifications, qualifications, prices, and rankings become necessary. Within the internal processing of large social operations, these are means of locally reducing branching, comparison load, and explanatory load.

The principal reasons compression occurs include:

- finite cognitive capacity;
- finite processing time;
- demand for comparability;
- standardization and statistical processing;
- simplification of responsibility allocation;
- acceleration of market exchange;
- reduction of explanatory cost; and
- ease of exercising authority.

Compression introduced to lower processing burden often does not eliminate that burden. It sends it to another boundary.

The more manageable an operation becomes within an organization, the more unprocessed difference may be transferred to workplaces, households, nature, regions, the future, or minorities.

---

## 4. Benefits of Compression

Compression is not evil.

Civilization cannot operate without compression.

Compression enables:

- rapid decision-making;
- comparability;
- market formation;
- statistics;
- legal institutions;
- standardization;
- automation;
- large-scale division of labor; and
- the systematization of normal processing.

Price compresses numerous conditions into a single exchange metric. A qualification compresses a certain educational history and set of capabilities into a short symbol. Legal classification compresses complex cases into types that can be handled procedurally.

The benefit of compression lies in increasing social processing capacity.

That benefit, however, must be evaluated together with the question of who assumes the differences that were not included in the compression.

---

## 5. Losses Produced by Compression

Compression readily drops:

- history;
- context;
- tacit knowledge;
- trust;
- locality;
- exceptions;
- bodily burden;
- long-term responsibility;
- untranslatable differences; and
- the quality of relations.

Existence phases rendered invisible disappear from institutions, markets, and evaluation.

They have not disappeared from reality.

In a company that does not evaluate technical succession, technical knowledge disappears when people leave. When household care is excluded from economic indicators, the burden of care settles as unpaid labor. When environmental burdens are excluded from price, nature assumes the residue.

The losses of compression alter downstream conditions while remaining unseen.

---

## 6. Compression as Redistribution of Unprocessed Difference

Through classification, standardization, and metricization, compression locally reduces the branching and comparison load that must be handled at once inside an operation.

The differences not handled in that operation do not thereby disappear. They can remain at boundaries, in histories, and along return paths as residuals, re-collation costs, or processing responsibilities.

```text
inside
  branching and comparison load reduced through classification, standardization, and metricization
        ↓
boundary
  excluded differences remain as residuals and re-collation costs
        ↓
outside
  processing responsibility and burden may be externalized to bodies, regions, nature, and the future
```

Compression does not eliminate differences.

It changes where, by whom, and when they are processed.

This redistribution is not a claim that thermodynamic entropy or Shannon information is physically transferred or conserved across a boundary. The operational objects of this note are unprocessed difference, re-collation cost, and processing responsibility under finite operation.

From this perspective, social dissatisfaction, cultural loss, regional decline, environmental problems, breaks in technical succession, and workplace exhaustion may be read not only as separate failures, but as existence phases excluded from an evaluative space and returned at another boundary.

A residual is a difference that remains available for re-collation. Residue is a historical burden that settles in bodies, institutions, environments, or relations even after processing has been declared complete.

---

## 7. Relation to Cognitive-Axis Formation

Existence-phase compression occurs downstream of cognitive-axis formation.

A cognitive axis stabilizes what receives attention, what is abstracted away, what is retained as value, and what is backgrounded as cost.

When that cognitive axis becomes fixed in institutions, evaluations, and metrics, existence-phase compression becomes a social mode of operation.

When attention is concentrated on profit, care, safety, culture, and time are readily backgrounded as costs. When attention is concentrated on order, exceptions, objections, and holds are readily treated as noise.

Dismantling compression therefore requires more than adding metrics.

It requires collation of what has been foregrounded as attention and what has been abstracted away in order for the present evaluative space to exist.

---

## 8. Relation to the Logical-Depth Axis

The Logical-Depth Axis is not the number of existence phases.

It is a depth direction for retaining residuals, contradictions, branches, and meaning pressure that cannot close on the present logical plane, and for reconnecting them with other language games, concepts, experiences, or institutions.

Existence-phase compression has two forms.

The first is structure-preserving compression, which shortens a path while preserving its central structure.

The second is destructive compression, which deletes nodes, history, and return paths, leaving only a short conclusion.

Structure-preserving compression can return to its original depth when necessary. Destructive compression cannot be re-collated because it does not record what was lost.

Differences removed from the present plane through compression may need to be sent to a Depth Port.

---

## 9. Intrinsic Time and History Networks

Whereas external time indicates elapsed clock time, Intrinsic Time is a concept for describing the density with which histories are connected, compressed, and transformed into subsequent response conditions within an existence.

Even within the same year, the formation of Intrinsic Time differs between an experience connected to existing history and organized into a meaning loop, and an experience that is merely repeated.

Compressing existence phases into a single metric reduces the diversity of paths through which history can form.

Organizations, institutions, and individuals consequently become myopic toward short-term results and less capable of retaining long-term history.

A compressed institution can decide quickly, while readily forgetting why it reached that decision and which existence phases it lost.

---

## 10. Perceptual Ports and the Three-Dimensional Spatial Axis

Existence-phase compression does not occur only through social indicators.

Human cognition itself constitutes the world through multiple perceptual ports.

In visually centered cognition, differences are readily constituted as configurations of objects along a three-dimensional spatial axis. Distance, direction, contour, front and back, and inside and outside are foregrounded through a visual-spatial port.

This is an important cognitive form, but it is not the world as a whole.

Temporal history, bodily sensation, meaning pressure, institutional responsibility, and distance between language games cannot be sufficiently represented through a three-dimensional spatial axis alone.

The three-dimensional spatial axis must therefore be treated as one among multiple cognitive ports. A difference must not be compressed into nonexistence merely because it cannot be visually positioned.

## 11. Compression by Domain

### 11.1 Markets

Price enables exchange, but may fail to represent trust, regional culture, safety, education, ecological restoration, or future burdens adequately.

### 11.2 Education

Scores and qualifications compress learning history, but may drop the ability to formulate questions, collaborate, recover, create, or connect learning with life.

### 11.3 Research

Publication and citation counts make circulation visible, but have difficulty evaluating the retention of unresolved problems, foundational maintenance, commensuration, or long-term conceptual formation.

### 11.4 Organizations

KPIs translate purpose into operation, but may conceal burdens outside the metric, successful anomalies, and dependence on particular individuals.

### 11.5 States

Compression into GDP, population, military power, or tax revenue backgrounds culture, autonomy, community, environment, and intergenerational responsibility.

### 11.6 Individuals

When a person compresses the self into income, educational history, social-media presence, or title, the whole person recedes into external evaluation.

### 11.7 AI

AI compresses its objects into objective functions, loss functions, labels, and benchmarks. A central question for AI ethics is what enters the objective, what is dropped, and to which port the resulting residual is returned.

## 12. Dismantling Compression and Reallocating Existence Phases

Dismantling compression does not mean increasing evaluative items without limit.

It means returning each existence phase to an appropriate boundary, time horizon, and responsible actor.

Markets should retain their First Purpose as markets, education as education, culture as culture, safety as safety, and research as research.

Diplomatic ports must then be established so that these existence phases do not destroy one another.

Reallocation requires:

- the scope of compression;
- a record of lost differences;
- holding of residuals;
- reopening conditions;
- routing to another port;
- commensuration between boundaries; and
- observation of the destination of externalized burdens.

---

## 13. Relation to Peace

Peace is not a state in which no existence phase is ever compressed.

Not everything can be handled at once.

Peace is a state in which necessary compression is performed without erasing other existence phases, so that they can be reopened through another port, at another time, or within another language game.

When distinct existence phases are forced into a single competitive space, education, research, care, culture, and daily life compete for the same external metric.

A condition of peace is not the elimination of competition, but the refusal to make the space of competition singular.

## 17. Provisional Conclusion

Compression of existence phases is a natural processing method of large societies and finite cognitive capacity.

Compression enables comparison, statistics, institutions, markets, and automation.

The histories, contexts, responsibilities, bodies, and time horizons dropped through compression do not disappear. Responsibility and burden for processing them are redistributed toward boundaries, where they alter downstream conditions as residuals or residue.

The question is therefore not whether compression should occur.

The question is what must be preserved, what must be held, what must be sent to another port, and what must remain reopenable.

Controlling existence-phase compression requires collation of cognitive axes, boundary diplomacy, port allocation, implementation of responsibility, and holding along the logical-depth direction.
