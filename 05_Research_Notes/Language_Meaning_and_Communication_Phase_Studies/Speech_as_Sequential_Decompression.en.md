## 0. Position

This document does not treat speech as the act of transferring a fully completed sentence from an internal location to the outside.

The question owned by this note is:

> How is the phase-dependently distributed whole pressure of “what one wants to say” sequentially cut into speech, separated into expressed portions and residuals, and reorganized through the speaker's own self-listening?

General Cognitive Field, meaning generation, qualia, residuals, residues, and communication in general are imported from upstream documents. This note specializes them to speech as one communication phase.

## 1. Central Thesis

Speech is not the transfer of whole pressure to the outside all at once.

Speech is a sequential movement in which differences that can be released in the present phase are segmented into speech through grammatical frames and port configurations; that segmentation distinguishes expressed portions from residuals; and the emitted speech is received again by the speaker, reorganizing residual pressure and the conditions of subsequent speech.

Conceptually:

```text
phase-dependent whole pressure Pφ
  ↓ segmentation through grammatical frames and port configuration
speech S1 + residual R1
  ↓ self-listening / response from others / change in the situation
reorganized whole pressure Pφ'
  ↓
speech S2 + residual R2
  ↓
…
```

This is not a numerical model. `Pφ` is neither a conserved quantity nor a single scalar. It is a distribution of differences that changes according to what the present Cognitive Phase foregrounds, backgrounds, and experiences as pressing.

## 2. Whole Pressure

### 2.1 Local Use

In this note, whole pressure does not mean a completed sentence already present before speech.

It is a state in which emotion, images, judgments, bodily sensation, relational history, the effect one wants to have on another person, outcomes one wants to avoid, reservations, and not-yet-observed discomfort remain insufficiently segmented while pressing speech in multiple directions.

Whole pressure need not consist only of components pointing in the same direction.

I want to tell them, but I do not want to hurt them.
I want to move closer, but I do not want to be rejected.
I want to clarify responsibility, but I do not want to condemn.
I want to explain, but I do not want to destroy it by putting it into words.

These do not cancel one another out to zero. They constrain the order, intensity, silence, and reformulation of speech.

### 2.2 Pressure and Bandwidth

Pressure may be read as a state in which differences requiring processing, expression, or return within a given phase occupy available bandwidth, or remain distributed beyond the bandwidth currently available.

This note does not, however, define pressure uniquely as bandwidth occupancy. Urgency, direction, competition, residual quantity, and bodily load are not the same variable.

## 3. Sequential Decompression

Whole pressure cannot be externalized all at once, not only because speech organs and attention are finite, but also because grammar, relationships, and situational constraints limit what can be expressed at a given moment.

The speaker cuts from whole pressure the sound and meaning that can most readily be released under the present conditions.

When one unit of speech is produced, the pressure corresponding to that portion is released outward. What remains, however, is not merely the remainder of a subtraction.

By saying it, one may learn for the first time what one intended.
One may realize that the wording came out stronger than expected.
One may feel that it was too weak to communicate anything.
An unintended implication may emerge.
The other person's response may foreground a different history.

Speech is therefore decompression and, at the same time, reorganization of pressure.

Speech also creates new pressure. A promise, declaration, insult, apology, command, or question can generate relations, obligations, expectations, and conflicts that did not exist before the utterance. For this reason, “sequential decompression” is not a complete definition of every function of speech. It is the central model for the aspect in which an unsegmented state is transformed into words.

## 4. Residual-Preserving Segmentation

Speech is a cutoff. It cuts a portion from whole pressure and externalizes it as speech.

There are, however, two forms of cutoff.

### 4.1 Destructive Cutoff

- Treat the sentence that was spoken as the speaker's entire true intention.
- Treat what could not be said as though it never existed.
- Close return paths for correction and re-collation.
- Fix personality, responsibility, or meaning on the basis of one expression.

### 4.2 Residual-Preserving Cutoff

- Distinguish what has been expressed from what remains unexpressed.
- Retain that the speech is one form of the original pressure, not the original pressure itself.
- Preserve a route back to residuals.
- Permit reformulation, supplementation, withdrawal, and transfer to another medium.

This note calls the latter **non-destructive segmentation** or **residual-preserving cutoff**.

Non-destructive segmentation here is not a definition of safety in general and does not mean avoiding distress. It preserves what went outward, what remains, and where one can return, without allowing the speech alone to replace the original pressure.

## 5. The Speaker Is the First Recipient of Their Own Speech

The speaker hears externally the sound they have produced.

Through this self-listening, differences that could not be observed before speech return to the speaker.

Pressure that felt unitary before speaking may, at the moment it is verbalized, separate into anger, grief, a demand for explanation, and a demand for apology. Conversely, things that had been scattered may come together as a single proposition.

Self-listening is not merely error detection. It includes:

- collation of what was said;
- collation of volume, speed, pauses, and bodily tension;
- differences between the speaker-image and the speech actually produced;
- updating predictions about the effect on the listener; and
- redistribution of residuals.

In this sense, speech is both an expression of thought and an external contact that generates and revises thought.

## 6. Many-Body Conditions Including the Other and the Situation

Speech does not copy identical content from speaker to recipient.

It contacts the recipient's history, emotion, attention, vigilance, vocabulary, grammatical understanding, and present bodily state, generating a different movement.

Moreover, not only the speaker and recipient but also institutions, observers, past conversations, future evaluation, media, and roles participate in the present sentence.

The effect of speech therefore cannot be uniquely determined from the sentence alone.

This note does not claim ownership of this many-body structure as a general theory of communication. Meaning formation after speech returns to [`Meaning_as_Collation_and_Return_Path.en.md`](./Meaning_as_Collation_and_Return_Path.en.md) and the upstream Meaning Generation Model.

## 7. Difference from Writing

In conversation, a sound once released cannot be fully retrieved.

In writing, externalized speech can be rearranged, deleted, expanded, and reordered. Writing therefore includes, in addition to sequential decompression, the addition, retrieval, preservation, and transfer of pressure.

Written sentences are nevertheless not the original pressure itself. Revision may reduce residuals, but smooth prose may also conceal them.

## 8. Collation with External Research

### 8.1 Levelt's Model of Speech Production

Levelt's research on speech production distinguishes conceptualization, grammatical and phonological formulation, articulation, and self-monitoring. This provides a point of collation with whole pressure, grammatical frames, speech, and self-listening in this note.

Whole pressure here is broader than a preverbal message, however. It includes body, emotion, relationships, directions the speaker wishes to conceal, and the speaker's reorganization after speaking. The models are therefore not identical.

### 8.2 Dynamic Semantics

Dynamic semantics treats sentence meaning not only as fixed content but also in terms of its capacity to change context. This note likewise treats speech as changing the conditions of subsequent speech.

It does not replace formal semantics. It is a conceptual model of a communication phase that also includes body, relation, and residuals.

### 8.3 Luhmann

Luhmann distinguishes communication as selections of information, utterance, and understanding rather than as transfer of internal content. This contacts the present model in its refusal to identify a speaker's internal whole pressure with the speech that is produced.

This note nevertheless does not erase the subject's body, injury, or self-listening.

## 9. Failure Modes

1. **Completed-script hypothesis**
   Assuming that a complete sentence already exists internally before speech.

2. **Single-pressure hypothesis**
   Reducing whole pressure to one intensity value.

3. **Absolutization of the uttered sentence**
   Treating one sentence as the entirety of personality or intention.

4. **Decompression universalization**
   Ignoring that speech can generate new pressure, obligations, and relations.

5. **Omission of self-listening**
   Failing to receive differences returned by one's own speech.

6. **Recipient-copy model**
   Assuming that the speaker's meaning is copied unchanged into the recipient.

## 10.1 Definitional Residues

- Defining the state as “whole pressure” foregrounds a phase in which the pre-speech state is experienced as one whole, but does not adequately type multiple independent pressures, unconscious processes, bodily metabolism, or external pressure generated by institutions.
- Describing speech as “decompression” backgrounds its capacity to generate relationships, obligations, questions, and new pressure for inquiry.
- “Residual-preserving cutoff” explains a return path to residuals, but the existence of a return path must not be equated with actual re-collation.
- “Speech” is used locally for an utterance unit, not only voiced sound; its boundary with gesture, silence, writing, and media is returned to the Grammar document.

## 11. Non-Claim Boundary

- Whole pressure is not claimed to be a measurable physical quantity.
- Speech is not reduced entirely to decompression.
- Human subjectivity is not claimed to be fully explained by Levelt's model.
- Speaker intention is not treated as the sole correct interpretation.
- A recipient's felt response alone does not determine the speaker's personality.
- AI distributional states are not identified with human whole pressure.
- Entropy-like distributions in this document are not identified with entropy in standard thermodynamics or information theory.
