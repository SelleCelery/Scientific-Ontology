## 0. Position

Whole pressure before speech is not transferred outward unchanged.

What is placed as a subject? What is closed as a predicate? Which forms are used for cause, time, comparison, hypothesis, reservation, quotation, or silence? Who may speak, through which medium, in what order, and with what responsibility?

These frames change which speech can be segmented from the same whole pressure.

This note treats the repeatable structures that constrain this segmentation and transport as multilayer grammar.

## 1. Central Thesis

Grammar is not merely an outer form applied to already completed meaning.

> A grammatical frame is a repeatable set of constraints within a language game that determines which differences are foregrounded, how they are segmented into order, relation, and responsibility, through which port configuration they pass outward, and what is sent into the background as residual.

A grammatical frame passes pressure through a locally expressible order. In that sense, grammar functions as a channel of meaning pressure.

The channel, however, does not carry pressure neutrally. The cross-section of the channel changes the shape of the pressure. Choosing a grammar is also the act of realizing one relation from the original pressure and residualizing other relations.

## 2. Grammatical Frames Are Not Limited to Speech

Grammar is not syntax alone. Word order, discourse order, honorifics, silence, gesture, media, and institutionally defined speaking positions constrain what can be segmented and what can be placed onto a return path.

This does not mean that every rule or cultural pattern should be called grammar. The five layers in the next section are a local classification intended to distinguish the ranges that act directly on speech, interpretation, and return paths.

## 3. Five Layers of Grammar

This note distinguishes at least five layers.

| Layer | Main objects | What it segments | Typical residuals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linguistic grammar | Word order, morphology, particles, tense, aspect, modality | Who did what and how; certainty, inference, condition | Ambiguous agency, overlapping time, textures not contained in words |
| Discourse grammar | Presupposition, topic, order, quotation, repetition, genre | Where to begin, what to center, where to close | Alternative causal sequences, deferred questions, unresolved foreshadowing |
| Relational grammar | Honorifics, forms of address, distance, role, authority | Who speaks to whom and from what position | Asymmetry of speaking rights, mixture of intimacy and domination |
| Bodily/gestural grammar | Voice, pauses, posture, gaze, expression, silence, gesture | Non-propositional direction, pressure, refusal, approach | Mismatch between words and body, cultural dependence, overreading |
| Media/institutional grammar | UI, forms, meeting procedure, character limits, publication scope, approval fields | What can be entered, stored, spoken, or decided | Histories that cannot be entered, objections outside authority, bodily loads outside the institutional form |

These five layers are not independent boxes. Several layers overlap in a single utterance.

For example, “There is no problem” is grammatically an affirmative statement. But if it is spoken at the end of a meeting, as a response to a superior, with downcast eyes, or within an interface that permits only Yes/No, it may function as acceptance, resignation, coercion, or reservation.

## 4. Not Everything Is Grammar

Broadening grammar carries a risk.

If every repeated action, institution, or bodily response is called grammar, the concept loses explanatory force.

This note uses “grammatical frame” in the broad sense only when the candidate satisfies the following conditions:

1. It constrains what is recognized as a valid difference.
2. It constrains the order, relation, or role in which differences can be arranged.
3. It is repeatable across similar situations.
4. Deviation produces responses such as incomprehensibility, rudeness, invalidity, or acting outside one's authority.
5. Even without full conscious awareness by the user, it changes the form of output and return.

A one-off accidental gesture is therefore not grammar by itself. But if a gesture is repeated within a community and stably segments relations such as insult, submission, or refusal, it becomes a candidate for bodily/gestural grammar.

## 5. Grammatical Frames Segment Pressure

The same event can externalize different pressure depending on its grammatical frame.

```text
I was in trouble because you did not come.
When I was in trouble, you were not there.
I think you probably had reasons you could not come. Still, I was in trouble.
```

The first sentence strongly connects cause and responsibility. The second places the situation and absence side by side. The third separates the possibility of understanding from the injury that remained.

Even when the underlying event is the same, the grammar chosen changes the responsibility allocation, reservation, and emotional pressure passed to the recipient.

A grammatical frame does not mechanically extract meaning from pressure. It transforms pressure into relational forms that are currently available.

## 6. Grammatical Cutoff and Frame-Induced Residuals

Choosing a particular grammatical frame simultaneously creates what can be expressed and what becomes difficult to express.

This note calls differences that remain unsegmented because a grammatical frame was adopted **frame-induced residuals**.

For example, grammar that attributes a cause to one agent makes responsibility explicit while backgrounding institutions, chance, groups, or long histories.

Conversely, passive or subjectless expressions can preserve multiple causes or state changes while making an acting agent less visible.

The choice of grammar is not reducible to correct versus incorrect. What was preserved and what was dropped must be returned to a residual ledger.

## 7. Grammatical Frames and Port Configuration

A grammatical frame and a port are not the same.

- A **Port** specifies a point of contact: what is received, under what conditions, from where, and where it can be returned.
- A **port configuration** includes the order, synchronization, delay, authority, and return relations among multiple ports.
- A **grammatical frame** constrains how differences passing through that port configuration are segmented into relations and order.

Port 4042 has no meaning in itself. Even when the same port carries a bit sequence, different protocols, sequences, and histories can produce different sentences.

Likewise, meaning should not be assigned to the single port through which one word or sentence was output. Meaning is formed at the next stage through the integration of multiple speech units, residuals, returns, and histories.

Rather than positing a single “definition port,” the present model fits better with **definition port groups** that separately open definition, examples, counterexamples, lineage, non-identity, residuals, and return paths. The general canonicalization of this term remains on hold.

## 8. What Do Gesture and Institutions Say?

Grammar is not closed within spoken language.

An insulting gesture can segment the other person's position, speaking rights, and relational boundary even without carrying a proposition.

Institutions work similarly.

A speaking turn is limited to three minutes. Only Yes/No is available. There is no objection field. Minutes are summarized. Changes are impossible after approval.

Even without directly ordering content, these conditions govern what can be said, what is recorded, and what becomes residual.

Gesture and institutions are called grammar here not because they are identical with language, but because of a structural correspondence: they constrain the segmentation, ordering, roles, and returns of differences.

## 9. Grammatical Frames Generate Pressure

Grammar does not merely carry pre-existing pressure.

A question creates an obligation to answer. An imperative creates role and the possibility of obedience. A contract fixes responsibility. Honorifics create distance and can also soften it. A public post creates an audience and evaluative pressure.

A grammatical frame is therefore both a channel and a boundary condition for the next whole-pressure state.

Pressure remaining after an utterance includes not only the unreleased portion of the original pressure but also relational pressure newly generated through grammaticalization.

## 10. Collation with External Research

### 10.1 Wittgenstein

Language games provide a point of collation for understanding the meaning of a word not as an isolated definition but in relation to use, activity, and forms of life. This note likewise treats grammatical frames not as syntax alone but as local operations involving actual action and return.

It does not claim that Scientific Ontology reduces every language game to one underlying structure.

### 10.2 Saussure

The view that value arises through differences within a system provides a basis for reading the meaning of a node through relational position rather than isolated attributes.

This note also addresses how grammatical frames themselves change through history and contact, rather than only static systems of difference.

### 10.3 Speech-Act Theory, Pragmatics, and Conversation Analysis

The fact that speech can establish promises, orders, apologies, and institutional acts rather than merely describe facts, and that turns, repairs, and silences alter conversational operation, provides points of collation with multilayer grammar.

This note does not merge these fields into one theory. It compares which layer of grammar segments what.

## 11. Failure Modes

1. **Reduction to school grammar**: treating syntax and morphology as the whole of grammar and overlooking relational, bodily, and institutional constraints.
2. **Grammar universalization**: calling every regularity grammar and emptying the concept.
3. **Transparent-channel hypothesis**: assuming that the same pressure passes unchanged through different grammatical frames.
4. **Confusion with ports**: treating contact points and segmentation rules as the same thing.
5. **Residual erasure**: treating a natural sentence as proof that the original pressure was completely expressed.
6. **Bodily overreading**: assigning a unique proposition to gesture or silence.
7. **Institutional naturalization**: treating forms and UI constraints as neutral operations.

## 12. Local Uses of High-Density Terms

| Term | Local use in this document | Not identical to | Definitional residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grammar | Repeatable frame constraining validity of differences, order, relation, role, and return | All social norms, or school grammar alone | One-time emergence, gestures before regularization |
| Channel | Operational surface that transforms pressure into local order and passes it onward | A physical pipe, a Shannon channel itself | The side on which the channel generates pressure |
| Pressure | Distribution of differences that demands grammaticalization and is reorganized by it | A single emotion, physical pressure | Types of pressure and substrate differences |
| Gesture | Bodily operation that segments relation and direction outside propositional content | Universal reading of internal states | Culture, bodily ability, misreading |
| Institution | Media/authority configuration constraining speech, recording, decision, and return | Society in general, the state in general | Boundary with non-institutional practice |

## 13. Definitional Residues

- Treating grammar as multilayered places syntax, pragmatics, body, and institution on a shared plane but compresses the domain-specific explanations of each field.
- Calling it a “channel” foregrounds direction and capacity but does not adequately express creativity, play, or formation through deviation.
- “Frame-induced residual” preserves differences not expressed by the selected frame, but cannot fully distinguish what existed beforehand from what was generated only through grammaticalization.
- Bodily/gestural and institutional grammar are promising extensions, but the expansion of the concept of grammar must be continually audited.

## 14. Non-Claim Boundary

- Not all social rules are claimed to be linguistic grammar.
- Gesture is not claimed to contain a unique inherent meaning.
- Grammar is not claimed to determine human whole pressure completely.
- Grammatical differences among Japanese, English, or other languages are not projected onto ethnicity or personality.
- Port configurations are not identified with neural organs or physical communication pathways.

## 15. Unresolved Questions / Depth Interface

- How far can frame-induced residuals be reconstructed on the recipient side?
