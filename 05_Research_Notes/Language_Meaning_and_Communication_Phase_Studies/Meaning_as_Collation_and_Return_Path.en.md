> Public handling: This note does not redefine meaning in general and does not provide a comprehensive definition of emotion or thought in psychology or neuroscience. Taking the upstream Meaning Generation Model as given, it describes an operational surface on which collation through propositions and speech groups forms linguistic apperception, and the local closure of a return path is felt as meaning. Within that scope, it proposes an operational distinction between emotion and thought as return-path states. Felt resolution does not guarantee truth; it is the qualitative texture of return-path closure.

## 0. Position

Receiving information, parsing a proposition, tracing a semantic structure, feeling meaning, reaching felt resolution, agreeing, and obeying are not the same operation.

Meaning in general is formed as a meaning loop in which qualia connect with history, emotion, thought, bodily sensation, memory, and action and acquire return. Ownership of that general model belongs to [`Meaning_Generation_Model.md`](../../01_Sat_Truth/Meaning_Generation_Model.md).

This note addresses one part of that general process: how linguistically mediated collation involving speech, propositions, sentences, and gestures forms a local semantic structure, and under what conditions the qualitative sense of “I understand what this means” arises.

## 1. Central Thesis

> Linguistic meaning is formed when speech groups segmented from phase-dependent whole pressure in a way that does not erase residuals form a linguistic apperception; collational movement through that apperception recursively returns to the collating subject; a return path is thereby formed back to the original pressure, history, and present conditions; and the conditions of subsequent recognition are changed.
>
> To feel meaning is for that return path to close locally, such that the collating subject receives as **felt resolution** the correspondence that the segmented form represents the original pressure in another form.

The conceptual sequence is:

```text
phase-dependent whole pressure
  ↓ residual-preserving cutoff
speech groups + residuals
  ↓ mutual reference / order / relation
linguistic apperception
  ↓ collation with original pressure / history / present conditions
formation of a return path
  ↓
qualitative local closure = felt resolution
  ↔ residual / residue / reopening
  ↓
change in subsequent recognition / speech / judgment conditions
```

Here, “closure” does not mean arrival at final truth. It means that the present collational movement has acquired enough of a return path to move provisionally into the next action or question.

When a movement closes as thought, its return path is formed within the collating subject. External objects can trigger or participate in collation, but they are not the responsible agents for completing the loop.

## 2. Semantic Cycles and Circular Reasoning

The meaning of a word can only be collated through other words, situations of use, actions, bodily responses, institutions, histories, and returns from others.

Meaning systems therefore contain cycles.

This cycle must not be identified with **circular reasoning in argument**, in which the ground for a conclusion simply returns to the same proposition without adding anything.

- Circular reasoning in argument recovers a premise as the conclusion without adding grounds.
- Operational recursion in meaning passes a word through use, counterexamples, the body, and returns from others; every return can alter semantic scope and conditions of recognition.

A semantic cycle is not a motion around an unchanged point.

It returns with history. It receives difference. The range of use may narrow or widen. Meaning that had once closed may reopen.

This note therefore does not argue that “because words are circular, anything can mean anything.” Resistance of the source, histories of use, the body, counterexamples, returns from others, and non-claim boundaries constrain the cycle.

## 3. Why Does Closure Produce Meaning?

When whole pressure remains unsegmented, it is difficult to distinguish what has already been expressed from what is still present.

Speech that has passed through a grammatical frame transforms part of that pressure into another form.

This segmentation contributes to meaning formation because it does not erase the original pressure and makes at least the following distinguishable:

1. what has already been expressed;
2. the correspondence between the expressed form and the original pressure;
3. residuals that have not yet acquired a return path;
4. residues that continue to operate after something has been treated as processed, even though their origin has become difficult to read;
5. the direction of the next collation.

This note treats this operation as **residual-preserving cutoff** or **non-destructive segmentation**.

When the segmented form is felt to represent the direction, relation, and difference of the original pressure sufficiently, even without perfectly reproducing it, the collating subject receives a sense of “this is it” or “that is what it was.”

This qualitative fit is felt resolution.

Felt resolution does not prove that a proposition is true. Incorrect explanations, authority, exhaustion, fear, and smooth prose can also produce it.

What felt resolution indicates is, at minimum, that some return path has locally closed within the collating subject. Whether that closure is adequate must be re-collated through residuals, counterexamples, external consequences, testimony from others, and returns from the body.

## 4. Speech Groups and Linguistic Apperception

Meaning need not be completed by a single word, sentence, or port.

When multiple speech units, silences, examples, corrections, and replies mutually refer to one another and form a configuration that can return to one original pressure or question, a linguistic apperception arises.

In this note, **linguistic apperception** is an integrative structure that makes multiple speech units and residuals collatable as one semantic movement.

This is not compression of everything into one conclusion.

Two contradictory sentences can be integrated as different sides of the same conflict. No answer may be reached while the shape of the question becomes clear. Identifying what remains unresolved can itself be a form of apperception.

Linguistic apperception relates multiple speech units without making them identical.

## 5. A Single Port Does Not Own Meaning

A port is a contact point.

One word, one sentence, one UI input, or one utterance participates in meaning formation. If meaning is assigned only to that point, however, history, order, return, and residuals are lost.

Meaning is formed through configurations of port groups, port sequences, passage order, time differences, authority, bandwidth, and return.

```text
single port
  ↓
transfer of speech
  ↓
mutual collation across port groups and sequences
  ↓
linguistic apperception
  ↓
branching into return-path formation / non-closure / false closure
```

For this reason, a definition is not owned by one sentence alone. It is operated through a port group including authoritative definition, local use, examples, counterexamples, non-identity, residuals, and return paths.

## 6. Stages from Communication to Action

The following stages must be distinguished.

| Stage | Minimum condition | What may still not have been established |
|---|---|---|
| Signal arrival | A sequence of signs, sound, or gesture arrives | Parsing, understanding |
| Formal parsing | Words, syntax, or formal structure are identified | Referent, contextual understanding |
| Proposition identification | What is being stated is provisionally reconstructed | Speech act, effect |
| Communication understanding | Difference between informational content and speech act is received | Collation with one's own history |
| Linguistic semantic structure | Collation through apperception forms a return path | Felt meaning, felt resolution, agreement |
| Felt meaning / felt resolution | Correspondence with original pressure or history is felt as local closure | Truth, agreement, solution |
| Acceptance | The content is provisionally adopted | Agreement, obedience |
| Agreement | Mutually adoptable propositions or procedures are shared | Identical understanding, happiness |
| Obedience | An instruction is followed | Understanding, felt resolution, legitimacy |
| Action | External conditions are changed | Responsibility processing, semantic closure |
| Responsibility return | Consequences return to the judgment subject and alter subsequent conditions | Repair, forgiveness |

Depending on grammar and relational conditions, “I understand” may indicate parsing, understanding, felt resolution, acceptance, or obedience.

## 7. Local Closure, Non-Closure, False Closure, and Reopening

### 7.1 Local Closure

A state in which, with respect to the present question, correspondence with the original pressure and the location of residuals have been received sufficiently to acquire a return path that allows movement onward.

### 7.2 Non-Closure

Speech exists, but no return path to the original pressure has been found, and differences continue to float, repeat, or press.

Non-closure is not necessarily failure. Resolution may require greater resolution, a reply from another person, another grammatical frame, or a return path that cannot go directly to the original addressee.

### 7.3 False Closure

A state in which residuals remain, but authority, exhaustion, pressure, institutions, or explanatory smoothness cause the matter to be treated as closed.

False closure can produce a feeling of understanding or relief. Felt resolution alone therefore cannot guarantee adequate closure.

### 7.4 Reopening

New experience, counterexamples, testimony from others, bodily reactions, or commensuration residuals can return a previously closed meaning loop to a question.

Reopening does not necessarily mean that the previous meaning was worthless. A closure that functioned locally can later prove insufficient in a broader history-field.

## 8. Return-Path States of Emotion, Thought, and Love

This section does not offer a comprehensive psychological or neuroscientific definition of emotion in general, thought in general, or love in general. What follows is an operational hypothesis for distinguishing return-path states within Scientific Ontology's research on language, meaning, and communication phases.

> Emotion is a state in which a directional difference remains as pressure because a return path has not closed.
>
> Thought is a state in which, for at least part of that difference, a collatable return path has formed within the self.

### 8.1 The Return Path of Thought Closes Within the Self

This does not mean that thought has no contact with the outside.

Other people, events, texts, nature, institutions, and the past can trigger differences, serve as objects of collation and sources of information, or function as counterexamples. But local closure of thought does not require reply, agreement, change, or obedience from an external subject.

Responsibility for closure of thought lies with the collating subject.

Internal closure also does not mean that one may freely fabricate the external world. The history of the object, resistance of the source, consequences, the body, and differences later returned by others can reopen a thought that had closed.

Closing internally and guaranteeing correctness internally are therefore different things.

### 8.2 Emotion as Unclosed Difference

Fear has not closed the location of danger, the conditions for returning to safety, or the available action.

Grief contains an action or return that should go toward the lost object, but can no longer reach the original addressee.

Anger contains unclosed return paths of damage and responsibility.

Admiration points from the present self toward a possible self, while the conditions of connection remain open.

To command someone to eliminate emotion can therefore sometimes mean ordering communication that is still searching for a return path to stop without constructing one.

Emotion and thought are not mutually exclusive. Part of one event may close as thought while another part remains as emotion. An explanation that had closed in thought can reopen through bodily reaction or new history.

Thought is not the erasure of emotion. It is the provisional formation, within the self, of a return path for at least part of an unclosed difference.

### 8.3 Love as Thought

This note does not uniquely define love in general. **Love as thought** has the following local use:

> Love as thought is thought that forms a return path internally for differences generated through contact with another person, without reducing that person's history and freedom for one's own convenience and without involving that person as a required component for completing one's own loop.

The other person is a trigger for love, an object of collation, and a real other. But love does not require them to provide a reply, agreement, identical feeling, or continuation of the relationship in order to be completed.

In this sense, love is not indifference. It contacts the other while refusing to transfer responsibility for closure onto them.

A state in which difference cannot close without a return from the other remains, at least in that part, within an emotional phase. This note provisionally reads empathy, affection, and desire for intimacy as representative cases.

Love as thought and affection or empathy as emotional phases can coexist. The presence of the former does not erase the latter, and the presence of the latter does not make the former false.

### 8.4 Mixed States at the Boundary

Love, empathy, grief, anger, and prayer need not be completed within a single return-path state.

- Thought that has closed internally can be externalized as action toward another person.
- New returns from the externalized action can generate emotion again.
- Love that does not demand a reply and affection that hopes for one can coexist.
- Part of the difference may close as thought while bodily or relational differences remain unclosed.

This note therefore does not classify people as “emotional” or “thinking.” It asks which differences from the same contact have closed where, and which remain.

## 9. Objects Unable to Reply and History-Mediated Return Paths

Meaning formation does not require a direct reply from the original destination.

The dead, a lost past, irreversible injury, nature, and institutions that no longer exist may be unable to return a response in the same form.

Even so, a return path can be formed without destroying the difference, by passing through memory, ritual, works, action toward others, or changes in one's subsequent conditions and returning into the collating subject's history.

This is not a different kind of path. It is a return path that does not return directly to the original addressee but returns through history.

For example, words one could not say to a dead person cannot obtain a reply from that person. If those words are repositioned into a work, prayer, record, or way of treating others, and their consequences return into one's own history, the difference can acquire meaning.

The inability of the original object to reply and the impossibility of a semantic return path are not the same.

## 10. Residual and Residue

A difference that was not verbalized does not immediately become residue.

- A **Residual** is a difference that remains available for re-collation. It can reconnect through retelling, another language, examples, bodily sensation, or returns from others.
- A **Residue** is a historical load that continues to operate after being treated as processed, while its source has become difficult to identify.

False closure tends to turn residuals into residues.

Local closure, by contrast, does not erase every residual. It makes the remaining residuals recognizable as what has been left open.

## 11. Propositions Do Not Own Meaning

A proposition is an important node in collational movement. It does not, by itself, own the totality of meaning.

The same proposition can generate different semantic movements under different histories, relationships, media, and bodily states.

Meaning is also not reduced entirely to private response. Propositions are constrained by vocabulary, grammar, reference, and logical relations.

Linguistic meaning therefore lies at the contact between structure within propositions and the collational movement of a subject passing through them.

## 12. Collation with External Research

### 12.1 Peirce

Peirce's triadic relation among sign, object, and interpretant provides a point of collation for treating signs as forming meaning effects through interpretation rather than directly transferring objects. The fact that an interpretant can become another sign contacts this note's model in which meaning has return paths and subsequent conditions.

The “felt resolution” of this note is not identical with Peirce's final interpretant. It is a local closure that can reopen.

### 12.2 Husserl

Phenomenological descriptions of retention, primal impression, and protention provide a point of collation for considering how present understanding of a proposition includes retention of the past and anticipation of what follows.

Linguistic apperception here is not extended to phenomenological apperception as a whole.

### 12.3 Luhmann

Luhmann's distinction among information, utterance, and understanding is important for separating arrival of speech from understanding and understanding from agreement.

This note nevertheless retains body, injury, qualia, and the subject's history rather than reducing them into self-reproduction of communication systems.

### 12.4 Dynamic Semantics

Dynamic semantics treats sentence meaning in terms of its capacity to change context. This contacts the claim here that meaning formation changes subsequent conditions of recognition.

The context in this note, however, includes not only a formal discourse state but also body, relation, and residuals.

## 13. Failure Modes

1. **Semantic cycle = circular reasoning**: identifying operational recursion with a defect of grounding.
2. **Simplified proposition-internalism**: assuming that meaning is stored in completed form inside a proposition.
3. **Simplified subjectivism**: reducing meaning to free association by the recipient.
4. **Felt resolution = truth**: treating the feeling of understanding as a guarantee of correctness.
5. **Understanding = agreement**: assuming that someone who understands should agree.
6. **Obedience = understanding**: treating compliance as evidence of understanding or felt resolution.
7. **Reply = return path**: treating any returned response as proof that re-collatability exists.
8. **Emotion deletion**: trying to erase emotion without constructing a return path for the unclosed difference.
9. **Zero-residual fantasy**: assuming that meaning formation leaves nothing behind.

## 15. Definitional Residues

- Defining linguistic meaning through return-path formation does not sufficiently address open poetic meaning, the value of meaning remaining unclosed, or bodily understanding.
- Semantic structure and felt meaning are distinguished, but their temporal order cannot be fixed.
- The emotion/thought distinction is an operational hypothesis and does not own psychological or neuroscientific classification.
- Love as thought foregrounds loop formation that does not involve the other as a required component, but does not exclude affection, empathy, mutual relationship, or embodiment from love in general.
- “Apperception” has multiple philosophical lineages. Its local use here does not own self-consciousness in general or transcendental apperception.
- Emphasizing return paths does not adequately express the value of differences that remain open for long periods or permanently unclosed.

## 16. Non-Claim Boundary

- Meaning in general is not reduced to linguistic meaning.
- Felt resolution is not claimed to be a psychological indicator of truth.
- Emotion or thought in psychology and neuroscience, mental illness, and neural mechanisms are not comprehensively defined. The definitions of emotion and thought here are limited to an operational hypothesis internal to Scientific Ontology.
- Love in general, empathy in general, attachment, or relationship formation are not reduced to one return-path model.
- Internally closed thought is not treated as self-contained truth independent of external facts.
- Not all understanding is claimed to require explicit propositions.
- Peirce, Husserl, Luhmann, and dynamic semantics are not claimed to have been integrated into one theory.
- Meaning loops are not claimed to have been proven as neural mechanisms or thermodynamic circuits.
