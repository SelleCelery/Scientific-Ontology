## 0. Position

Scientific Ontology does not place the Japanese authoritative text and its English commensuration in a one-way translation relation.

Simply replacing Japanese words with English words can deform subjects, responsibility, causality, reservations, unresolved residuals, concept ownership, and non-claim boundaries. Conversely, English commensuration can expose relations held in suspension in Japanese, terminological collisions, or insufficient explanation, and return them to the Japanese authoritative text.

The question owned by this note is:

> When a text passes through a different grammatical frame, how can we collate what the source text segmented, what it backgrounded, and which relations it established; reactivate those relations within another grammatical frame without erasing differences and residuals; and form a return path back to the original authoritative text?

The general governance contract for commensuration is owned by [`Publication_and_Commensuration_Policy.md`](../../90_Repository_Governance/Publication_and_Commensuration_Policy.md). This note specializes that contract to linguistic and grammatical phases.

## 1. Central Thesis

Commensuration is not the transfer of completed meaning into another language. Nor is it a one-way movement toward the target language.

> Commensuration is a collational movement that passes linguistic apperception formed within one grammatical frame through another grammatical frame, preserving the central thesis, concept ownership, logical relations, claim strength, non-claim boundaries, and residuals, while forming a return path that sends differences back to the authoritative source, concept owner, or judgment field.

This movement does not aim at perfect identity.

Preserving the same operation matters more than using the same word.
Making the same causal, reserved, and responsibility relations traceable matters more than preserving the same number of sentences.
Preventing non-commensurable elements from disappearing into fluency matters more than producing natural prose.

These priorities govern the operation.

## 2. Inputs to Commensuration

What enters commensuration is not a character string alone.

At minimum, the following are treated as inputs:

1. the authoritative wording;
2. the document's central thesis;
3. concept owners and imported concepts;
4. grammatical frame and discourse order;
5. claim strength;
6. non-claim boundaries;
7. unresolved residuals and residues;
8. related documents and return paths;
9. private and public lineages of terminology; and
10. the expected reader and language game.

Even with the same character string, a difference in these inputs can change the correct commensuration.

## 3. Cross-Grammatical Collation

This note calls contact between different grammatical frames **cross-grammatical collation**.

Before matching source and target sentences one by one, cross-grammatical collation asks what the source text commits to, and in what order.

- Is the agent explicit or held in suspension?
- Are action and result causally linked, or merely juxtaposed?
- Where in the sentence does judgment close?
- To whom is responsibility assigned?
- How far are givenness, number, tense, aspect, and modality fixed?
- What is preserved not by vocabulary but by silence, order, spacing, headings, or notes?
- What is entrusted to the reader's shared history?

This collation extracts the semantic movement formed by the grammatical frame before lexical correspondence is attempted.

## 4. Preservation Contract for Commensuration

Commensuration must preserve at least the following.

| Preserved item | What must be preserved | Typical damage |
|---|---|---|
| Central thesis | What the document distinguishes and proposes | Smoothing the explanation until the claim is weakened |
| Concept ownership | Which document defines a concept and what is imported | Redefining an imported concept only in the commensurated version |
| Logical relations | Causality, conditions, exceptions, suspension, dependencies | Similar vocabulary with altered relations |
| Claim strength | Status as hypothesis, structural analogy, empirical claim, etc. | Turning analogy into fact, or substantive proposal into mere metaphor |
| Non-claim boundary | What the document explicitly does not claim | Removing it for readability |
| Residual / residue | What remains unresolved, non-commensurable, or backgrounded | Closing it through natural-sounding wording |
| Return path | Where problems discovered through commensuration must return | Treating the commensurated version as an independent canonical source |
| Revision history | Why terms, order, or explanation were changed | Preserving only the result and losing the transformation process |

## 5. Local-Use Transfer Contract

High-density terms cannot always be commensurated through one-to-one dictionary correspondence.

For example, `閉じる` may mean closing a meaning loop, treating a matter as processed, treating responsibility as settled, cutting off a relation, or falsely closing a question.

Each document therefore records, where necessary:

```text
Term:
Existing owner / lineage:
Local use in the source document:
Not identical to:
Source grammatical function:
Target rendering used here:
What the target rendering foregrounds:
What it backgrounds:
Residuals produced by transfer:
Return path if conflict arises:
```

This is not a glossary. It is a contract for transferring what a term was doing within one document into another grammatical frame.

## 6. Grammatical Transformation History

Commensuration does not prohibit transformation.

Word order, sentence length, paragraph order, explanatory sequence, explicit subjects, and auxiliary explanation may be changed. The important requirement is not to conceal the transformation.

This note records major changes as **grammatical transformation history**.

| Transformation | Purpose | What is preserved | Residual produced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supplying a subject | Establish English argument structure | Candidate agent of action or judgment | Suspension among multiple subjects in Japanese |
| Splitting a sentence | Preserve long chains of reservation in readable English | Stages of proposition and qualification | Continuity of pressure within one sentence |
| Reordering paragraphs | Give an English reader required premises earlier | Logical dependency | Retrospective recovery effect of Japanese ordering |
| Compound terminology | Avoid collision with established English terms | Conceptual boundary | Brevity and lexical texture |
| Added note | Preserve claim strength or non-claim boundary | Public boundary | Explanatory load absent from the source wording |

## 7. Stages of Successful Commensuration

Success is not treated as one Yes/No variable.

### 7.1 Surface Correspondence

Major terms and sentence-level information correspond. Operational relations, residuals, and return paths are not yet guaranteed.

### 7.2 Structural Correspondence

The central thesis, logical relations, concept ownership, and claim strength remain traceable.

### 7.3 Operational Correspondence

Readers in the target language can perform distinctions, suspensions, and re-collations close to those available in the source text.

### 7.4 Recursive Correspondence

Differences exposed through commensuration return to the authoritative source and update the recognition conditions of both source and commensurated texts.

Scientific Ontology aims at least for structural correspondence and, where possible, for operational and recursive correspondence.

## 8. Commensuration Residuals

After commensuration, the following may remain:

- lexical texture, rhythm, and silence in the source;
- judgments withheld until the end of a sentence;
- agents entrusted to shared history;
- culturally or institutionally dependent implications;
- multiple conceptual lineages compressed into one word;
- phases difficult to preserve in one sentence in the target language; and
- distinctions newly demanded by the target language.

These are recorded as **grammatical commensuration residuals**.

The existence of residuals is not itself a failure. Treating them as though they did not exist is the failure.

## 9. Reverse Commensuration

After commensurating a Japanese authoritative text into English, the English text is passed back into Japanese.

This is not a grading exercise for the translation.

It checks:

- what English made explicit;
- where Japanese suspension was lost;
- what appears excessive when English logical relations are returned to Japanese; and
- whether new terminological collisions reveal an insufficiency in the Japanese authoritative text.

Reverse commensuration does not aim to reproduce the identical Japanese wording. Its purpose is to create differences through the round trip and use those differences to re-collate both documents.

## 10. False Commensuration

The following states are treated as false commensuration.

1. **Word-for-word identity type**: words correspond, but logical relations have changed.
2. **Naturalization type**: unresolved elements or non-claim boundaries are erased for readability.
3. **Authority-assimilation type**: wording is moved toward established academic terminology until the conceptual boundary of Scientific Ontology disappears.
4. **Claim-strengthening type**: a conceptual hypothesis is expressed as an empirical fact or physical mechanism.
5. **Claim-dilution type**: a substantive proposal is weakened into a metaphor or impression.
6. **Residual-erasure type**: non-commensurable elements are presented as though a direct equivalent existed.
7. **One-way ownership type**: the target language version adjudicates the authoritative source and loses its return path to it.

## 11. Bidirectional Commensuration with Empirical Science

Contact with empirical science is also a commensurative operation.

### From Scientific Ontology to Empirical Science

Internal concepts are segmented into measurable variables, observable behavior, and falsifiable propositions. Depth hypotheses that cannot be transferred remain as residuals.

### From Empirical Science to Scientific Ontology

The observational surface, variables, conditions, and statistical scope of research are checked, and the phase within which the result is valid is limited. What was not measured is not treated as nonexistent.

Both directions require transformation. Empirical science is placed neither as a superior adjudicator nor as an irrelevant outside. Bidirectionality, however, does not mean identical methods, measurement regimes, evidential force, or power. Each direction has different preserved items, losses, falsification conditions, and return-path bandwidths.

## 12. Collation with External Research

### 12.1 Jakobson

The distinction among intralingual, interlingual, and intersemiotic translation provides a point of collation for considering how residuals vary with the target semiotic system.

### 12.2 Derrida

A sign functions partly because it can be repeated in another context, yet such repetition does not guarantee perfect identity with the original context. This provides a point of collation for thinking about both the conditions and residuals of commensuration.

### 12.3 Eco

The distinction between openness of interpretation and unlimited use helps prevent commensuration from becoming unrestricted creative rewriting.

### 12.4 Hermeneutics and Translation Studies

The view that understanding does not begin without presuppositions, and that it develops through movement between part and whole, provides points of connection to reverse commensuration and recursive correspondence.

These sources do not prove Scientific Ontology. They are used to collate what kinds of transformation are permitted and where resistance from the source must be received.

## 13. Failure Modes

- Treating one-word/one-meaning correspondence as commensuration.
- Placing natural English at the top of the success criteria.
- Unilaterally resolving omissions in the source text.
- Changing concept ownership in the commensurated version.
- Discarding residuals as errors rather than recording them.
- Assimilating the target text to an established theory and thereby changing claim strength.
- Turning reverse commensuration into a word-for-word identity test.

## 14. Local Uses of High-Density Terms

| Term | Local use in this document | Not identical to | Definitional residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commensuration | Reconstruction into another language game while preserving differences, residuals, and return paths | Literal translation, perfect equivalence, free adaptation | Who determines the Core to be preserved |
| Grammar | Multilayer constraints across language, discourse, relation, body, and medium | Syntax alone, culture as a whole | Boundaries and interaction among layers |
| Authoritative text | The current priority reference surface | Absolute truth, immutable original | Unresolved issues and revision conditions of the authoritative text itself |
| Residual | Traceable mismatch or unresolved element remaining after commensuration | Mere mistranslation, deletion target | How the residual should be recommensurated |
| Return path | Route by which commensuration differences return to the authoritative text or owner document | Mere back-translation | Conditions under which actual revision occurs |

## 15. Definitional Residues

- Defining commensuration as structural preservation cannot fully reduce poetic resonance, bodily rhythm, or accidental creativity to contractual items.
- Priority of the authoritative source is necessary for governance, but it can suppress important objections returned from the target language game.
- Evaluating operational correspondence requires knowledge of the target reader's history and use context.
- General stopping conditions cannot be fixed for cases in which revision through reverse commensuration threatens the stability of the authoritative text.

## 16. Non-Claim Boundary

- This note does not claim either complete translatability or complete untranslatability.
- It does not replace established translation studies.
- It does not position Japanese as essentially deeper or English as essentially clearer.
- Explicitness produced through English commensuration is not automatically equated with theoretical progress.
- Mutual commensuration between empirical science and Scientific Ontology is not claimed to always succeed.

## 17. Unresolved Questions / Depth Interface

- Who determines the commensuration Core for each document?
- How should operational correspondence be tested?
- Where should non-commensurable residuals be placed: notes, separate documents, or a terminology registry?
- Can grammatical transformation history be made machine-readable?
- How should governance work when the authoritative relation is expanded from one-to-one into one-to-many across languages?
