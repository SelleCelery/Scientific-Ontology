# 0. Aim

In Scientific Ontology, history is not merely a record.

History acts on how the present receives, judges, draws boundaries, communicates, and produces the next commit.

Two things must nevertheless be separated.

First is **history as events that occurred**.

Second is **the logical dependency structure by which the present adopts some history as a condition of its own formation**.

The central proposition of this note is:

> **A commit as an event is irreversible. Yet, under certain conditions, which commits are adopted as present formation conditions and which routes are used as the active lineage can be reconstructed.**

Under this reading, a Lock in Scientific Ontology is not mere fixation.

A Lock can be read as a **restart point** that retains relations established up to a certain point in a form that can later be re-collated and reconstructed.

Conceptually:

```text
history is not erased
    ->
the present dependency structure can change
    ->
a past Lock can become a restart point for rebuilding later relations
    ->
valid differences from a failed branch can be recovered when needed
```

This is not physical time travel.

> **One does not return to the past itself. One reactivates a boundary that was viable in the past while retaining present knowledge and the history that has occurred since.**

This note provisionally calls such an operation **logical intervention**.

# Part I. Separating history from the present

## 1. A commit is irreversible as an event

Let the commits that have occurred through time $t$ be provisionally represented as

$$
H_t=(c_1,c_2,\ldots,c_t).
$$

Here $c_i$ does not mean only a human decision.

Within AMP, any event in which a difference becomes fixed and remains as a history acting on later states may be treated as a candidate commit.

Accordingly, observation, judgment, institutional change, structure formation, boundary formation, physical state transition, biological formation, and adoption within a knowledge system are not being declared identical. They become comparable only on the abstract surface that they **leave a historical difference in later states**.

For commits as events, this note provisionally assumes

$$
H_t \subset H_{t+1}.
$$

Once a commit has occurred, even if it is later judged mistaken, its occurrence cannot be made never to have happened.

Therefore:

$$
HistoryReconstruction \neq EventErasure.
$$

---

## 2. The present is not the whole of history

A present state does not directly refer to all past history with equal force.

Some history functions as a premise of the present. Some has expired. Some remains as reference. Some is retained as a counterexample. Some belongs to a failed divergent lineage.

Let the **currently effective dependency structure** at time $t$ be

$$
A_t = Active(H_t).
$$

$A_t$ need not be merely a subset of history. It is better read as a graph containing support, dependency, transformation, expiration, exception, priority, and boundary relations among histories.

Thus:

$$
H_t \neq A_t.
$$

This is the first separation of the note.

> **History itself and the historical dependencies presently adopted are different objects.**

---

## 3. What can be rewound is active lineage, not history

Suppose a downstream structure is judged mistaken.

A Scientific-Ontological rewind is not

$$
H_t \rightarrow H'_t.
$$

It is instead

$$
(H_t,A_t)
\rightarrow
(H_t,A'_t).
$$

History $H_t$ is retained while the currently effective dependency lineage $A_t$ is reconstructed.

In minimal form:

$$
J:(H_t,A_t)\mapsto(H_t,A'_t),
$$

where $J$ is the logical-intervention operator.

The essential point is:

> **Logical intervention is not alteration of the past. It is reconstruction of the formation conditions of the present.**

# Part II. Reading Lock as a save point

## 4. Lock is not mere fixation

AMP generative theory has read Lock as a stabilization mechanism that allows asymmetry to persist.

This note adds **reconstructability**.

Let the structure established through point $k$ be

$$
L_k.
$$

For a Lock to serve as an effective restart point, at least the following must be traceable:

- what was established at that point;
- what premises it depended upon;
- which support paths were active;
- which residuals remained unresolved;
- which later structures had not yet been adopted;
- under what conditions it should reopen.

A Lock is therefore not merely a snapshot. Conceptually it may be read as

$$
L_k=(A_k,P_k,Q_k,R_k),
$$

where:

- $A_k$: the active dependency structure at the time;
- $P_k$: support / provenance;
- $Q_k$: residuals still present at the time;
- $R_k$: reopening conditions and Return paths.

---

## 5. Three functions of Lock

This note provisionally separates three Lock functions.

### 5.1 Stabilization

Retain a difference or relation in a form that can persist into later states.

### 5.2 Compression

Allow a later state to be formed without re-executing the entire generative process prior to the Lock every time.

This does not mean discarding the past. Provenance and Return are retained while the past is compressed into a form usable by the present.

### 5.3 Restartability

When a later structure fails, use the Lock as a restart point from which another later lineage can be built.

Provisionally:

$$
Lock
=
Stabilization
+
Compression
+
Restartability.
$$

This is not a theorem. It is a candidate definition to be stressed by simulation and external comparison.

---

## 6. Lock is not Seal

If something is fixed in a way that cannot reopen, it is closer to a Seal than to a Lock.

This note explicitly holds

$$
Lock \neq Seal.
$$

A Lock says:

> **Retain the structure established up to here as reusable, and reconstruct from here when necessary.**

A Seal says:

> **Do not re-examine this point and do not return behind it.**

If a Lock loses Return as it becomes more rigid, that may indicate pathological conversion of Lock into Seal.

This distinction can matter in knowledge systems, institutions, software, organizations, and personal judgment histories.

---

## 7. Lock is not a storage point for truth

A locked state is not necessarily correct.

A Lock is a point at which certain formation conditions were met well enough for later structures to be developed.

The premises before the Lock may later fail.

Thus:

$$
Locked(L_k) \not\Rightarrow True(L_k).
$$

A Lock is not a Correctness Certificate, although a Correctness Certificate may provide material from which a useful Lock can be formed.

# Part III. Logical intervention

## 8. What is logical intervention?

Logical intervention acts on currently effective dependency structures without erasing event history.

In minimal form:

$$
J:(H_t,A_t,L_t,Q_t)\mapsto(H_t,A'_t,L'_t,Q'_t).
$$

Here:

- $H_t$: irreversible history;
- $A_t$: active dependency structure;
- $L_t$: available Locks;
- $Q_t$: unresolved residuals and candidate defeaters.

Such an intervention may:

- remove an invalid premise from active lineage;
- reconstruct from an earlier Lock;
- recover only valid differences from a failed branch;
- integrate separate lineages;
- preserve unresolved conflict instead of falsely closing it.

---

## 9. Logical intervention acts on time structure but is not time travel

AMP reads time as a manifestation of a structure in which differences are committed and retained as history.

Rewinding a present configuration that depends on that commit history may therefore be called, within AMP, a **logical intervention into commits produced through time**.

It does not mean:

- erasing a past event;
- reversing a physical state into its previous physical state;
- moving backward along a time coordinate.

A commit as an event remains irreversible.

What logical intervention changes is **how the commit is connected to the present**.

Thus it retains both

$$
IrreversibleCommit
\land
RevisableDependency.
$$

---

## 10. Return is not restoration of the past

The phrase "return to an earlier Lock" can be mistaken for restorationism.

Here:

$$
Return \neq Restoration.
$$

More precisely:

$$
Return
=
Reconstruction\ from\ an\ earlier\ viable\ boundary.
$$

One does not reproduce a past society, institution, theory, or self-state unchanged. One retains the history through the present and uses an earlier viable boundary as a new baseline.

# Part IV. Structural similarity to Git operations

## 11. The Git analogy is highly useful, but remains an analogy

The structure proposed here has strong similarities to version-control systems, particularly Git.

Git must not become the ontological ground of the theory. It is an existing engineering implementation that separates history from present configuration, and is useful for understanding the analogy.

A provisional correspondence is:

| Git vocabulary | Candidate SO correspondence |
|---|---|
| commit | a difference fixed into history |
| branch | a distinct later dependency lineage |
| checkpoint / tag | Lock / restart point |
| rollback / reset analogue | restore an earlier Lock as active baseline |
| rebase | re-evaluate and reconstruct later differences on another baseline |
| cherry-pick | move only valid differences from a failed branch into another lineage |
| merge | integrate multiple lineages into the present structure |
| conflict | incompatible differences or dependencies |
| history rewrite | must be separated from falsification or concealment of event history, which SO rejects in principle |

---

## 12. Rollback: changing the restart point

Let a lineage be

```text
A -- B -- C -- D -- E
```

If a premise introduced at $D$ later produces a major defeater, a new lineage may be built:

```text
A -- B -- C -- F -- G
```

$D$ and $E$ do not disappear. Total history retains them:

```text
A -- B -- C -- D -- E
          \
           F -- G
```

Only the active lineage changes.

---

## 13. Rebase: rebuilding later differences on another baseline

Not every later structure is necessarily invalid.

It may be possible to remove only the problematic premise, then re-evaluate and rebuild later differences.

This is called a rebase-type logical intervention.

Conceptually, if

$$
A_t
=
L_k + \{d_{k+1},d_{k+2},\ldots,d_t\},
$$

then one may shift the baseline to $L_j$ and reconstruct

$$
A'_t
=
L_j + \{d'_{k+1},d'_{k+2},\ldots\}.
$$

Each $d_i$ cannot necessarily be reused unchanged. A change of baseline may change both meaning and dependency, so revalidation is required.

---

## 14. Cherry-pick: recovering differences from a failed branch

Even when an entire branch fails, not all information obtained on that branch is necessarily invalid.

Potentially recoverable material may include:

- valid observations;
- new measurement methods;
- new operators;
- discovered residuals;
- locally viable structures;
- implementations reusable on another branch.

Therefore:

$$
InvalidLineage
\not\Rightarrow
InvalidEveryCommit.
$$

Moving only some revalidated differences from the failed branch into a new active lineage is read here as a cherry-pick-type logical intervention.

---

## 15. Merge and conflict

When multiple lineages have valid histories, there is no need to erase one and absorb everything into the other.

Merge what can be integrated and preserve incompatible differences as conflict.

This is close to the idea of an interference zone in Scientific Ontology.

$$
Merge(A,B)
=
SharedStructure
+
PreservedResidual.
$$

A successful merge does not mean that differences vanish.

> **It means that what is shared, what is not shared, and what conflicts remain unresolved can still be traced.**

# Part V. Reconstruction requires capacity

## 16. A Return path does not by itself make Return executable

A route may be logically recorded while the actual capacity to reconstruct along it is absent.

Thus:

$$
PossibleReturn \neq ExecutableReturn.
$$

Depending on the domain, reconstruction may require computation, time, records, personnel, attention, material slack, institutional authority, energy, or capacity for relearning.

---

## 17. Candidate reading of negentropy as reconstruction capacity

A human-side hypothesis describes logical intervention as supplying negentropy to an entropized state and using thermal or free capacity to rewind a commit history produced through time.

This note does not elevate that phrase directly into a physical quantity.

Let the capacity available for reconstruction first be represented abstractly as

$$
N_t.
$$

If logical intervention $J$ has required cost

$$
C(J),
$$

then at minimum

$$
N_t \ge C(J)
$$

is required.

At this stage, $N_t$ is not identical to thermodynamic negentropy.

It may nevertheless have a contact surface with the more general proposition that **reconstructing order requires degrees of freedom, slack, and low-entropy resources**.

---

## 18. What does it mean to "rewind history using thermal capacity"?

Operationally weakened, the phrase can be read as follows.

Event history $H_t$ is already past. Yet if provenance, failed branches, Locks, residuals, and successful local structures are preserved, new capacity $N_t$ can be used to generate another present structure from them.

$$
FixedHistory
+
NewFreeCapacity
\rightarrow
AlternativeActiveLineage.
$$

In this sense, history may function not as consumed past alone but as an **available compressed resource** during reconstruction.

---

## 19. Capacity is not used to erase history

Even large reconstruction capacity cannot make the past nonexistent.

Greater capacity should instead be read as allowing more past branches to be compared, more distant Locks to be revisited, multiple rebase candidates to be tried, conflicts to be preserved while alternatives are compared, and cherry-pickable differences to be explored.

Negentropy as used here is therefore better read as **capacity for historical recomputation**, not capacity for altering history.

# Part VI. Accounting and symmetry

## 20. Rebase is not free

Changing active lineage also changes support, responsibility, resources, institutional relations, and meaning in later structures.

Merely declaring a return to a past Lock does not make the new lineage viable.

For candidate lineage $A'_t$, one may require both

$$
Viable(A'_t)=1
$$

and

$$
C(A_t\rightarrow A'_t)\le N_t.
$$

---

## 21. Hypothesis of accounting symmetry

A human-side hypothesis states that rebase or cherry-pick is possible when "symmetry of energy accounting" permits it.

Generalized, this means that constraints which cannot legitimately be ignored must either be preserved or explicitly accounted for before and after reconstruction.

Let the set of invariants or obligations requiring preservation or settlement be

$$
\Sigma_t.
$$

A candidate $A'_t$ may then be required to satisfy

$$
PreserveOrAccount(\Sigma_t,A'_t)=1.
$$

$\Sigma_t$ differs by domain: conservation and boundary conditions in physical systems; assets, liabilities, and obligations in accounting; rights, duties, and responsibility in institutions; factual constraints, support, and scope in knowledge systems; API contracts and data integrity in software.

These are not reduced to physical energy.

The central constraint is:

> **Freedom to reconstruct is not a right to erase unsettled burdens.**

---

## 22. Symmetry does not mean returning to the same state

A rebased structure need not equal the past state.

What matters is traceability of what was preserved, what changed, and what new burdens were assumed.

Thus accounting symmetry does not require

$$
A'_t=A_k.
$$

It requires something more like

$$
Accounting(A'_t)
\sim
Accounting(H_t,\Sigma_t),
$$

meaning that the new present remains viable without ignoring the history through the present.

# Part VII. An institutional trial example

## 23. Money as an example

The following is not an evaluation of economic history or monetary policy. It is a trial example for explaining logical intervention.

Suppose an institutional lineage is represented as

```text
L0: function mediating exchange value
 ->
L1: money
 ->
L2: convertible paper money
 ->
L3: fiat money
 ->
L4: later institutions formed on the premise of fiat money
```

Assume that after sufficient audit a later society judges that a premise introduced at $L3$ was mistaken and should no longer remain a current formation condition.

A Scientific-Ontological rewind does not erase the history in which fiat money existed.

History remains

$$
H=\{L0,L1,L2,L3,L4,\ldots\}.
$$

An earlier Lock, perhaps $L2$, can instead be selected as a new baseline and another lineage constructed:

$$
L2\rightarrow L3'\rightarrow L4'\rightarrow\cdots.
$$

---

## 24. Why cherry-pick is needed in this example

Not every institution developed after $L3$ must be invalid.

Payment technologies, accounting practice, legal knowledge, risk management, social learning, and failure records may contain material reusable in the new lineage after revalidation.

That recovery corresponds to a cherry-pick-type operation.

Thus the more accurate phrase is not "return to the old days" but:

> **use an earlier viable boundary as a baseline while retaining learning accumulated through the present to build another lineage.**

---

## 25. What this example does not show

The example does not show that convertible paper money is correct, fiat money is wrong, modern monetary systems should be rolled back, or real economic institutions can be rebased as simply as Git repositories.

Real institutions may have generated vast networks of rights, obligations, expectations, assets, and contracts, making

$$
C(J)
$$

extremely large.

Logical reconstructability and practical reconstructability are therefore distinct.

# Part VIII. Historical computation structure

## 26. Minimal state representation

A minimal candidate state for Historical Computation in Scientific Ontology is

$$
X_t=(H_t,A_t,L_t,Q_t,B_t,N_t,\Sigma_t,\Phi_t).
$$

- $H_t$: irreversible total history;
- $A_t$: currently effective dependency structure;
- $L_t$: available Locks;
- $Q_t$: residuals, defeaters, and conflicts;
- $B_t$: resources available for exploration and audit;
- $N_t$: capacity for executing reconstruction;
- $\Sigma_t$: constraints that must be preserved or accounted for;
- $\Phi_t$: topology map recording the current adjudicative or audit topological position of nodes, claims, findings, and related objects.

This state is not the world itself. It is a representation for auditing and simulating a target as historical computation.

$B_t$ and $N_t$ are deliberately separated.

If $B_t$ is insufficient, the system cannot adequately explore or audit where it is broken. If $N_t$ is insufficient, the system may understand the problem but be unable to rebuild.

Thus:

$$
B_t>0,\;N_t=0
$$

can describe "the problem is visible but cannot be repaired," while

$$
B_t=0,\;N_t>0
$$

can describe "capacity to rebuild exists but the system cannot adequately explore where to return."

$\Phi_t$ should not be treated as assigning one global topological position. A better provisional form is

$$
\Phi_t:V_t\cup Claims_t\cup Findings_t\rightarrow\mathcal{P},
$$

mapping each object to its current topological position.

---

## 27. History is closer to a DAG than to a sequence

Actual history is not a single linear sequence. There are parallel judgments, branches, mergers, and unconnected lineages.

Thus it is natural to model candidate history as a directed acyclic graph

$$
H_t=(V_t,E_t),
$$

where $V_t$ are commit nodes and $E_t$ are dependency, support, formation, or reference edges.

Active lineage $A_t$ is the currently adopted effective substructure of that graph.

---

## 28. Active lineage need not be singular

A present structure can depend on multiple lineages simultaneously.

Strictly, active lineage is therefore better treated not as one branch but as an active subgraph

$$
A_t\subseteq H_t.
$$

This allows the model to represent simultaneous use of multiple theories, overlapping institutions, roles with distinct histories, and multiple sources acting as support.

---

## 29. Lock is a re-entry point into a subgraph

A Lock need not be one node. A stable group of relations may jointly form a Lock.

Thus:

$$
L_k\subseteq H_k,
$$

and it must allow later active subgraphs to be reconstructed from it.

In this sense, Lock is closer to a **recomputable boundary surface** than to a simple tag.

# Part IX. Simulation design

## 30. Historical computation can be reduced to a toy simulation

At each step, a minimal simulation might:

1. generate a new candidate commit;
2. form support / dependency edges;
3. evaluate viability;
4. form a Lock under stated conditions;
5. accumulate residuals / defeaters;
6. generate logical-intervention candidates when a threshold is crossed;
7. search available Locks;
8. try rollback / rebase / cherry-pick / merge candidates;
9. retain candidates satisfying accounting and resource conditions;
10. adopt a new active subgraph without deleting the old lineage.

---

## 31. Selecting logical-intervention candidates

Let the candidate intervention set be

$$
\mathcal{J}_t.
$$

For a candidate $J_i$, one might consider

$$
Score(J_i)
=
f(
ResidualReduction,
Cost,
InvariantPreservation,
Reopenability,
HistoryFidelity
).
$$

Collapsing these dimensions into one score is dangerous. As in audit theory, it is better to retain them initially as multi-axis findings.

---

## 32. The first observation target is reconstruction history, not the "correct answer"

The first purpose of simulation should not be optimization toward one solution.

Instead observe where Locks formed, where Locks became Seals, which defeaters broke branches, how far the model returned, what it cherry-picked, what conflicts remained, and how much reconstruction capacity was consumed.

That is, make

$$
HistoryOfReconstruction
$$

itself an observation target.

---

## 33. Comparative models are necessary

To determine whether AMP-style historical computation adds explanatory power, comparison models are required.

### Model A: Append-only model

Append history without rewinding.

### Model B: Forgetful model

Delete older history with some probability.

### Model C: Full-reset model

Reset all state after failure.

### Model D: Lock-and-reconstruct model

Retain history and reconstruct from Locks.

### Model E: Optimization model

Maximize the present objective independently of history.

### Model F: Single-topology adjudication model

Do not distinguish fact candidates, theories, residuals, objections, and reconstructions by topological position; adjudicate and audit using one present state.

If Model F can reproduce the same residuals, rebuttals, Returns, and root-revision candidates at equal or lower resource cost, the explanatory increment of retaining $\Phi_t$ is weak.

Likewise, if the AMP model cannot be distinguished from a generic search algorithm or checkpoint method, its claimed explanatory increment must be doubted.

# Part X. Connection to audit theory

## 34. Separate adjudication from audit computationally

Historical computation retains **adjudication** and **audit** as distinct operations.

Adjudication handles the local justification of proposition $p$.

$$
Adjudicate(p,\phi_i)
$$

asks how far $p$ can presently be sustained under factual constraints, support, scope, defeaters, and search history.

Audit goes beyond the proposition itself. It asks:

- where the proposition has been placed in a document;
- whether the strength of expression is appropriate;
- whether the topological positions of fact, theory, and residual are still visible;
- whether document structure blocks objection or Return;
- what load that placement places on other branches or Locks.

A commit may therefore pass local adjudication yet still require relocation, restriction of scope, or return to a research-note layer after audit.

---

## 34.1 From low-resolution probe to high-resolution rebase

SO's own theoretical development is also an object of historical computation.

Conceptually:

```text
P0: low-resolution proposal
  -> external contact
P1: external facts, definitions, and objections return
  -> high-resolution reconstruction
P2: increase distinctions and reconstruct within SO
  -> internal path test
P3a: rebut or limit through an existing route
P3b: add a new theory commit
P3c: residual remains after available routes are exhausted
  ->
Return to a more upstream Lock / root
```

High-resolution distinctions returned from outside need not be assimilated into SO as they are.

But SO also must not claim successful commensuration while discarding the distinctions of the source domain.

---

## 34.2 Preserve path exhaustion as history

Let the known internal reconstruction routes for proposition $p$ be

$$
\mathcal{R}_{SO}(p).
$$

For objection $o$, preserve in history which routes were tried, where they succeeded, and where they failed.

If a new route $r_{new}$ is added, treat it as a new commit rather than pretending it was an already available route:

$$
r_{new}\notin\mathcal{R}_{SO}^{old}(p).
$$

If high-resolution constraints still cannot be reconstructed after both existing and newly added routes are made explicit, record a

> **residual after path exhaustion**

and return it to a more upstream Lock or a root-revision candidate.

This may allow the history to distinguish construction of a genuine rebuttal from theoretical immunization.

## 34.3 Observe topological transition itself as a commit

Fact candidates, theories, and residuals are not timeless boxes.

If the topological position of object $x$ changes from

\Phi_t(x)=\phi_i

to

$$
\Phi_{t+1}(x)=\phi_j,

retain the reason for that transition in history.

Examples include:

- hypothesis -> externally constrained hypothesis;
- objection -> unresolved residual;
- residual -> root-revision candidate;
- proposition -> scope-limited proposition.

Topological position labels may change later. The invariant requirement is that **the difference that changed how the object is treated remains traceable**.

## 34.4 An audit finding is a branch-invalidation candidate, not a verdict

Suppose an audit detector reports return failure, hidden externalization, self-application failure, Unsupported Join, false closure, or unresolved residual.

This does not immediately delete the branch.

Maintain:

$$
Finding \neq Verdict.

A finding is first a candidate indication of **where the currently active dependency lineage should be re-collated**.

A local problem may be repaired by changing only a recent commit.

Audit therefore asks not only

> What is wrong?

> **After which Lock must the structure be reconstructed?**

# Part XII. Boundary with historiography

## 40. Historical computation does not replace historical scholarship

"History" in this note does not replace specialist methods for factual description of events, source criticism, causal analysis, social history, economic history, political history, or related disciplines.

Questions such as what actually happened, which materials are reliable, and which causal explanations are warranted remain governed by domain-specific historical methods.

This note adds the structural question:

> **Which past does the present adopt as a condition of its formation, and from where can that dependency be reconstructed?**

## 41. Distinguish historical falsification from logical intervention

Historical falsification changes or conceals event history:

$$
H_t\rightarrow H'_t.
$$

Logical intervention preserves history and changes active dependency:

$$
(H_t,A_t)\rightarrow(H_t,A'_t).
$$

Concealing a past failure is therefore not logical intervention.

If the failed branch becomes invisible, neither cherry-pick nor re-audit is possible.

## 42. Present physical impossibility does not disappear

Even if one can logically return to an earlier Lock, one may be unable to restore the former physical or social conditions.

Lost resources, deceased people, extinct ecosystems, destroyed equipment, already formed rights and obligations, changed population structures, and irreversible environmental changes cannot be restored by logical operations alone.

Thus:

$$
LogicalRollback
\not\Rightarrow
PhysicalRestoration.
$$

Ignoring this difference would turn historical computation into a dangerous restorationism.

# Part XIII. Trial framework for historical inquiry

## 43. Position of this part: read dependency without replacing history

The following sections test how the preceding historical-computation structure might be brought into historical inquiry.

This note does not redefine historiography itself.

Source criticism, dating, institutional history, social history, political history, economic history, religious history, anthropology, and other specialist methods retain primary authority over what actually occurred.

Scientific Ontology adds a different question:

> **Through which carriers did an event, institution, authority, or narrative pass; which functions did it retain; which Locks did it depend upon; which Cuts did it cross; and by what reconstruction has it reached the present?**

"Historical inquiry" here is therefore not a device for deciding whether an event is true. It is an auxiliary coordinate for reading the **dependency structure, reproduction structure, and reconstructability** connecting events to the present.

Frazer, Levi-Strauss, structuralism, memetics, and other lines may provide inspiration, but the concepts of kingship, Lock, logical depth, or possibility-preservation competition are not derived from the authority of those theories.

> **Similarity is not source authority.**

---

## 44. Separate carrier from function in history

When reading a historical structure, separate a person, lineage, document, or institution from the function enacted through that carrier.

Provisionally:

$$
C_t = \text{carrier},
$$

$$
F_t = \text{function},
$$

$$
S_t = \text{structure enabling the function}.
$$

Even if carrier $C_i$ disappears, another carrier $C_j$ may take up the same or a related structure and thereby preserve or transform function $F$:

$$
(C_i,S)\rightarrow F
$$

to

$$
(C_j,S')\rightarrow F'.
$$

$F'=F$ is not required.

Historical succession often involves transformed reimplementation rather than identity.

Therefore:

> **Separate "who carried it" from "what persisted."**

---

## 45. Trial hypothesis: read kingship as authority, not merely as a right

Kingship is used here as a test case, not as an empirically established universal structure across all historical societies.

The hypothesis is:

> **Kingship may sometimes be read not merely as a right owned by a particular person, but as an authority structure that gives actions by a carrier connected to a position a state-transition capacity not ordinarily available.**

Let the king be $p$ and the kingship structure be $K$:

$$
(p,K)
\rightarrow
\{declare,appoint,judge,command,consecrate,represent,\ldots\}.
$$

$p$ and $K$ must not be identified.

Even after the individual king dies, some or all of $K$ may be transferred to another carrier through succession ritual, law, genealogy, myth, religious authorization, bureaucracy, land and taxation relations, military command, and everyday recognition or compliance.

Saying "kingship is a meme" may be suggestive, but this note adopts a narrower reading than simple information replication:

> **a relational structure that generates state transitions and can be reimplemented through multiple carriers.**

Provisionally:

$$
Kingship
\approx
TransmissibleAuthorityStructure.
$$

This is not a historical conclusion that every form of kingship has this essence.

---

## 46. Falsification, rewriting, and expiration: which layer changed?

Historical genealogies, legitimacy narratives, and institutional explanations can be altered or rewritten later.

The key distinction is between

> **a change in description**

and

> **a change in the dependency structure that sustained the described relation**.

Suppose a surface genealogy is changed from

```text
A -> B -> C -> D
```

to

```text
A -> X -> C -> D.
```

If authority $K$ depends on multiple layers such as

$$
K=
\{K_{ritual},K_{legal},K_{kinship},K_{economic},K_{symbolic},K_{bureaucratic}\},
$$

changing one genealogical statement does not automatically reconstruct all of $K$.

Conversely, the surface description may remain old while legal authority disappears, ritual stops, taxation or military administration moves elsewhere, or everyday recognition changes. The deeper dependency structure may then already have changed.

Thus:

$$
NarrativeChange
\neq
DependencyChange.
$$

Finding a falsification also does not guarantee that a "true deep structure" remains intact behind it. If repeated alteration changes multiple layers and removes old Return paths and provenance, the new structure may in fact become the active lineage.

Depth therefore does not guarantee that historical truth will prevail.

---

## 47. Logical depth: not a count, but how far reconstruction must go

The number of repetitions of a historical claim is not the same as how deeply it is embedded.

$$
ReplicationCount(r)
\neq
LogicalDepth(r).
$$

Logical depth is provisionally defined here as:

> **how far upstream one must reconstruct Locks, independent support layers, and dependencies in order to remove relation $r$ from the formation conditions of the present.**

Rather than a single scalar, begin with a vector:

$$
D(r)
=
(d_{lock},d_{layer},d_{support},d_{reconstruction}).
$$

- $d_{lock}$: how many and how old the Locks are that must be revisited;
- $d_{layer}$: how many institutional, ritual, legal, economic, or symbolic layers must change;
- $d_{support}$: the extent of independent support paths;
- $d_{reconstruction}$: the cost of rebuilding the present after removal.

Again:

> **deep does not mean true.**

A mistaken institution can become deeply embedded through long persistence. Depth measures embedding and resistance to reconstruction, not truth value.

Otherwise one creates a new Unsupported Join from "old" or "widely believed" to "correct."

---

## 48. Historical structure is multilayered, not a single surface/depth pair

A simple opposition between surface and depth is insufficient.

Historical structures are better modeled as graphs in which multiple partially independent layers reproduce one another.

For an authority structure, one might have:

```text
myth / cosmology
        <-> ritual
        <-> kinship
        <-> law
        <-> administration
        <-> economy
        <-> everyday recognition
```

One layer may be Cut while others reproduce the structure. Conversely, a name or carrier may remain even after enough layers have vanished that the function is largely gone.

Historical identity should therefore not be decided from a single variable such as

$$
Identity = Name
$$

or

$$
Identity = Bloodline.
$$

A candidate reading is instead:

$$
HistoricalContinuity
=
PatternOfRetainedDependencies.
$$

This still does not replace domain-specific factual judgment.

---

## 49. Absence and Cut: historical inquiry searches for lost connections

Historical computation attends not only to what remains but also to lost connections:

- gaps in genealogy;
- unexplained portions of institutional transition;
- authority that remains after its legitimating basis disappears;
- names retained after function has been Cut;
- rituals retained after their dependency target changes;
- an expected Return path absent from the record.

None should immediately be labeled concealment, falsification, or error.

First treat the difference as a candidate Expected Missing / Cut:

$$
r(v)=Expected(v)-Observed(v).
$$

At the same time, do not invent continuity merely because discontinuity is uncomfortable.

Historical inquiry audits both:

- **Cut**: a required basis or historical connection is missing;
- **Unsupported Join**: continuity is asserted without sufficient support.

The goal is not

$$
\min Cut.
$$

It is:

> **retain required connections, avoid adding unsupported ones, and record what has been lost as lost.**

---

## 50. "Possibility-preservation competition" as a candidate principle of competition

Historical computation does not require competition to mean that individual A defeats individual B.

More generally, multiple generative lineages may be compared by how much viable later possibility they preserve under finite resources, carriers, and environments.

This note provisionally calls that **possibility-preservation competition**.

Let the set of viable later lineages reachable from current structure $S$ be

$$
\mathcal{P}(S).
$$

A larger

$$
|\mathcal{P}(S)|
$$

is not automatically better. Falsehoods, Unsupported Joins, externalized damage, and infeasible branches could inflate apparent possibility count.

At minimum, separate:

- retention of necessary historical connection;
- avoidance of false join;
- availability of Return / Reopening;
- avoidance of false closure over unresolved differences;
- avoidance of unnecessary destruction of later possibilities;
- whether persistence costs are hidden in others, an external environment, or future states.

Do not collapse these into a single competition score.

> **Competition may be read not as destruction between beings, but as a local selection process among generative lineages over which differences and possibilities can be carried forward.**

That is the research hypothesis proposed here.

---

## 51. Competition among individuals is not assumed as a foundational principle

In Scientific Ontology, an individual is not merely a resource-acquiring unit. An individual can also be an exploration geometry with distinct initial state, history, search operators, and resource allocation.

For individuals $a,b$:

$$
Reach(a) \neq Reach(b)
$$

may hold.

Through collaboration or interference it may also happen that

$$
Reach(a\cup b)>Reach(a)
$$

and

$$
Reach(a\cup b)>Reach(b).
$$

Eliminating one through individual competition could then reduce the exploration space itself.

Individual competition can still function in allocation of scarce resources, comparison of hypotheses, parallel trials of strategy, or local selection pressure. It need not be elevated automatically into a foundational principle of existence.

Historical inquiry should first ask:

> **What has been cut out as the unit of competition?**

That unit may be an individual, lineage, dynasty, institution, ritual, organization, language, technology, knowledge system, or symbiotic structure. The cut itself is an observation boundary.

---

## 52. Provisional protocol for historical inquiry

When applying this framework to historical inquiry, the following order is a candidate procedure.

### 52.1 Fix the factual layer first

From specialist research, separate what is observed or recorded, which sources exist, what is disputed, and what remains unconfirmed.

Do not rewrite the factual layer through SO structural hypotheses.

### 52.2 Separate carrier from function

Distinguish who or what carried a structure from what was being enacted.

### 52.3 Place a provisional active lineage

Propose which histories the institution, authority, or meaning at the target time depends on.

### 52.4 Search for Lock candidates

Look for points repeatedly presupposed by later structures, but do not declare a Lock merely because it appears important in hindsight.

### 52.5 Build a multilayer dependency graph

Separate law, institution, ritual, economy, kinship, symbol, technology, and everyday practice to the extent necessary.

### 52.6 Search for Cut / Unsupported Join

Audit both missing connection and excessive connection.

### 52.7 Provisionally evaluate logical depth

Ask how far reconstruction must go to remove the relation. Do not convert depth into truth value.

### 52.8 Preserve alternative lineages

Do not close early onto one historical lineage. If multiple interpretations fit the evidence, preserve the differences among them.

### 52.9 Examine preserved possibility and cost

Ask what a structure makes possible, what it Cuts, and to whom it externalizes which burdens.

### 52.10 Return to specialist research

Translate questions generated by SO into questions that can be tested through sources, institutional research, or comparative research.

Do not close the inquiry inside an elegant SO structure.

## 53. What this framework cannot decide

The framework cannot by itself decide:

1. which historical source is factually correct;
2. the "true essence" of kingship or an institution;
3. whether deeper structures are truer;
4. whether longer persistence is more desirable;
5. whether preserving more possibilities is always ethically good;
6. where the proper unit of competition lies;
7. whether a pre-falsification structure can always be restored;
8. whether a historical lineage was necessary.

Historical inquiry here is therefore not a device that outputs "the correct history." It is a device for re-collating **which histories, structures, Cuts, Locks, and possibility selections a present historical understanding depends upon**.

When this framework conflicts with specialist historical research, factual description defers to the specialist research and the SO structural hypothesis reopens.

Metaphysical interpretations that specialist research cannot decide should remain undecided rather than being falsely closed.

# Part XIV. From historical computation to sustainability audit: SDGs as an external reference surface

## 54. Position of this part: do not make the 17 SDGs SO's own adjudication target

This note does not begin by adopting the SDGs as a simply correct goal system.

It also does not make the essence, mutual independence, redundancy, orthogonality, or policy effect of the 17 goals a direct adjudication task of SO in this part.

Those questions return to policy research, economics, environmental science, public health, education, law, and other domain-specific methods.

SO asks a narrower and more upstream set of questions:

> **How much search, audit, and reconstruction resource does a currently locked structure consume in order to remain viable?**

> **Does the Lock preserve later structures, or does maintaining it destroy surrounding structures and reconstruction capacity?**

> **When rewinding becomes necessary, where can the required search resource $B_t$ and reconstruction capacity $N_t$ be procured?**

The order is therefore:

```text
historical computation structure
  ->
Lock Health / pseudo-lock audit
  ->
accounting of search resource B and reconstruction capacity N
  ->
reconstructability / branch loss
  ->
interference with external sustainability goals, SDGs, and related frameworks
```

The SDGs are treated primarily as an **external reference surface / interference surface** already formed in the world, not as an object whose 17 axes SO must redesign.

SO may record where its findings contact existing sustainability goals and where they do not. Lack of contact is not automatically evidence that the SDGs are defective or that SO is superior.

---

## 55. Do not place civilization and natural environment as fixed inside and outside

Let human civilization be $C$ and natural environment be $E$.

A simple model may place environment as civilization's external resource:

```text
civilization C
  -> resource use / emissions
natural environment E
```

Scientific Ontology does not fix this separation.

Civilizational active lineage depends deeply on energy, water, food, land, climate conditions, ecosystems, minerals, microbial environments, and material cycles.

At the same time, the environmental active lineage changes under civilizational action.

Use

$$
C \leftrightarrow E
$$

as the basic form, treating both as a **coupled system in which each has its own active lineage**.

The coupled state may be represented as

$$
X_t^{CE}
=
(H_t^{CE},A_t^{CE},L_t^{CE},Q_t^{CE},B_t^{CE},N_t^{CE},\Sigma_t^{CE},\Phi_t^{CE}).
$$

$C$ and $E$ are not dissolved into one undifferentiated system. They retain different histories, boundaries, response speeds, and reconstructive capacities while changing one another's later conditions.

---

## 56. Hypothesis of environmental homeostasis

A human-side research hypothesis proposes that natural environments have stabilization mechanisms capable of retaining or reorganizing relations, functions, and cycles under disturbance.

"Homeostasis" here does not mean a force that must restore the original state.

An environmental system may absorb load, distribute it through other routes, exchange components, change response rates, move to a new equilibrium or metastable state, or preserve function through a different structure.

Provisionally, environmental homeostasis is read as:

> **the capacity, under disturbance, to preserve some relational invariant or functional viability range and, where necessary, reorganize into another viable state.**

This is not a claim to own the definition of ecology. Correspondence with resilience, alternative stable states, regime shifts, and related concepts requires external collation.

---

## 57. Strong symmetry requirement: persistent asymmetry requires a higher-order Lock

AMP generative theory has used the research hypothesis

$$
Persistent(\Delta)
\Rightarrow
Lock(\Delta).
$$

Extended to the civilization-environment coupled system, this means:

> **long persistence of a locally asymmetric stability on the civilizational side does not show that the structure is locked at the level of the coupled system.**

Civilization may maintain local stability by extracting resources in one direction, sending waste, heat, pollution, or wear outward, depending on environmental absorption and recovery, and failing to return environmental responses to its own decisions.

Such a structure may look stable inside civilization while unresolved residuals accumulate in the coupled system.

Thus:

$$
Persist(C)
\not\Rightarrow
Lock(C,E).
$$

The symmetry required here does not mean that $C$ and $E$ become identical states.

It is a stronger invariance requirement:

> **if an asymmetric flow persists for a long time, a higher-order relation including its burden, recovery, reorganization, and Return must itself be viable as a Lock.**

---

## 58. Local Lock / pseudo-lock

A structure stable from the perspective of civilization while externalizing unsustainability to the environmental side is provisionally called a **local Lock / pseudo-lock**.

It may be true that

$$
LocalLock(C)=1
$$

while

$$
Lock(C,E)=0.
$$

Candidate conditions for a pseudo-lock include:

1. high repeatability within civilization;
2. high short- to medium-term predictability within civilization;
3. dependence of that stability on environmental absorption, recovery, or reorganization;
4. exclusion of part of the burden from present civilizational accounting;
5. delayed return of environmental state change;
6. high LogicalDepth and reconstruction cost by the time the return becomes visible.

Under such a structure, distinguish

> **it has not broken yet**

from

> **a sustainable Lock exists**.

Therefore:

$$
SurvivalSoFar
\not\Rightarrow
SustainableLock.
$$

---

## 59. Environmental reorganization is not "punishment of humanity"

If environmental states change in ways unfavorable to human civilization, there is no need to read the result as nature punishing humanity.

Environment $E$ has no obligation to preserve a prior state for civilization $C$.

If $E$ moves to another stable or reorganized state, environmental conditions presupposed by

$$
A_t^C
$$

may disappear.

Thus an environmental transition

$$
E_t\rightarrow E_{t+1}
$$

can produce

$$
Viable(A_t^C)\rightarrow 0.
$$

Civilizational failure is then read not as nature rejecting humanity, but as **environmental conditions required by the civilizational active lineage no longer being preserved by the environmental active lineage**.

---

## 60. Look for the reconstruction-loss point, not only collapse

Historical computation does not need to wait for complete civilizational collapse before recognizing unsustainability.

Let reconstruction cost of logical intervention $J$ be

$$
C(J)
$$

and available coupled reconstruction capacity be

$$
N_t^{CE}.
$$

If

$$
C(J)>N_t^{CE},
$$

then an alternative branch may be logically describable yet no longer executable.

Future analysis should therefore ask not only when civilization collapses, but **when it loses executable capacity to rewind**.

This is provisionally called a **reconstruction-loss point**.

It may precede physical collapse, institutional collapse, or demographic collapse.

---

## 61. Natural environment is also a procurement surface for reconstruction capacity

Civilizational reconstruction capacity $N_t$ must be decomposed across multiple domains: energy, resources, ecological slack, food and water, stable climate zones, land, infrastructure, technology, personnel, knowledge, institutional capacity, and social trust.

The natural environment supplies part of that capacity.

Environmental degradation therefore does not merely mean that "natural value declines." It may **reduce the future $N_t$ needed to reconstruct a failed civilizational branch**.

Conceptually:

$$
Degrade(E)_t
\Rightarrow
N_C(t+n)\downarrow.
$$

This suggests a delayed effect and the hypothesis that large rebase operations are possible only while sufficient capacity remains.

If civilization uses up reconstruction capacity simply to maintain the current lineage, it may be unable to return even after the errors of the old branch become obvious.

---

## 62. Lock Health: separate long duration from a good Lock

Do not judge Lock health from persistence time alone.

For Lock $L$, separately examine at least:

- search and audit resources required for maintenance;
- material and institutional resources required for maintenance;
- residuals generated while maintaining it;
- effectiveness of Return / Reopening;
- reconstructability of later branches;
- destruction or externalization imposed on surrounding structures;
- net effect on $B_t$ and $N_t$.

Thus:

$$
Persistence(L)
\neq
HealthyLock(L).
$$

A long-persisting Lock may consume ever greater search resources, close surrounding Return paths, and reduce reconstruction capacity. Persistence itself is not evidence of health.

Do not immediately collapse Lock Health into a scalar score.

## 63. Detect search-resource sinks

One object relatively directly addressable by SO sustainability audit is a structure that **absorbs excessive search resources**.

A region $R$ is a candidate **search-resource sink** if it repeatedly demands the same exception processing, requires continuous monitoring to remain stable, consumes large resources merely tracing support, accumulates auxiliary hypotheses and local repairs, fails to reduce residuals in proportion to search resources invested, and continually takes resources away from other frontiers.

Let search-resource drain be

$$
Drain_B(R,t).
$$

Large resource use is not itself bad. Difficult problems may legitimately require it.

The question is:

> **Does investment localize residuals, improve Return, and increase reconstructability?**

If increasing investment only creates more local repairs while preventing return to upstream Cuts, audit the Lock itself.

## 63.1 Audit procurement of rewind resources as well

Even when rollback or rebase is necessary, $N_t$ is not automatically available.

If reconstruction capacity is procured by shifting burdens to natural environments, other regions, other groups, future generations, auditors, or another institutional domain, the operation may merely create a new pseudo-lock.

Therefore audit

$$
Acquire(N_t)
$$

itself.

> **The legitimacy of reconstruction cannot be separated from where the resources for reconstruction came from.**

not automatically converted into inclusion or superiority.

## 64. Future prediction becomes branch audit rather than point prediction

Future prediction in this framework is not restricted to statements such as "this will happen in 2050."

Scientific-Ontological future analysis therefore approaches:

> **auditing which futures close under which conditions, rather than merely predicting which future will occur.**

# Part XV. Return to time theory

## 68. Do not posit physical time and logical time as two separate clocks

AMP reads physical time as one stable rendering of intrinsic time generated from commit history.

This note therefore does not oppose "physical time" and "logical time" as two independent clocks.

It instead asks how different structures compress and render the same commit history in different forms.

---

## 69. Logical intervention is not recurrence of a commit

A past commit does not occur again.

Its meaning and dependency are re-evaluated from the present state of knowledge.

Thus:

$$
Replay \neq Re-occurrence.
$$

Replay is recomputation using retained history as material.

---

## 70. Contact with inflation

AMP generative theory considers the possibility that a large number of logical commits may be compressed inside the short physical-time representation of inflation.

From the perspective of this note, if stable physical laws or relations formed as Locks, the later universe might be read as generating subsequent states by using locked relations rather than re-executing their entire generative history each time.

This is not an established physical description.

It remains an internal generative interpretation candidate within AMP.

---

# Part XVI. Falsification candidates

## 71. If history cannot be separated from active dependency

This note is built on

$$
H_t \neq A_t.
$$

For some targets, past events may be inseparably embedded in the present state so that active dependency cannot be independently reconstructed.

In such domains, the applicability of this framework narrows.

---

## 72. If Lock cannot be independently detected

If "Lock" merely means "a point from which rollback later succeeded," the concept is circular.

Lock candidates must be at least partly detectable independently of rollback outcome through dimensions such as stability, provenance, restartability, compression, and invalidation conditions.

If that cannot be done, the explanatory value of Lock is weak.

---

## 73. If reconstruction capacity cannot be measured

If $N_t$ becomes a vague reserve into which anything can be placed, every failed rewind can be explained away by saying "capacity was insufficient."

Each domain must therefore reduce $N_t$ to independently observable quantities or explicitly declared proxies.

---

## 74. If accounting symmetry is defined after the fact

If $\Sigma_t$ is defined after seeing a successful reconstruction so that only successful cases satisfy it, the account becomes circular.

Constraints that must be preserved or settled should be specified as far as possible before the reconstruction result is known.

---

## 75. If the Git analogy distorts the target

In Git, commit objects are relatively clear and branch operations are formal.

In the world, commit boundaries may be ambiguous, actors may be multiple, processes may run concurrently, causality may be cyclic, records may be missing, and states may be irreversible.

If the Git analogy encourages the false belief that the world is a perfectly stored DAG, the analogy must be weakened.

---

## 76. If rebase creates a new false history

If reconstruction cleans up active lineage by hiding failed branches or conflicts, rebase approaches historical falsification.

After rebase, require

$$
Trace(A'_t,H_t)=1.
$$

The new present must still allow return to the old lineages and their failures.

---

## 76.1 If a single-topology model is sufficient

This note adds $\Phi_t$.

If Model F, which has no topological separation, can reproduce across the target set the same adjudication results, audit findings, residuals, path exhaustion, root-revision candidates, and Return / Reopen outcomes with equal or less search resource, the topology map is unnecessary.

If this result appears systematically across core SO targets, the finding should return not only to the historical-computation topological structure but to SO's upstream assignment of theoretical importance to topological differences.

---

# Part XVII. Open questions

## 77. What is a commit?

If commit is defined too broadly, every change becomes a commit and the concept becomes empty.

A boundary condition is needed between transient fluctuation and a difference sufficiently fixed to remain as history.

---

## 78. Is one Lock concept sufficient?

A stabilization Lock, compression Lock, and restart Lock may not be the same structure.

Future work may need to separate:

- Stability Lock;
- Compression Lock;
- Recovery Lock.

---

## 79. Boundary between logical and physical intervention

Rewinding active dependency is relatively intelligible in institutions, software, and knowledge systems.

In the physical world, changing dependencies itself requires a new physical process.

How far AMP can treat the two under the same generative structure remains unresolved.

---

## 80. Does negentropy remain only an analogy?

It remains unestablished whether reconstruction capacity is formally commensurable with negentropy in nonequilibrium thermodynamics.

At present, the note claims no more than the structural similarity that reconstructing order requires degrees of freedom and resources.

---

## 81. Which baseline should be selected?

The newest Lock is not necessarily best. The oldest is not necessarily safest.

Baseline selection must consider the origin of the problem, reconstruction cost, support quality, current constraints, residuals, and irreversibility.

---

## 82. When multiple Locks compete

If distinct valid lineages can be built from different Locks, there may be no unique correct answer.

In some cases the proper design may be to preserve multiple branches rather than force one closure.

---

## 83. How does individuality enter branch selection?

The correctness-topology line treats individuality as exploration geometry.

Historical computation may likewise vary by individuality in which Lock is revisited, what is cherry-picked, and which conflicts are preserved.

This relation is not yet formalized.

---

## 83.1 Can search resource and reconstruction capacity really be separated?

$B_t$ and $N_t$ are conceptually separated here, but the same real resources may be used for both.

Personnel, time, computation, money, and social trust can support both exploration and reconstruction.

They should therefore not simply be treated as independent scalars; resource allocation may require a richer model.

---

## 83.2 SO's own Persistence Bias

SO strongly favors preservation of history, Lock, Return, Reopening, and branch preservation.

That preference also shapes this document.

Yet preserving every branch consumes $B_t$ and $N_t$.

Do not elevate

$$
PreserveEverything
$$

to correctness.

Termination, forgetting, compression, and pruning can sometimes protect a larger exploration space.

# Part XIX. Final compression

## 89. History

> **Do not erase the history of commits that occurred. Branches later judged mistaken remain as failure history.**

## 90. Present

> **The present is not the whole of history; it is the active subgraph describing which histories the current state depends upon.**

## 91. Lock

> **A restart boundary that sustains differences and relations, compresses generative history, and can support reconstruction of later structures when necessary.**

## 92. Logical intervention

> **An operation that rewinds the dependency lineage adopted by the present without erasing the past.**

## 93. Return

> **Not restoration of the past, but construction of another lineage using an earlier viable boundary as baseline while retaining the history through the present.**

## 94. Negentropy / reconstruction capacity

> **Degrees of freedom, resources, and slack that make reconstruction executable. No identity with a thermodynamic physical quantity is claimed at present.**

## 95. Git

> **Engineering implementations that manipulate branch, rebase, cherry-pick, and merge without deleting commits provide a strong analogy for this note, but not its theoretical ground.**

### Historical inquiry

> **Read history not only as an event sequence but as a graph of carriers, functions, multilayer dependencies, Locks, Cuts, and alternative lineages, while returning factual judgment to specialist research.**

### Logical depth

> **How far upstream Locks, independent support layers, and dependency structures must be reconstructed in order to remove a relation from present formation conditions. Depth is not truth value.**

### Possibility-preservation competition

> **Read competition not only as destruction between individuals, but as a selection process over which generative lineages can carry later possibilities forward while retaining necessary connections, avoiding false joins, and preserving Return.**

### Civilization-environment coupled system

> **Do not fix civilization and natural environment as inside and outside; treat them as a coupled system with mutually acting active lineages.**

### Pseudo-lock

> **Even a long-stable civilizational structure is not a Lock of the coupled system if it externalizes unsustainability to the environmental side.**

### Reconstruction-loss point

> **Before complete collapse, there may be a point at which reconstruction cost exceeds remaining capacity and movement to another branch becomes infeasible.**

### Sustainability audit and SDGs

> **SO does not directly adjudicate the essence, orthogonality, or policy effect of the 17 SDG axes. It audits Lock Health, search-resource sinks, reconstruction capacity, Return, and externalization, and returns those findings to interference with the SDGs and other external frameworks.**

### Adjudication and audit

> **Adjudication addresses local justification of a proposition; audit addresses whether the proposition, expression, structure, and placement are appropriate within the document or system.**

### Topology

> **Do not collapse low-resolution proposal, external return, high-resolution reconstruction, rebuttal, residual, and root-revision candidate into one state. Preserve which difference caused a topological transition.**

### Search resource and reconstruction capacity

> **$B_t$ is capacity to find the problem; $N_t$ is capacity to rebuild after finding it. A Lock should be audited for whether it consumes both.**

X_t=(H_t,A_t,L_t,Q_t,B_t,N_t,\Sigma_t,\Phi_t),
