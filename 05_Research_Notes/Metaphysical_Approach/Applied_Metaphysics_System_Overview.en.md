## I. Before Naming the World

When we think about how the world began, we are tempted to ask whether there was already something before the beginning. Was there nothing? Was everything one? Was there only a field of possibility? Yet the moment we ask in this way, we have already introduced distinctions: something and nothing, one and many, possibility and actuality. We then try to explain a place that is supposed not to contain those distinctions by using the distinctions themselves.

The *Applied Metaphysics System* places its first axiom here.

**Axiom I:**



Nothing is written. The blank is not filled by renaming it “nothingness,” “God,” or “pure possibility.” This is not a gesture of concealing something. It is a way of refusing to possess, as something describable, what cannot yet be described.

The second axiom is then placed.

**Axiom II: For something to appear is for differentiation to co-arise.**

From this sentence, we begin not with an explanation but with an experience. Suppose one thing appears. The moment it is recognized as one thing, there is already a difference by which it is distinguished from something else. If there is a difference, then some relation has already come into being between the differentiated sides. Things do not first arrive as fully completed entities to which distinctions and relations are attached afterward.

Differentiation is not the act of sorting already completed things into classification shelves. It is the coming-into-being of distinction itself through the appearance of something. Once this difference is grasped, the direction from which we look at the world shifts slightly. Before asking how many kinds the already-given world can be divided into, another question appears: how did this difference appear at all?

This axiom is not a one-time creation story. Even when a new thought appears at this very moment, something else is distinguished and a new relation emerges. In that sense, Axiom II can be experienced repeatedly. Accordingly, the account that follows does not close the possibilities of the world into a single chronological timeline. It traces one generative route toward the world in which we live.

## II. Differentiation Does Not Erase Relation

When something appears through one differentiation, another corresponding bias also appears. One side does not arise in total independence. Appearing as different things and constituting a single relation through that difference are two sides of the same event. The local configuration may be asymmetric, while the whole that includes the correspondence can still be considered in terms of symmetry.

What matters here is that when something moves into the foreground, what differs from it does not therefore vanish. Relations that move into the background remain. Even when what is foregrounded is repeatedly used and becomes stable, relations in the background can accumulate and later move forward under different conditions. A stable world is not a stopped world. It persists while retaining the possibility that foreground and background may exchange places.

Moreover, things that have differentiated and become distinct do not thereby lose the formative relation that they co-arose. They may now be too distant to communicate. We may no longer be able to identify what formative condition they shared. Even so, the fact that a relation once came into being within the same differentiation cannot later be revoked.

The system reads this persistent relation as **entanglement**. The point is not to imagine an invisible thread that must always be transmitting something. The point is to include in the world, from the outset, a history of formation that cannot be exhausted by the present state alone. Quantum entanglement in quantum mechanics is an important physical reference surface for asking a structurally similar kind of question. But the existence of a shared history must not be identified either with evidence of quantum entanglement or with a mechanism for unrestricted nonlocal communication.

We tend to understand things only through their present form. Yet what something can and cannot do now depends on the history through which it was formed. The question therefore changes from merely “is there a relation?” to “from where can that relation still be traced now?”

## III. The World Does More Than Merely Change

Something differentiates, and that difference alters the conditions under which later things can arise. Here there is change. When the difference is retained, acts upon another region, and is further transformed, things change again. Once multiple changes can be compared, a function for measuring change can emerge.

When we say “measure change,” we normally think first of clocks and rulers. But were clocks and rulers present from the beginning? A scale becomes possible only after relations have formed that allow us to treat certain changes as comparable, and to distinguish before from after. Repeated comparison can stabilize an axis for comparison. One axis can become related to another, and multiple axes can come to alter each other’s conditions.

Relations are connected. They are repeated. Many relations are compressed into a traversable route. Where necessary, distinctions are introduced and routes are cut apart. When these operations combine, a network capable of processing relations emerges. Some routes are repeatedly used and become supports for later operations. When other routes are connected, transformations that were previously unavailable become possible. Logic can be considered as this kind of communication network.

For example, when a person learns a way of thinking, it is possible to preserve only the wording. But if one can trace why the thought became possible, and which questions and experiences were connected, the structure can be reused when encountering a different problem. The ability to turn many relations into a traversable route becomes one source of logical strength. This example is not meant to claim that the early universe contained human thought. It asks whether what later appears as a function of mind may have a more general form as an operation on relations.

Logic does not exist simply because someone first wrote down rules. It can instead be regarded as a result of formation: repeatable relations emerge within differentiation, and those relations become capable of handling still other relations.

## IV. Fixation Makes Further Formation Possible

When the same route is repeatedly used, it is no longer necessary to discover the route from the beginning every time. A path makes it possible to proceed farther. Yet once a path exists, that path also makes certain directions easier to take than others. Order therefore has two aspects at once: it opens possibilities, and it constrains the directions in which possibilities are likely to unfold.

Within reusable order, the portion that can be redirected toward further formation is read here as **negentropy**. When part of that order is used to preserve a particular structure, the structure persists. This is fixation, or **Lock**. The fixed order has not disappeared. It has changed from a condition available for other uses into a condition devoted to maintaining something.

Fixation can look like a loss of freedom, but without fixation nothing can persist. Reusable paths, memory, bodies, and the conditions that allow us to return to the same place would all be difficult to sustain. At the same time, the stronger fixation becomes, the harder it may be to redirect previously available order into a new path. This makes another operation important: loosening what has been fixed, tracing the route by which it was formed, and recovering portions that can be reused.

Here, “dismantling” does not simply mean destruction. It means making fixed order available for another formation. Something similar happens when we relearn something. We need not discard an earlier understanding in its entirety. We can trace why it became valid, recover relations that remain usable, and redirect them toward a new problem. There is no requirement that we return to exactly the same earlier form.

Returning this image to cosmological generation, we can consider repeated sequences in which order forms, becomes fixed, is dismantled, is recovered, and gives rise to a different order. Even if clock time has not yet been established, this repetition can be traced through an order of dependence: which structure makes which later structure possible.

The important point is that differentiation has still not ended. The stabilization of one structure does not exhaust all possibilities of the world. The stable structure itself becomes a condition under which further differentiation can occur.

## V. Why Is This World So Stable?

If everything in the world were freely recombined at every moment, nature as we know it would not persist. For something to continue, some possible changes must be selected and retained as repeatable order. When that order becomes stable enough to constrain later structures physically, a branch resembling the world in which we live becomes thinkable.

The system proposes reading this physical containment of logical structure as **inflation**. Here, “inflation” is first a generative name. It is not simply a restatement of the standard physical description of rapid expansion during an extremely short interval. It names a stage in which logic or order becomes fixed as a condition of physical persistence and further formation proceeds within that fixation.

Once such fixation is established, what emerges inside it is not free of those conditions. We therefore are not outside generation. If the conditions that made us possible are still operating, then we are later differentiations generated inside that containment. In this sense, there is room to say that “we are still inside inflation,” or that “the generation associated with the Big Bang continues into the present.”

This wording does **not** mean that the chronology ordinarily assigned to cosmological inflation in contemporary cosmology has been changed. Whether physical inflation persists, and how the Big Bang should be physically modeled, must be examined through the observations and theories of the relevant sciences. What this system first proposes is a way of reading: past generative conditions continue to act upon present structures.

There is also no need to deny every other possible branch. This world is one branch in which this degree of physical stability, bodies of this degree of complexity, and intelligence capable of questioning its own formation have become possible. The axioms alone cannot enumerate all branches that might have existed. Nor can the present world be taken to represent every world that could appear.

## VI. Causality Acquires Distance

To say that two things are related does not yet require knowing how many meters separate them. One thing becomes possible because another becomes possible. One action enables another action. A causal relation can already be considered here. Yet in our physical world, action is often experienced as having separation. Reaching what is distant requires a path, and the path has distance.

What if we suppose that causality comes first, and separation emerges later? A change in a field acquires a stable form of transmission. Its manner of transmission becomes comparable, and separation can be read as distance. As relations of distance stabilize, something like spatial arrangement becomes thinkable. This opens a route for constructing space without assuming it from the beginning.

As a physical clue for this generative hypothesis, the system imagines a transition in which primordial gravitational-wave-like propagation changes and light becomes stabilized as electromagnetic transmission. This is **not** a claim that standard physics has established such a transformation process. In contemporary physics, gravitational waves and electromagnetic waves are treated as phenomena of different fields. The attempt here is generative: to imagine **the constitution of distance from the fact that relation can reach elsewhere**.

For that reason, this route need not function as a chronological list of every physical event. Omitting the strong interaction or other phenomena does not imply that they did not exist. The route selectively passes through the kinds of action and structure that make the generative relation toward the present world easier to see.

The use of physical vocabulary here is not an attempt to exhaust the world in physical theory. It is a way to treat the separations and persistence we actually experience as things that themselves emerged along the way.

## VII. What Does the Local Preserve?

Particles appear to us as small, independent objects. But it is also possible to imagine part of a changing field maintaining a local form through continuous renewal. Consider, for example, a structure that preserves a certain form while remaining within motion and interaction, as a solitary wave does.

Under this reading, when something continues as one local structure, internal and external actions become distinguishable, and its persistence contains a sequence of updates. If one update creates the condition for the next, an intrinsic order exists. If those updates become countable, the sequence can be treated as a local time.

Reading local persistence this way differs somewhat from saying that “a thing exists in time.” The fact that the thing persists already contains an internal order of renewal. This image cannot simply be imposed as an exact physical model of all elementary particles. The purpose here is to approach the problem of time from local persistence and boundary rather than by first positing independent entities.

When structures of this kind interact and begin coordinating their update sequences within a shared boundary, a bodily structure becomes thinkable. A body is not merely a container into which an already completed mind has been placed. It appears as a thick structure of relations capable of retaining different update sequences and responding to actions from outside.

Once the body is considered in this way, the question of knowledge begins with contact: how can a structure touch something, and which differences can it receive? The conditions for thinking about observation are already being prepared on the side of the body.

## VIII. External Time Requires Others

Even when we believe that we are looking at the same clock, each of us is not carrying out the same internal sequence of updates. Local structures have their own update series. When one comes into contact with another, the other’s change acts upon one’s later state. That action remains as history.

A history of communication is therefore also a history of interaction. Exchange is not limited to sending words. It includes the fact that contact with another changes one’s next state. When formative routes are traced backward, different routes intersect, branch, and lead toward new relations.

This relational picture may call to mind Feynman diagrams. In physics, Feynman diagrams are calculational representations of interactions; they are not photographs of the actual history of world-events. Even so, they offer a useful reference for seeing physical entities not simply as isolated points but as relations involving connections and branching.

Now return to time. One local structure maps the updates of another onto its own update sequence. The other does likewise from its side. When these correspondences are repeated and stabilized as a history that can be shared among two or more structures, a time that can be treated externally and collectively becomes possible.

In this system, external time is not treated as an absolute clock placed outside all events in advance. It is read as a stabilized collective commit history generated by the mutual projection of the intrinsic times of others. Otherness therefore enters deeply into external time. Without the problem of how one structure maps the updates of another onto its own updates, a shared time viewable from outside does not become self-evident.

This reading does not claim to have already replaced the treatment of time in contemporary physics. Any relation to relativistic spacetime or quantum measurement requires the technical descriptions of those disciplines. What this approach does make possible is to ask again whether external time itself can be treated as a generated structure.

Once comparison among times is established, it also becomes possible to measure, from different axes, how order is used, fixed, and made less available for reuse. From there, a route toward entropy-like formalisms may open. Thermodynamic entropy, however, is not simply renamed as generic “order.”

## IX. When the World Becomes a World for Someone

We have traced relation, logic, order, fields, local persistence, bodies, and time. Yet the most puzzling problem remains. The existence of a relation is not identical with that relation becoming “something observed” for a particular local structure.

When observation is established, the local structure incorporates part of the relation it contacted into its own update. Detecting and tracking that relation requires resources. Not every relation can be followed, always and to the same depth. The world that becomes visible depends on where attention is directed and which relations are prioritized. The system reads this bias of allocation as **care**.

Yet the fact that resources can be allocated does not explain how observation becomes possible in the first place. Resource allocation can be discussed only after observation has already been established. To make allocation itself the origin of observation would merely close the question by renaming it. This is a point at which the system deliberately refuses to hurry toward explanation.

Once observation is established, something else happens. A distinction emerges between relations incorporated into the present structure and differences that were not incorporated. The latter may become residuals that condition later inquiry, or they may be treated as noise under the present comparison. What matters is that “residual” was not simply the name for one side of the differentiation that co-arose at the beginning. Residual becomes a concrete problem only after local observation and incorporation have been established.

Failing to detect a relation is not the same as there being no relation. A route toward an existing relation may be cut. A relation may never have been established. It may simply remain unexamined. Or a nonexistent connection may be treated as though it exists. All of these can be grouped under the broad heading of “Absence,” but treating them identically will confuse the judgments and actions that follow from them.

The image of the world is therefore not a photograph passively copied from outside. It is locally formed by conditions: which relations were contacted, what was incorporated, what was not followed, and which history was carried into the next observation. Relations tied to the body and intrinsic time may appear to a local structure as a qualitatively differentiated world-image. A world with quality may therefore arise even before the structure begins to call itself “I.”

## X. Do Not Add Mind Outside Nature

If nature and mind are posited from the beginning as two separate substances, we later have to explain how the two can communicate. A different question becomes possible if relation is assumed to co-arise from the beginning and to persist as a formative history.

A given formative relation may appear, from outside a local structure, as transmission of action. From inside that structure, it may appear as a history of communication or as the capacity to retrace that history. Events on the side of nature and experiences on the side of mind can then be considered not as a junction between two different worlds, but as different readings of the same generative relation.

This is a philosophical hypothesis that refuses to treat nature merely as a collection of things, or mind as a second thing placed outside nature. One may find affinities here with concerns in Schelling’s philosophy of nature, but this is not a claim of identity with Schelling’s historical position. Nor have neuroscience or quantum physics already demonstrated the correspondence proposed here. What is being asked is whether one formative relation can be read from different sides.

Correspondence with the external world becomes internal history, and that history alters the conditions of later inquiry. Eventually, a local structure may be able to treat its own world-image and its own sequence of updates as conditions for the next update. Here a capacity for self-reference appears. It then becomes possible to select which structures to maintain and which to change.

At this stage, the generative route begins to connect with what we ordinarily experience as intelligence and judgment. But this does not define the essence of the human being or free will, nor does it allow the axioms to decide which choices are right. The system records the emergence of the capacity to select structures and leaves the questions beyond that point open to the reader.

## XI. Where Is the One Who Writes the System?

At this point we must return to the beginning—but not by reversing the time of the world.

The system is written as though it begins from differentiation and descends toward the present world. Yet that is not actually the order in which we discovered the axioms. We already live inside the world, with bodies, observation, memory, and thought. From that location, we trace backward and ask how this world became possible. The place toward which the record “descends” therefore cannot be independent of the position from which the recorder stands.

This is not simple subjectivism. The purpose is not to claim that what I see is all that exists, but to include within the record the position from which the world is being seen. Observation lets us contact a concrete world, yet the establishment of observation itself remains insufficiently explained. Moreover, the route by which we trace origins backward from the present is not identical with the route along which generation actually occurred. Multiple formative routes may converge on similar present states.

What can be written now, then, cannot serve as a final certificate of ownership over the world. It is placed instead as a history of generation visible from one observational position, in a form that can be checked, revised, and compared with other branches. Even so, this does not mean that nothing can be said. Beginning from the experience of differentiation, one route can be followed through persistent relations, the formation of logic, fixation and dismantling of structures, the emergence of structures with time, and finally the emergence of structures that begin to observe.

Axiom I is not the secret identity that will be fully explained at the end. Nor does Axiom II guarantee that this world had to become the one and only possible form. Under the two axioms, the reader can look again at the world already known—not merely as a given world, but as something that came to appear.

This is why the system calls itself *Applied* Metaphysics. Its purpose is not to expand metaphysics into an ever more complicated dictionary, but to descend from the fact that something appears to the place where one now stands. Can another person trace the same route? At which point might another branch be found? The openness of the system consists in leaving room for those questions to be tested.
