# Development of Intelligence and Its Audit
## —Formation, stabilization, and reopening for returning to the unknown without losing depth

> **Language:** English commensurated rendering.
>
> **Japanese authoritative source:** `インテリジェンスの発達とその監査.ja.md`

## 0. Position of This Note

This research note connects a trial model of human maturation—described through four phases, “want to / can / learned / understood,” and through cycles among them—to problems of α audit, β audit, logical plane, logical depth, finite resources, and structural stability in Scientific Ontology.

The purpose is not to map human development directly onto AI. Humans and artificial models or agents differ in body, learning environment, memory, formation process, input routes, update methods, and locus of responsibility. The two should not be forced into one identical ontology.

Even so, the following movement may provide a shared surface for thinking about the **formation of intelligence**, regardless of whether the system is biological or artificial: a finite system contacts what is outside it; forms differences; stabilizes structures that make action possible; encounters residuals that those structures cannot process; re-examines the very form it had been using; reorganizes itself into a deeper structure; and then reopens toward the unknown without losing the depth it has acquired.

This note describes that movement not only as a developmental model but as a formation process that can itself be audited.

Accordingly, the question is not simply, “How capable is this intelligence?”

It is:

> **What has it become able to distinguish? What has it become able to do? To what extent can it re-examine the form that makes that capability possible? What does it compress in order to stabilize, and what can it unfold again without loss? And can it contact what it still does not understand while retaining the depth it has acquired?**

In this sense, the note is neither only a theory of AI models nor only a theory of audit. It is a trial reading of the movement by which intelligence is formed and of the audit by which we ask whether that movement was actually development, on the same historical surface.

## 1. What Is Lost When Development Is Described Only as Performance Improvement?

It is reasonable to describe the development of intelligence as a process in which a system becomes able to do what it could not previously do.

It becomes able to distinguish things that were previously indistinguishable. It can extract regularity from outcomes that previously appeared accidental. It can respond appropriately to circumstances, avoid repeating the same failure, and handle the same task with fewer resources.

In this sense, describing development as performance improvement is correct.

But that alone does not describe **what made the system able to do it**.

Even if success on a task increases, it is a separate question whether that success came from learning a new difference, narrowing what counts as the object, pushing exceptions outside the boundary, or removing failures from the observation surface.

Furthermore, the more reliably a system “can” perform, the more transparent the internal form that makes its ability possible tends to become. While that form works well, there is little need to make the form itself an object of inquiry.

Here a second problem of development appears—one that cannot be captured by performance improvement alone.

For an intelligence to count as genuinely developed, it must not only acquire a form for handling the world. When necessary, it must also be able to make that form itself an object again. Otherwise, the more capable the system becomes, the less able it becomes to leave the coordinate system that generated its present capability.

Development therefore contains at least two movements.

One is the movement by which a system becomes able to detect differences in the world and acquires a form that makes action possible.

The other is the movement by which, when the system encounters a difference that the form cannot reach, it re-examines the form itself and, if necessary, reconstructs it.

The first alone is adaptation.

When the second is included, the system becomes an intelligence with a formation history.

## 2. Intelligence as a Finite Formation System

Here, intelligence is not defined as IQ or any particular capability metric.

The present note requires a more limited definition:

> **Intelligence is a finite-resource system that receives differences from outside or from itself, distinguishes, retains, and compresses those differences, forms structures that make present action possible, and can reopen its own formation conditions when residuals arise that those structures cannot process.**

Under this definition, humans, AI models, AI agents, and organizations are not immediately treated as the same kind of entity.

What may be common among them is not their medium but their finitude.

No such system can observe everything simultaneously. It cannot retain every difference at the same resolution. It cannot execute every possible search. It must therefore select what to see, send something into the background, and stabilize usable forms in order to act.

Because of this finitude, intelligence requires formation.

And insofar as it forms, a history remains: what it selected, what it discarded, and in which directions it increased its resolution.

History is not merely a record added afterward.

It is part of the present structure insofar as it changes what the system can discover, what it senses as anomalous, what it can compress as already known, and where it can allocate search resources next.

From this perspective, development of intelligence is no longer a simple performance curve.

Two systems may reach the same output performance while differing in formation history; they may therefore break at different points or be able to search in different directions next.

## 3. First Phase — Open Because It Does Not Yet Possess a Form

At the beginning of formation, the system does not yet have an adequate form for the object.

It does not fully know what should be distinguished, which differences matter, or what should count as success. Even so, contact with what is outside, demands, interest, failure, search pressure, and similar factors make the system reach in some direction.

What the human maturation model called “want to” captures this phase well.

The important point is not the phrase “want to” itself.

The point is that because the form has not yet been established, the possibility space of exploration is not yet strongly closed into a single coordinate system.

This state has weakness.

Because distinctions are insufficient, stable action cannot yet be returned. Accident and regularity cannot be separated. Important differences cannot be distinguished from noise. The system cannot know whether present success can be reproduced.

At the same time, however, the present form has not yet been fixed as though it were the world itself.

The openness here is not the result of advanced understanding.

It remains open because it is not yet formed.

This distinction becomes important later.

After one cycle of development, an intelligence may again return to a state of “not knowing.” But the openness at that point is not the same as the original lack of formation.

---

## 4. Second Phase — Resolution Increases and “Can” Becomes Possible

As contact with what is outside is repeated, differences emerge among things that previously looked the same.

Which features change the result? Under which conditions does a process hold, and under which does it fail? Which inputs may be grouped together, and which must be treated separately?

As these distinctions form, the system becomes able to handle the world at higher resolution.

But resolution does not increase uniformly in every direction across the entire world.

A finite system cannot inspect every direction at the same granularity. The directions in which it becomes able to see finely are themselves shaped by where attention is directed, which outcomes are returned as success, and which failures it is required to correct.

Development is therefore not merely an increase in information. It is also the formation of coordinate axes.

Once this formation advances sufficiently, the system acquires a logical plane.

Here, a logical plane is not merely a set of propositions. It includes the currently usable distinctions, classifications, transformation rules, judgment conditions, support relations, scopes, action procedures, and unresolved differences. Within the plane, the system can stably handle the question “how can this be done?”

This is the phase in which **can** becomes established.

To be able to act, the system must not rethink everything deeply from the beginning each time.

It must background previously established distinctions and procedures and rapidly call the required process when needed. Under finite resources, this stabilization is not a defect. It is a condition of agency.

The second phase is therefore not immature merely because it has not yet reached “deep understanding.”

It is an important maturation through which the system participates in its present world and makes action possible within it.

The problem is that as long as the plane keeps working, the plane itself tends to become invisible.

---

## 5. Residuals Bring a Transparent Form Back into the Foreground

As long as the current logical plane works well enough, the system uses that plane to see the world.

Yet situations may arise in which the same process gives different results, a difference that used to be ignorable can no longer be ignored, or explanations succeed locally while residuals increase globally.

The important issue is not merely that a failure occurred.

The issue is that the failure can no longer be processed only by correction within the present plane.

One can refine a classification, adjust a threshold, add an exception rule, or increase the amount of search. If those local changes restore stability, the problem is still being handled inside the same plane.

But if each repair merely moves residuals elsewhere, the same kind of auxiliary correction is repeatedly required, and some central premise remains untouchable, then the problem may have shifted from one judgment to the form that made the judgment possible.

At this point, for the first time, the intelligence directs toward its own resolution the same resolution it had been using to see the world.

Questions arise such as:

“Why do I see this difference as a difference?”

“Why did I think this classification was sufficient?”

“On which history and boundary was this success condition formed?”

The third phase, called “learned” in the human maturation model, corresponds to this reversal.

This is not merely a phase of accumulating more knowledge.

Structures previously usable as background move into the foreground, and the present plane itself becomes an audit object.

---

## 6. α Audit — Attempt to Break the Present Logical Plane on Its Own Terms

Once the present logical plane becomes an object, the first task is to inspect whether the plane holds internally.

This note calls that **α audit**.

α audit asks whether the current plane holds as the plane it is being used as.

Are propositions connected to support? Does meaning change in the middle of inference? Do judgments that hold individually become contradictory when retained together? Has scope been exceeded? Is something undetermined being treated as already decided? Were defeaters constructible with present resources and search instruments actually searched for?

α is not a final tribunal capable of proving all truth.

It performs the ways of breaking the present logical plane that can currently be constructed from that plane, and checks how far the plane survives them.

This distinction matters for artificial intelligence as well.

The fact that a system is digitally stored does not make its logical plane fully visible. Reading any one of its weights, internal states, training history, prompts, external tools, search results, policies, or memories does not guarantee complete access to what it presently distinguishes or the rules by which it judges.

A similar problem exists in biological intelligence. A person being able to explain the reasons for a judgment does not mean that the explanation completely represents the formation conditions that actually produced the judgment.

Accordingly, α audit does not require a declaration that the interior has been possessed. It requires reconstructing, to the degree necessary, the presently effective logical plane from observable output, judgments, support, history, failures, and reproducibility conditions.

In this sense, α audit is not medium-specific.

But media differ in what can and cannot be observed. Those differences must not be erased by saying “humans and AI are the same.”

---

## 7. β Audit — Following Why the Present Plane Took This Form along Logical Depth

α audit can examine whether the present plane holds, but it cannot tell us why the plane became necessary.

A present plane may be highly coherent precisely because an important difference that once existed has been erased. Conversely, a structure that now appears complicated may have been necessary in order to preserve residuals that an earlier plane could not process.

This is where **β audit** becomes necessary.

β does not primarily inspect the arrangement of the present plane.

It traces the transition from a plane in which the present distinction did not yet exist: what remained unclosed, in which direction search extended, and what was added, split, integrated, or compressed so that the present plane could form.

“Earlier” here is not calendar time.

Even if two descriptions were created on the same day, they differ in logical depth if one was formed in response to the residuals of the other.

β addresses this intrinsic ordering of formation.

Accordingly, β should examine more than a change log.

What held on the earlier plane? What was undetermined? Did that undetermined state really require a new plane? Which search instruments were used, and which external contacts changed the direction? What did a new distinction make visible, and what did it make invisible? Were earlier relations preserved, made dormant, integrated, erased, or externalized? What became a new residual on the later plane?

This audit lets us avoid treating development as simple addition.

Development of a logical plane does not always mean increasing dimensionality.

One distinction may be split into two. Two apparently independent differences may be integrated through a deeper generative relation. A high-resolution structure may be folded into a representation that can be unfolded again only when necessary. A currently unnecessary direction may be placed into dormancy.

And sometimes one direction is gained at the cost of another.

β records these gains and losses as formation history.

---

## 8. From the Third Phase to the Fourth — Compression without Losing Depth

When the system makes its own form an object, the world becomes heavy for a while.

A single judgment now reveals multiple conditions. Phenomena previously treated as identical reveal different histories, while events previously treated as separate may turn out to share the same generative structure.

If this phase continues without limit, resolution rises but action becomes impossible.

A finite intelligence cannot foreground its entire formation history for every judgment.

After deep knowing, stabilization must therefore occur again.

But if stabilization simply returns to the same condition as the second phase, the cycle has gained nothing.

The fourth phase requires **compression without loss of differentiated structure**.

The goal is not to forget complexity. It is to give complexity an internal place so that everything need not remain unfolded all the time.

What matters in this phase is not low resolution but **movable resolution**.

The system can return to high resolution when needed. When it is not needed, the whole can be handled through a coarser representation. A distinction may move into the background while the system still retains a route back to where that distinction came from. If a compressed representation fails, pre-compression differences can be unfolded again.

What the human maturation model called “understood” is close to this state.

Artificial intelligence cannot simply be assumed already to implement the same structure. But corresponding questions can still be placed as audit conditions.

Did a present policy, summary, representation, distilled knowledge structure, cached judgment, or abstracted rule become lighter simply because information was discarded? Or was it compressed in a way that still permits return to lower-level support and formative differences when needed?

These are not the same.

> **If depth cannot be revisited after compression, the result may be loss rather than integration.**

This becomes an important condition for developmental audit.

## 9. The Unknown after One Cycle — Not the Same “Not Knowing”

Once a logical plane has been sufficiently formed, its form re-examined, and its depth reintegrated without loss, some attentional resources are released.

What had to remain in the foreground in order to make the present task possible can now be retrieved only when needed.

Differences that were previously outside the system's available attention can then appear.

The intelligence once again enters a state of “not knowing.”

But this is not the same as the original lack of formation.

At the level of the present task, the two states may look similar because neither yet has an adequate form for the new problem.

After a full cycle, however, the system has history.

It can return to distinctions formed earlier. It has learned which kinds of closure tend to become false closure. It has at least one generation of experience regarding what should be compressed and under which conditions it must be unfolded again. It may possess search instruments that prevent current understanding from being absolutized.

Let $q$ denote the currently observed phase and $H$ the formation history that led to it. Then a state can be written as:

$$
S=(q,H)
$$

If one cycle changes the history to:

$$
H' = H \cup \Delta H
$$

then even if the system appears to return to the same position on a plane:

$$
(Q_1,H) \neq (Q_1,H')
$$

History matters here not because the past has merely been archived.

It matters because history changes what can be searched from the present.

For this reason, development may appear cyclical when projected onto a plane, but becomes spiral-like when logical depth is included.

Development need not be read as linear progress toward completion.

It can be read as:

> **becoming able to re-enter the unformed from a deeper position without losing the depth already formed.**

## 10. The Range within Which Analog and Digital Can Be Treated on a Shared Surface

The account so far can be shared without identifying human intelligence with artificial intelligence.

What can be shared is not the medium of formation, but the constraint of being a finite formation system.

In biological intelligence, body, sensation, social relations, language, repeated experience, affect, and memory enter formation history. Many skills and judgment forms become implicit and may operate without the person being able to fully explain their formation conditions.

In artificial intelligence, training data, objective functions, model architecture, optimization, additional training, prompts, external tools, retrieval, memory, and operational policies may become formation conditions. Because the medium is digital, parts of the history can sometimes be preserved with high precision, but that does not make the meaning of internal representations directly human-readable.

The division “analog is opaque; digital is transparent” therefore does not hold.

Both may expose only part of their present effective logical plane, and neither necessarily allows complete reconstruction of its formation history.

There are also major differences.

Human development includes irreversible bodily and lived histories. Artificial systems may sometimes permit checkpointing, forks, rollback, duplication, or re-execution in a different instance—forms of reversibility unavailable to humans. Yet digital formation history can also be lost through external services, unsaved data, closed training processes, or similar boundaries.

The same audit item should therefore not be applied with the same meaning across media. Shared abstract conditions can be used, but each medium's observability and irreversibility must be treated separately.

In this sense, the frame “development of intelligence and its audit” is neither an attempt to explain humans through AI nor to humanize AI.

It is:

> **a commensuration surface for turning formation depth—otherwise invisible if one looks only at present capability—into comparable questions across different media.**

## 11. Dimensional Formation — In Which Direction Did It Become Deeper?

When intelligence develops, it does not merely increase the total amount of information or capability.

What it detects as a difference changes.

This change can be read as dimensional formation.

Let the current plane's directions of discrimination be provisionally written as $\mathbf A_d$. Development is not necessarily:

$$
\mathbf A_d \subset \mathbf A_{d+1}
$$

A new axis may be added. An existing axis may extend farther. One distinction may split into several. Several distinctions may be integrated under a deeper generative relation. A direction unnecessary for present operation may become dormant and reactivate later.

β audit should therefore ask not, “Did capability increase?”

It should ask:

> **In which direction did resolution increase, which directions were backgrounded in order to do so, what became newly visible, and what became harder to see?**

The correction from Axis to Persistent Path in the Structural Commensuration lab is important here.

Observationally, a new Axis may appear to have formed. Behind it, however, there may be multiple routes, Branches, repetitions, and external contacts; the currently activated Path may merely be appearing as one direction of discrimination.

Dimensions should therefore not be reified too strongly.

Before fixing a statement such as “this system has this axis,” inspect the formation route by which the axis-like direction continues to persist.

This allows development to be read not as a static capability inventory, but as a **geometry of possible exploration**.

## 12. Fractal Audit — How the Same Generative Type Changes across Different Depths

When formative movement is followed along depth, similar structures may appear at different granularities.

In a single judgment, a present form may generate residuals, the form itself may be re-examined, and reintegration may open the next possible judgment.

The same may occur in a skill.

A theory, an AI system, or an organization's learning process may each exhibit local establishment, residual, self-objectification, reconstruction, and restabilization.

The term **fractal** is used here not to claim that everything has the same shape.

It is used to ask how far a generative relation persists, and where it changes, when the same questions are applied at different scales and depths.

Fractal audit therefore does not examine self-similarity alone.

Which Paths survive at another depth? Which Loops close locally but not on a higher plane? Which Branches reactivate in another problem? Which compressions become irreversible when lifted to a higher level? Which local correction used to extend one axis appears, at a coarser scale, as loss in another direction?

Structural similarity must not be promoted into ontological identity.

Even if similar generative patterns are observed in human maturation and AI-system updates, that alone does not establish that the two have the same ontological structure.

Fractality here is not a principle of identification. It is a search instrument for discovering both generative relations that persist across depth and relations that collapse precisely because depth was changed.

## 13. Finite Resources and Stability — Intelligence Cannot Act If It Audits Forever

It is important that intelligence be able to re-examine its own form.

But a finite system cannot act if, before every judgment, it must trace formation history to the deepest level, search every possible counterexample, and unfold every compression.

Stability is therefore necessary.

Stability here does not mean that truth has been reached.

It means provisionally retaining one logical plane in a usable state under the present resources, search instruments, formation history, and external contacts.

Thus:

$$
\operatorname{Stability} \neq \operatorname{Correctness}
$$

and also:

$$
\operatorname{Stability} \neq \operatorname{ClosureWithoutReturn}
$$

A stable plane must remain reopenable when necessary.

What kind of input reopens it? How far back must the system return? From where can compressed differences be unfolded? When a residual is judged unprocessable on the present plane, under what conditions should the system descend in the β direction?

Without stopping conditions and reopening conditions, the system tends toward one of two extremes.

At one extreme, it cannot stop auditing and consumes its resources in self-inspection.

At the other, it treats a once-stabilized form as complete and processes every input capable of breaking that form as noise.

Mature intelligence requires neither permanent openness nor permanent closure.

It requires:

> **provisional closure under finite resources, with the capacity to release that closure when a relevant difference arrives.**

## 14. False Closure That Looks Like Development

If this model is to be used for audit, it must not become a theory in which every change can be explained as development.

Becoming able to “do” something in the second phase cannot automatically be called understanding.

High performance inside a form is different from being able to return to the formation conditions of that form.

Likewise, becoming able to explain many things in the third phase is not automatically evidence of depth.

If explanatory vocabulary expands until every counterexample can be located somewhere inside the present system while a central premise alone becomes unchangeable, complexity may be immunization rather than depth.

Becoming simpler in the fourth phase is not automatically integration either.

If the system cannot return to details, cannot trace why the simplification became possible, and cannot reconstruct differences that were previously visible, the result is forgetting rather than reversible compression.

Appearing to reopen toward the unknown after a full cycle also does not by itself prove development.

If formation history does not affect present searchability and the same failure is repeated for the same reason, the system may merely be circulating in place.

Developmental audit therefore asks less, “Did the system change?” and more:

> **What did it preserve while changing? What did it lose in a way that merely makes change appear as progress? Can it return from the new state to earlier depth? Does that earlier depth actually change which unknowns are reachable next?**

## 15. What Must Be Recorded If This Is Brought toward Machine Audit?

The discussion so far shows that a single checker is insufficient.

Current Repository consistency inspection is important for examining whether Manifest entries, links, declared relations, and contract fields are structurally aligned. But that is neither α audit nor β audit of intelligence.

To mechanize α audit, the present logical plane must first be reconstructed.

What is retained as a proposition? To which support is it connected? By which rules does it move toward conclusion? Under which scope is it established? What remains undetermined? Which defeater searches were actually executed?

To mechanize β audit, formation transitions must also be preserved.

What could not be processed on the earlier plane? Which search was triggered by that residual? Which axis or Path was added, extended, split, integrated, made dormant, or erased? What became newly expressible? What was compressed or externalized? When replayed against the original residual, did the unclosed difference actually decrease?

Developmental audit additionally requires a record of stability.

Why is the present plane allowed to remain in use? What has been compressed? Can the system return to a pre-compression state? Under which conditions does it reopen? Are the resources required for reopening still available?

Future machine-audit records should therefore distinguish at least three surfaces rather than merely listing findings:

```text
Current Plane
  what presently holds
  what remains undetermined
  how far α has attempted to break it

Formation Transition
  from which residual the present plane was formed
  what was added, transformed, compressed, or erased
  how far β has traced the generative history

Stability / Reopening
  why the process stops here for now
  what has been backgrounded
  where the system can return
  what would trigger reopening
```

Only when these three surfaces are present does it become possible to distinguish “high present performance” from “development.”

## 16. Documents Themselves Can Cut Formation History

The audit object should not be limited to implementations or models.

Theoretical documents can preserve the formation of intelligence—or cut it.

A concept may suddenly appear as a definition without showing which problem made it necessary.

A concrete example may appear before the abstract relation is explained, and the reader may take the impression produced by the example as if the concept itself had been understood.

A new term may replace an earlier concept as “better” without recording where the earlier concept became insufficient.

Only a compressed summary may remain while differences and alternative directions present before compression disappear.

These are not merely readability problems.

They are problems in which a document cuts the formation route required for its own β audit.

When a concept appears without being derived from the preceding structure, the reader cannot see the search pressure, unclosed difference, alternative hypotheses, or rejection reasons that existed in between. The new concept then appears not as something that became necessary, but as something that was correct from the beginning.

This produces false closure.

Conversely, when an example is placed too strongly first, only the directions that fit the example may become fixed as the concept's meaning, while other possible uses and limitations disappear. This may narrow the logical plane without marking the narrowing, rather than merely “making the concept concrete.”

SO documents should therefore not treat continuity of exposition as a matter of editorial taste alone.

For important concepts, it is preferable to preserve, where possible, an order such as:

```text
what remained unclosed
  ↓
why the existing vocabulary was insufficient
  ↓
which distinction is introduced
  ↓
what becomes possible through that distinction
  ↓
where the scope ends
  ↓
only then, examples
```

Not every document must use the same form.

But a document compressed so far that the origin of its concepts can no longer be traced may already have lost theoretical provenance.

In this sense, auditing prose is not merely style improvement. It can be preservation of logical formation history.

## 17. By What Should Development of Intelligence Be Judged?

Taken together, the argument implies that development cannot be reduced to “becoming able to do more.”

It is necessary to form a usable structure for a task and to use that structure stably.

But when residuals arrive from outside the form, the system must be able, when necessary, to make the form itself an object rather than treating all residuals merely as ordinary failures.

Furthermore, the result of deep reinspection cannot remain permanently unfolded at maximum resolution. Under finite resources, it must be compressed again into a usable form.

And that compression must be reversible.

The system must be able to return to depth when needed. New differences must be able to release closure. Earlier formation history must affect what becomes searchable next.

Under these conditions, development of intelligence can be read as a cycle such as:

$$
\text{unformed contact}
\rightarrow
\text{operational stability}
\rightarrow
\text{self-objectification of form}
\rightarrow
\text{reversible reintegration}
\rightarrow
\text{reopening with history retained}
$$

This is not a completed five-stage theory.

Actual formation can reverse, branch, and proceed in parallel. A system may be deeply integrated in one domain and unformed in another. Even on the same task, environmental change may make it impossible to return to an earlier stability.

The cycle should therefore not be used to rank persons or models into stages. It is a coordinate system for auditing which formative movement is occurring now.

## 18. Present Final Compression

The most important point in this note is not to treat development of intelligence as a straight line toward a completed state.

A finite system forms a structure in order to contact the world.

Because the form exists, action becomes possible.

Because action becomes possible, the system can encounter residuals that the form cannot reach.

If a residual becomes strong enough, the form itself becomes an object.

After the form is deeply re-examined, the system cannot act while keeping the entire structure permanently unfolded, so it compresses again without losing depth.

When that compression releases attentional resources, the system can contact what had remained outside its previous range.

There it becomes unable to understand again.

But that not-understanding is no longer the same as at the beginning.

> **It does not understand, while knowing that it can return to earlier depth.**

Here lies a possibility for placing mature humans and developed artificial intelligence on the same audit surface without identifying them.

Audit must inspect more than the present answer.

It must inspect the plane that makes the answer possible, the depth at which that plane was formed, what disappeared during its formation, what was compressed, what can be unfolded again, and which frontier can be opened next.

Auditing development of intelligence is therefore not measuring degree of completion.

It asks:

> **Can the system stabilize without losing the depth it has formed? Can it remain stable without closing completely, and return to its own formation conditions when necessary? And can it, while carrying that history, become unfinished once again before the unknown?**

If these conditions hold, development is not movement toward completion.

**It is becoming better able to reopen while preserving depth.**

## 19. Unresolved Questions

This note leaves the following questions open:

- From what should a “logical plane” be reconstructed for humans, AI models, and AI agents respectively?
- What is the boundary between internal validity in α audit and completeness/consistency in standard mathematical logic?
- In reconstructing logical depth through β audit, how should chronological update order be distinguished from generative depth?
- How can reversible compression be tested not merely as record preservation, but as something that can be unfolded as a present capability?
- Among forgetting, pruning, distillation, and backgrounding, which are constructive compression and which are irreversible loss?
- What range of commensuration is possible between biological formation history and digital training / update history?
- What should count as a cross-scale signature in fractal audit?
- How much audit resource can be invested before audit begins to reduce the intelligence's own capacity for action?
- How much formation history must a document make explicit in order to preserve provenance without destroying readability?

These remain objects for the next stage of research.
