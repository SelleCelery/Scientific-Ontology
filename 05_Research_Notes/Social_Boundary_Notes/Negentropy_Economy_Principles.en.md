## Chapter I. Definitions

### Article 1. Negentropy Economy

A negentropy economy is a boundary-economic framework for assessing how exchange, ownership, price, and institutions increase or decrease a field's order-generating capacity, and what residuals they externalize as residues.

### Article 2. Value

Value is not exchangeability itself.

Value is a function that, within a given field, increases or maintains the order-generating capacity of life, judgment, relation, knowledge, institutions, environment, and the future.

### Article 3. Price

Price is not the whole of value.

Price is one cross-section of value that has become institutionally available for operational correspondence.

### Article 4. Exchange

Exchange is the movement of resources, labor, information, attention, rights, responsibilities, and expectations between multiple subjects or fields.

Exchange is not sufficiently assessed merely by the fact that consent has been established.

Exchange must be re-collated according to what was cultivated, what was depleted, and what was transferred to another field as a result.

### Article 5. Ownership

Ownership is a social heuristic for operationally aligning resources, responsibilities, use rights, exclusivity, and inheritability.

Ownership is powerful.

However, the quantity of ownership does not directly indicate capacity, personhood, insight, creativity, empathy, capacity to hold residuals, or history-density.

### Article 6. Operational Correspondence

Operational correspondence is the condition in which a heuristic meshes operationally with external structures such as society, institutions, accounting, contracts, markets, technology, nature, bodies, and communities.

Operational correspondence enables function, prediction, recordkeeping, comparison, and institutional operation.

However, operational correspondence does not guarantee the whole of value.

### Article 7. Collation

Collation is the operation of reading what the success or failure of operational correspondence, its agreement, disagreement, residuals, and residues mean.

Collation asks not merely whether something functioned, but what became invisible behind that function.

### Article 8. Closure

Closure is a boundary operation that treats further collation as unnecessary for an exchange, process, contract, account closing, evaluation, institutional judgment, or semantic process, and handles it as processed.

In epistemological contexts, closure appears as closing a semantic loop or internal loop.

In economic and institutional contexts, closure appears as treating something as resolved, accounted for, settled, cleared, or responsibility-settled.

Closure is necessary for social operation, but it does not mean that residuals or residues have disappeared.

### Article 9. False Closure

False closure is the treatment of an exchange, process, contract, account closing, evaluation, or institutional judgment as resolved, accounted for, cleared, or responsibility-settled even though residuals or residues in fact remain.

False closure blocks access routes to residuals and makes the externalization of residues invisible.

### Article 10. Residual

A residual is what remains as a re-collatable difference that could not be fully closed by operational correspondence, classification, pricing, ownership, evaluation, explanation, or account closing.

### Article 11. Residue

A residue is what continues to act while remaining difficult to see after an unclosed residual has sedimented into bodies, environments, institutions, relations, signs, the unconscious, future generations, or other fields.

### Article 12. Residue Assessment

Residue assessment is the operation of describing what burdens have been transferred where, and who bears them across what time span, behind value generation, exchange, institutions, profit, ownership, or growth.

## Chapter II. Value, Price, and Account Closing

### Article 13. Limits of Pricing

Things that can be priced are not the only things that have value.

Among things that cannot be priced, there are also things that support the order-generating capacity of life, relation, knowledge, creativity, community, environment, and the future.

### Article 14. The Ambiguity of Profit

Profit can indicate an increase in order-generating capacity.

However, profit can also arise through the externalization of residues into other fields.

Therefore, profit is not by itself proof of value.

### Article 15. Collating Growth

Economic growth can be evaluated as an increase in order-generating capacity only when it is collated with the renewability of bodies, environments, institutions, relations, and future generations.

Uncollated growth may involve the accumulation of residues.

### Article 16. Account Closing

Account closing is the operation of institutionally closing value movements, revenues, costs, and liabilities within a given period.

Account closing produces records and comparability.

However, bodily burdens, environmental burdens, relational burdens, future burdens, and capacity residuals that were not included in the account closing have not disappeared.

They remain subject to assessment as off-account residues.

### Article 17. Off-Account Residues

Off-account residues are unprocessed burdens that have been treated as processed in accounting, contractual, or institutional terms, but that continue to affect bodies, environments, institutions, relations, capacities, and the future.

Accounts close.

Fields do not close.

### Article 18. Limits of Settlement

Settlement is the operation of treating institutional debts, claims, responsibilities, and transactional relations as processed.

Settlement creates legal or accounting closure.

However, the fact that something has been settled does not mean that residues in the field have disappeared.

### Article 19. Limits of Responsibility Settlement

Responsibility-settled means a condition in which an institution has determined who has taken on what.

However, even when institutional responsibility settlement has been completed, residues may not have disappeared.

Burdens that remain after something has been treated as responsibility-settled are subject to re-collation.

## Chapter III. The Ownership Heuristic

### Article 20. The Operational-Correspondence Capacity of Ownership

Ownership makes responsibility attribution, resource allocation, recordkeeping, quantification, institutionalization, and inheritance easier.

For this reason, ownership has high operational-correspondence capacity.

### Article 21. Mismatched Correspondence Between Ownership and Value

The quantity of ownership must not be directly projected onto capacity, personhood, or value.

Ownership is a heuristic for resource management. It is not a measure of the total value of human existence.

### Article 22. One-Dimensional Compression by Ownership

The ownership heuristic compresses multidimensional history-fields into one-dimensional rankings such as assets, annual income, status, or market value.

This compression increases operational efficiency, but it also generates large quantities of residuals.

### Article 23. Limits of Inequality Display

Difference itself is not immediately the problem.

The problem is converting multidimensional differences into one-dimensional rankings such as ownership quantity, income, status, or market value, and treating those rankings as higher or lower human value.

### Article 24. Capacity Residuals

A capacity residual is an undiscovered or underutilized capacity that has fallen outside evaluation systems, ownership systems, connection structures, qualification systems, or title systems.

Capacity residuals are not a problem of the individual, but a problem of society's search capacity.

### Article 25. Opportunity Allocation Through Connection

When connections, affiliations, networks, membership systems, or eligibility to enter closed fields replace capacity search itself rather than merely assisting it, capacity residuals arise.

Society is not necessarily making maximum use of capacity.

Society may be treating only easily observed capacities as capacity.

## Chapter IV. Residuals and Residues

### Article 26. Residuals Are Not Failures

Residuals are not merely failures, noise, or errors.

Residuals may generate the next question, the next classification, the next institution, or the next heuristic.

### Article 27. Disposal of Residuals

When residuals are disposed of prematurely, false closure occurs.

False closure means treating what is unresolved as resolved and blocking the route to re-collation.

### Article 28. Excessive Retention of Residuals

When residuals are retained without limit, judgment becomes chaotic.

Therefore, the retention of residuals requires boundary operations.

### Article 29. Transfer of Residues

Residuals do not disappear.

Unprocessed residuals may be transferred to another boundary and appear as bodily, environmental, institutional, relational, capacity-related, or future residues.

### Article 30. Reappearance of Residues

Residues may not have vanished from the field, but may have sedimented into places where they are difficult to see.

They reappear as fatigue, distrust, pollution, inequality, isolation, loss of meaning, institutional dysfunction, or undiscovered capacity.

### Article 31. Semantic Pressure

Semantic pressure is the pressure by which unclosed residuals or residues demand re-collation, reclassification, reinterpretation, or institutional revision.

Semantic pressure is not merely subjective discomfort.

It is a sign that unprocessed burdens remaining in a field are demanding epistemic or institutional response.

## Chapter V. Residue Assessment

### Article 32. Assessment Objects

Residue assessment covers at least the following objects:

1. bodily residues
2. environmental residues
3. institutional residues
4. relational residues
5. capacity residuals
6. future-generation residues
7. semantic residues

### Article 33. Bodily Residues

Bodily residues are residues that appear as fatigue, illness, burnout, depletion of attentional resources, or decline in judgment.

### Article 34. Environmental Residues

Environmental residues are residues that appear as pollution, resource depletion, ecosystem destruction, or climate burden.

### Article 35. Institutional Residues

Institutional residues are residues that appear as responsibility shifting, invisible labor, educational inequality, health inequality, information inequality, or institutional distrust.

### Article 36. Relational Residues

Relational residues are residues that appear as isolation, distrust, recognition-game dynamics, opportunity allocation through closed connections, or deterioration of community.

### Article 37. Capacity Residuals

Capacity residuals are residues generated when undiscovered capacities, non-market value, creativity, sensitivity to long-term safety, philosophical understanding, structural grasping capacity, and similar capacities fall outside evaluation systems.

### Article 38. Future-Generation Residues

Future-generation residues are burdens transferred to future generations for the sake of current profit, consumption, ownership, or institutional stability.

### Article 39. Semantic Residues

Semantic residues are residues that remain in a field as discomfort, distrust, a sense of meaninglessness, a sense of loss, or difficult-to-verbalize pressure, even though they have been treated as processed institutionally, in accounting, contractually, or evaluatively.

## Chapter VI. Exchange, Profit, and Externalization

### Article 40. Collating Exchange

Exchange is not sufficiently assessed merely by the fact that both parties agreed.

Exchange is re-collated according to what burdens were transferred behind it to third parties, nature, institutions, or the future.

### Article 41. Residue Assessment of Profit

When assessing profit, the following questions must be placed before it:

1. What did it cultivate?
2. What did it wear down?
3. What did it leave as invisible debt?
4. Who bore it?
5. Across what time span will it return?

### Article 42. Externalized Burdens

An externalized burden does not disappear from the field even if it has disappeared in accounting terms.

It reappears at another boundary.

### Article 43. Assessment Without Presupposing Malice

Residue assessment does not presuppose the malice of a specific subject.

The object of assessment is not who is bad, but which heuristic corresponded with what, which collation it omitted, and where it transferred residues.

### Article 44. Exploitation

Exploitation is a structure in which, behind an exchange that is institutionally or accounting-wise valid, burdens, responsibilities, pain, future opportunities, and capacity residuals are transferred to others or to the outside, and that transfer is made invisible.

Exploitation is not judged only by the presence or absence of malice.

Exploitation is assessed as a structure of residue transfer.

### Article 45. Limits of Discharge

Even when discharge is established through contracts, account closing, settlement, certification, or audit, residues remaining in the field may not disappear.

Discharge is institutional closure, not the disappearance of residues.

## Chapter VII. Collation With the Biosphere, Nature, and Future Generations

### Article 46. The Financial Sphere and the Life Sphere

Finance is a highly efficient heuristic for value operational correspondence.

However, operational correspondence within the financial sphere does not guarantee collation with the life sphere.

### Article 47. Collation With Regeneration Speed

Institutions, exchange, ownership, and profit must be collated with the regeneration speed of bodies, communities, ecosystems, and future generations.

Value extraction that exceeds regeneration speed generates residue accumulation.

### Article 48. Collation With Nature

The fact that something can be owned does not mean that it is sustainable.

The fact that something can be owned does not mean that it is renewable.

The fact that something can be owned does not mean that it corresponds operationally with an ecosystem.

### Article 49. Collation With the Future

The stability of present institutions does not mean that collation with future generations has been established.

Burdens transferred to future generations are difficult to see in present accounts.

That invisibility is not a reason for discharge.

## Chapter VIII. Non-Institutionality and Institutional Collation

### Article 50. The Non-Institutional Status of These Principles

These principles are not laws, policies, accounting standards, or ethical codes themselves.

These principles are a theoretical framework for collating with them.

### Article 51. Collation With Institutions

Institutions are assessed according to what they make observable, what they evaluate, what they discharge, and what they externalize as residues.

### Article 52. Purpose of Assessment

The purpose of assessment is not to immediately negate institutions.

The purpose of assessment is to make visible again what institutions have treated as closed.

### Article 53. Connection to Institutional Revision

Residue assessment may connect to institutional revision, contract design, accounting support, audit, organizational evaluation, public policy, and technology design.

However, residue assessment must not be immediately converted into institutional command.

Assessment is first performed for the sake of re-collation.

## Supplementary Chapter. Tokens, Audit, and Asymmetry

### Article 54. Token

In these principles, a token is not a monetary unit. It is an operational unit for recording the time, steps, attention, computational resources, and resources spent on processing, search, collation, re-collation, judgment, waiting, and responsibility transfer.

Tokens are auxiliary units for assessing burdens that are not expressed by money.

### Article 55. Non-Ownership of Tokens

Tokens in these principles must not be treated as currency, assets, ownership rights, circulation permits, or credit securities.

Tokens are operational units for describing processing, search, collation, re-collation, judgment deferral, responsibility transfer, and residue transfer.

When tokens are converted into ownership quantity, credit ranking, market value, or passage rights, a new ownership heuristic arises.

In that case, the token system itself becomes an object of residue assessment.

### Article 56. Real-Direction Tokens

Real-direction tokens are institutionally recordable processing units such as payment, receipt, contract, account closing, settlement, ownership transfer, work time, or computational steps.

Real-direction tokens are used to visualize value that has been brought into operational correspondence.

### Article 57. Imaginary-Direction Tokens

Imaginary-direction tokens are auxiliary units for describing search pressure, judgment deferral, ambiguous responsibility, bodily burdens, environmental burdens, relational burdens, and future burdens that remain as unprocessed burdens in a field even though they have been treated institutionally as processed.

Imaginary-direction tokens are introduced for residue assessment.

### Article 58. Real Direction and Imaginary Direction

Money is primarily strong in real-direction operational correspondence.

Residues appear as unaccounted burdens extending in the imaginary direction.

Therefore, residue assessment is the operation of collating real-direction account closing with imaginary-direction unprocessed burdens.

### Article 59. Invisible Criteria

Invisible criteria are standards for burdens that are not directly recorded by price, ownership, account closing, contracts, accounting, or evaluation systems, but that should be considered in residue assessment.

Invisible criteria include bodily burdens, environmental burdens, relational burdens, capacity residuals, judgment deferral, ambiguous responsibility, and burdens transferred to future generations.

### Article 60. Prohibition on Conversion Into Circulation Gatekeeping

Residue assessment must not be used as a means of circulation gatekeeping, passage permission, discharge certification, ranking, market control, or information control.

Residue assessment is not an authority for stopping circulation, but an operation for re-collating burdens transferred through circulation.

When new gatekeeping revenue is generated by residue assessment, that revenue structure is included in the object of assessment.

### Article 61. The Residue Responsibility of Auditors

Those who assess residues are not irresponsible observers standing outside the object of assessment.

Auditors, evaluators, certifiers, and operators of visualization devices bear responsibility for burdens, exclusions, misclassifications, tolls, information asymmetries, and judgment delegation newly generated by their assessment activities.

Audit must not be performed in order to sell discharge.

Audit is performed in order to restore capacity for re-collation.

### Article 62. Assessment of Asymmetric Profit

When a subject profits through residue assessment, audit, certification, visualization, or token management, it must be assessed which residues that profit reduced and which residues it newly generated.

The following points in particular must be checked:

1. Whether the burden is biased only toward the assessed party.
2. Whether only the auditor obtains informational advantage.
3. Whether the aim has become passing certification rather than reducing residues.
4. Whether token acquisition has become a new toll.
5. Whether weaker subjects are being made to bear more assessment costs.
6. Whether the assessment system has become a new ownership heuristic.

### Article 63. Right to Re-Collation

A subject that undergoes residue assessment has the right to request re-collation of the assessment result.

The right to re-collation is the right to request explanation and reconsideration regarding the grounds for assessment, judgment criteria, records used, residues inferred, responsibilities assigned, token evaluation, and calculation of imaginary-direction burdens.

Assessment without the right to re-collation does not make residues visible. It fixes residues.

## Supplementary Provisions. AI-Assisted Audit

### Supplementary Provision 1. Position of AI

AI may be used in residue assessment as audit assistance, visualization assistance, and search assistance.

AI is not a judging subject.

AI must not make the final determination that a residue exists.

AI is used to detect discrepancies between processing recorded in the real direction and unprocessed burdens remaining in the imaginary direction, and to return the need for re-collation to humans or institutions.

### Supplementary Provision 2. Asymmetry in AI Audit

When AI audit is introduced, the following asymmetries must be checked:

1. Whether only the auditing side owns the data and model.
2. Whether explainability is returned to the audited side.
3. Whether there are routes for objection and re-collation.
4. Whether AI scores are being abused as discharge certificates.
5. Whether audit costs are biased toward weaker subjects.
6. Whether AI audit itself has become new gatekeeping revenue.

### Supplementary Provision 3. Prohibited Uses of AI Audit

AI audit must not be used for the following purposes:

1. Selling discharge without reducing residues.
2. Unilaterally ranking assessment subjects.
3. Circulating only real-direction scores while leaving imaginary-direction burdens invisible.
4. Fixing responsibility through black-box evaluations that cannot be re-collated.
5. Creating circulation gates in order to maximize the auditor's profit.

### Supplementary Provision 4. Implementation Principles

When implementing a residue assessment system, at least the following conditions must be satisfied:

1. Record the grounds for assessment.
2. Distinguish real-direction tokens from imaginary-direction tokens.
3. Display the transfer destination of residues.
4. Display the auditor's profit structure.
5. Secure routes for re-collation.
6. Do not treat AI outputs as final judgments.
7. Include the assessment system itself among the objects of assessment.

### Supplementary Provision 5. Critical Point

At present, it remains unformulated how much residue accumulation causes a field to undergo phase transition or collapse.

### Supplementary Provision 6. Assessment Indicators

Reappearance frequency, transfer distance, time delay, degree of invisibilization, difficulty of re-collation, life-correspondence failure, and capacity-search loss are candidate indicators for residue assessment.

### Supplementary Provision 7. Future Tasks

Future tasks are to lower residue assessment into a visible format and collate it with concrete cases in institutions, organizations, AI, and economic activity.

### Supplementary Provision 8. Ethical Pressure

If, contrary to these principles, residue assessment is used as a circulation gate, discharge market, certification interest, information control, or ranking device, that act is recorded as residue externalization carried out under the name of negentropy economy.

At that point, what should be asked is not merely whether there was a violation.

The question is: even then, did you still do it?
