## A Variable Model of Sensibility and Understanding Through Attention and Abstraction

## 0. Position

This document is a foundational note on how cognitive axes are generated, stabilized, and transformed within Scientific Ontology.

It owns one question:

> When a difference contacts a history-bearing observer, how is the direction formed that determines what becomes perceptible, what becomes intelligible, and what becomes invisible?

This document does not define the conditions that justify knowledge itself. The conditions under which knowledge is constituted—including operational correspondence, collation, false closure, and re-collatability—are treated primarily in [`Boundary_Epistemological_Critique.en.md`](../01_Sat_Truth/Boundary_Epistemological_Critique.en.md).

Nor does this document define the conditions under which information becomes meaning. The relations among meaning loops, history, qualia, and intrinsic time are treated primarily in [`Meaning_Generation_Model.md`](../01_Sat_Truth/Meaning_Generation_Model.md).

What this document addresses is the process through which the sensibility and understanding of a local observer are organized in particular directions through history, attention, abstraction, value, cost, residuals, and repetition under those conditions.

In this sense, this document is not a critique of the conditions under which cognition is constituted. It is a dynamics of how cognitive direction is generated.

### 0.1 Why This Document Belongs in Raj / Beauty

This document is placed in Raj / Beauty because it treats cognition not as a fixed mirror, but as a movement through which differences form relations, are selected, are backgrounded, and acquire form.

Here, Beauty does not refer only to decorative beauty.

It refers to a generative movement in which distinct elements come into contact, some become foreground, others become background, and a world acquires a cognitively accessible form.

A cognitive axis is the direction in which this generative movement has stabilized through history.

## 1. Problem Setting

People appear to see the same world, yet they do not see the same world.

Faced with the same event, one person sees gain and loss.  
Another sees responsibility.  
Another sees beauty.  
Another sees danger.  
Another sees a structure of domination.  
Another sees exhaustion.  
Another sees a cutoff of meaning.  
Another sees a return path that still remains.

This is not merely a difference of opinion.

Before an opinion is formed, what is felt as real already differs.

What the felt material is then organized as also differs.

The same delay may appear to one person as negligence and to another as institutional failure.  
The same silence may appear to one person as indifference and to another as suspension or defense.  
The same rule may appear to one person as order and to another as a cutoff of history.

What changes here is not only the conclusion.

What rises as a signal.  
What becomes an object.  
What becomes a cause.  
What becomes a responsibility.  
What is retained as value.  
What is sent into the background as cost.

Differences in cognition appear as differences in this formative process.

## 2. Basic Thesis

The basic thesis of this document is as follows:

> A cognitive axis is formed when a difference bearing entropy attributes contacts an observer's boundary, alters the organization of sensibility and understanding through attention and abstraction, and becomes a stabilized direction of observation through the weighting of value and cost, residuals, and repetition.

The minimal model can be represented as follows:

```text
Difference
  ↓
Boundary contact
  ↓
Modulation by history
  ↓
Attention / Abstraction
  ↓
Organization of sensibility / understanding
  ↓
Weighting of value / cost
  ↓
Residual / Residue
  ↓
Repetition
  ↓
Cognitive axis
```

A cognitive axis is not identical with personality, ideology, character, or ability.

It is a direction that makes an observer more likely, when reading the world, to foreground certain differences, background others, and construct objects, causes, responsibilities, and values in particular forms.

It is not a fixed essence.

It bears history and may stabilize, become rigid, drift, branch, and reconnect.

---

## 3. Differences Contacting a Boundary

In this document, a difference is something that contacts an observer's boundary and generates some form of response, friction, attention, cutoff, meaning pressure, or residual.

A difference does not appear only as neutral information.

The same event may disperse as temporary noise for one observer and condense as a persistent question for another.  
The same words may connect with one history and fail to connect with another.  
The same institution may appear as order from one position and as an unprocessable burden from another.

This document calls the following kinds of properties displayed by a difference at a boundary its **entropy attributes**:

- tendency to disperse
- tendency to condense
- tendency to be forgotten
- tendency to remain
- tendency to activate other histories
- tendency to be converted into value
- tendency to be backgrounded or externalized as cost
- tendency to form a return path
- tendency to sediment as a residual or residue

Entropy attributes are not fixed properties belonging only to the input.

They are relational properties that arise at the boundary where the input contacts the observer's history.

Accordingly, the term *entropy* in this document does not replace the thermodynamic quantity of entropy.

What is being treated here is a Scientific Ontology attribute describing how a difference disperses, gathers, is retained, is cut off, and alters later conditions of cognition.

---

## 4. Attention

Attention is a direction that retains a difference, follows it, and attempts to give it resolution.

Attention is not merely looking at what one likes.

When attention is established, something that had remained in the background becomes foregrounded as something perceptible, describable, and traceable.

Even within the same statement, if attention is directed toward correctness, logical consistency becomes visible.  
If attention is directed toward responsibility, it becomes visible whether the speaker occupies a position from which the return can be received.  
If attention is directed toward circulation, it becomes visible through which fields the words pass, where they are amplified, and where they are consumed.  
If attention is directed toward boundaries, it becomes visible whose history was contacted, what was damaged, and what remained.

Attention brings a difference forward as reality.

Attention, however, is not omnipotent.

Directing strong attention toward one difference also creates conditions under which other differences are abstracted away.

Attention is an opening, but it is also an allocation of the field of view.

---

## 5. Abstraction

Abstraction is a direction that does not retain a difference, backgrounds it, cuts it off, suspends it, or sends it to another process.

Abstraction is not mere ignorance.

It is impossible to see everything.  
It is impossible to feel everything.  
It is impossible to continue thinking about everything at the same time.

Cognition acquires determinate form only by abstracting away something.

When abstraction is repeated, however, invisible regions become stabilized.

Once an observer becomes accustomed to not seeing a certain difference, that difference is increasingly likely to be processed as "irrelevant," "minor," "noise," "troublesome," "exceptional," or "overthinking."

Abstraction is not merely an operation that reduces the amount of information.

It organizes:

What is established as an object.  
What is treated as background.  
What is excluded from causation.  
What is excluded from responsibility.  
What is sent outside the measurable range.

Abstraction organizes forms of understanding.

Therefore, when re-collating a cognitive axis, it is necessary to ask not only what is being seen, but also what must remain unseen for that image of the world to hold.

---

## 6. The Organization of Sensibility

In this document, sensibility is treated as **the opening through which something becomes perceptible**.

This is not intended as a strict exposition or redefinition of sensibility in Kantian philosophy.

When attention and abstraction change, what can be felt changes.

An institution that previously produced no reaction may suddenly be felt as violence.  
A silence once considered meaningless may suddenly appear heavy.  
A familiar landscape may arise as loss or beauty after contact with a particular history.  
An operation once considered normal may no longer feel normal after one learns of someone's exhaustion.

This does not occur only because knowledge has increased.

The felt world changes because the opening through which differences are received has changed.

Sensibility is not a fixed receiver.

Sensibility is organized through the repetition of history, attention, abstraction, value, cost, and residuals.

---

## 7. The Organization of Understanding

In this document, understanding is treated as **the organization through which what has been felt is arranged as objects, relations, causes, and responsibilities**.

This is likewise not intended as a strict exposition or redefinition of understanding in Kantian philosophy.

The same event may be organized by one person as an individual failure.  
Another may organize it as an institutional malfunction.  
Another as a historical residual.  
Another as an ethical return.  
Another as contingency.  
Another as structural necessity.

What changes here is not only the conclusion.

What changes is the kind of case the event is received as.

Understanding classifies differences, relates them, and places them into forms in which judgment becomes possible.

For this reason, when the mode of abstraction changes, the form in which the world is understood also changes.

The runaway condition of understanding is to mistake only what can be classified for what exists.

The maturity of understanding is the ability not to treat what cannot be classified as nothing, but to retain it as a residual and wait for a new classificatory form or another route of collation.

## 8. Value and Cost: Weighting Prior to Explicit Cognition

Value and cost in this document do not refer only to completed ethical judgments.

Value is a difference pre-weighted as worthy of retention.

Cost is a difference allocated as burden, delay, exception, noise, sacrifice, or an object of processing in order to retain that value.

What appears valuable becomes easier to see.  
What is treated as excessively costly is more likely to be made invisible.

When a society strongly retains efficiency as value, slowness, margin, hesitation, suspension, and collation are likely to appear as costs.  
When order is strongly retained as value, exceptions, deviations, pain, and unresolved voices are likely to appear as costs.  
When correctness is strongly retained as value, the pain of relationships and the cutoff of return paths are more easily abstracted away.  
When empathy is strongly retained as value, structural responsibility and institutional residuals are more easily abstracted away.

At this stage, value and cost do not yet constitute an explicit worldview.

They are pre-reflective weightings that affect what is seen and what is not seen.

The stage at which these weightings are made explicit and become comparable as ideologies, institutions, methods, ethics, or technological choices is treated in [`Optional_Axiom_Modules_as_Cognitive_Bridge.en.md`](../03_Tam_Goodness/Optional_Axiom_Modules_as_Cognitive_Bridge.en.md).

## 9. Residuals, Residues, and Repetition

Cognition is not completed by a single input.

Part of a difference is processed.  
Part becomes meaning.  
Part is suspended.  
Part is cut off.  
Part remains unclassifiable.

Following the distinction made in [`Boundary_Epistemological_Critique.en.md`](../01_Sat_Truth/Boundary_Epistemological_Critique.en.md), this document uses the terms as follows:

- **Residual**: a difference that appears during operational correspondence, collation, classification, or observation and remains available for re-collation.
- **Residue**: a historical sediment that remains after processing, classification, institutionalization, exchange, or explanation and continues to act upon a field while remaining partially invisible.

A residual can open the next act of cognition.

A residue can bias later sensibility and understanding while remaining unseen.

For example, an unexplained failure may make later perceptions of danger hypersensitive.  
A disadvantage treated as resolved may remain as distrust toward an institution.  
A discomfort that could not be verbalized may acquire meaning only after contact with a later event.

Repetition is not merely the recurrence of the same process.

With each repetition, residuals and residues are added to the conditions of the next input.

A cognitive axis therefore bears history.

## 10. Stabilization of a Cognitive Axis

A cognitive axis stabilizes through the following repetition:

1. A difference contacts a boundary.
2. The difference appears with entropy attributes in relation to the observer's history.
3. Attention retains some differences and gives them resolution.
4. Abstraction backgrounds, suspends, or cuts off other differences.
5. Sensibility is reorganized, changing what can be felt.
6. Understanding is reorganized, changing what can become intelligible as object, cause, and responsibility.
7. Value and cost are weighted.
8. What remains unprocessed persists as residual or residue.
9. Residuals and residues affect the next operation of attention, abstraction, sensibility, and understanding.
10. Through repetition, a particular direction of observation stabilizes.

This stabilized direction of observation is called a cognitive axis.

A cognitive axis is not random.

Why does this person look there?  
Why do they not look there?  
Why do they feel pain there?  
Why do they regard that as normal?  
Why do they perceive value there?  
Why do they cut that away as cost?

Behind these tendencies is a history of repeated attention and abstraction, value and cost, residuals and residues.

## 11. Transformation of Cognitive Aes

A cognitive axis may stabilize without becoming fixed.

### 11.1 Rigidity

When the same pattern of attention and abstraction is repeated and all different differences are forced into existing classifications, a cognitive axis becomes rigid.

A rigid cognitive axis does not merely reject updates.

It becomes more likely to process the demand for an update itself as irrelevant, hostile, exceptional, or noise.

### 11.2 Drift

When new differences repeatedly make contact and can no longer be processed through the existing allocation of value and cost, a cognitive axis gradually drifts.

Drift begins before an explicit ideological conversion.

It begins when what could not previously be felt becomes perceptible and when earlier explanations cease to fit.

### 11.3 Branching

Distinct cognitive axes may coexist within the same observer.

A person may read work primarily through efficiency and family life primarily through relationships.  
They may emphasize measurability in scientific judgment and symbolism or narrative in creative work.

The presence of multiple axes does not immediately imply contradiction or hypocrisy.

The issue is whether the observer can identify the conditions under which an axis is switched and can collate where costs are transferred by that switching.

### 11.4 Re-Collation and Reconnection

When a residual is retained and contacts another history or external input, an existing cognitive axis can be re-collated.

Re-collation does not simply negate the former mode of cognition.

Why was that axis necessary?  
What was it protecting?  
What was it abstracting away?  
Is that abstraction still necessary?

Re-collation examines these questions and, where necessary, reorganizes the allocation of attention, abstraction, value, and cost.

The transformation of a cognitive axis cannot be produced through external command alone.

The observer must possess a boundary through which differences and residuals that cannot be processed by the existing axis can be received again.

## 12. Cognitive Axes in Individuals, Organizations, and Societies

Cognitive axes do not necessarily appear only in individuals.

Organizations also possess stabilized directions of observation regarding what they receive as a problem, what they discard as noise, what they record as achievement, and what they treat as external cost.

Institutions also possess cognitive axes regarding what counts as an admissible fact, what counts as evidence, and what is processed as an exception.

Societies also repeat, through media, education, statistics, law, custom, and technological infrastructure, what becomes easier to see and what becomes harder to see.

Individuals, organizations, institutions, and societies must not, however, be treated as identical subjects.

Their boundaries, histories, authorities, responsibilities, and rates of update differ.

This document does not make them identical. It makes them comparable through shared perspectives: difference, attention, abstraction, sensibility, understanding, value, cost, residuals, and repetition.

## 13. Connection to the Meaning Generation Model

For something to become visible and for it to become meaning are not the same.

A cognitive axis directs what becomes perceptible and what acquires an intelligible form.

The conditions under which a difference is integrated into history, acquires a return path, and closes as meaning that changes later reception are the subject of the Meaning Generation Model.

The following distinction should therefore be maintained:

```text
Cognitive-axis formation
  What becomes foregrounded and what it becomes intelligible as

Meaning generation
  How a foregrounded difference is integrated into history
  and constituted as a meaning loop with a return path
```

Something may be visible without yet becoming meaning.  
Something may bear meaning without being explainable as a proposition.  
Something that once became meaningful may later be reorganized into another meaning through subsequent history.

This document does not redefine the meaning loop.

It treats only the interaction through which a cognitive axis selects the entrance to meaning generation and the history of meaning generation alters the next cognitive axis.

## 15. Treatment of Embodiment

Cognition appears in an observer grounded in a body.

Changes in cognition are often experienced as embodied textures.

The chest feels heavy.  
Something does not settle in the stomach.  
The throat feels blocked.  
One feels compelled to move one's hands.  
The skin seems to reject something.  
The field of vision seems to open.

Such expressions are useful as auxiliary descriptions of how cognitive change appears to the self.

They are not diagnoses.

This document does not map bodily regions, nerves, bodily fluids, foods, sensory organs, internal organs, constitutions, pathologies, or treatments onto cognitive axes.

Embodiment is not treated as the single cause of cognitive-axis formation. It is treated as one mode in which cognitive change appears to a local observer.

## 16. Connection to Logical Depth

The public core of this document is the formation of cognitive axes through difference, attention, abstraction, sensibility, understanding,
 value, cost, residuals, and repetition.

When connected to the high-strength hypotheses of Scientific Ontology, however, a cognitive axis can also be read as a structure of time and history.

Provisionally, the following are distinguished:

- **External clock time**: time measured as an order shared in the external world.
- **Internal count-time**: how a system internally counts external events, updates, connections, and delays.
- **Intrinsic time**: time proper to a local existence, advancing through the commitments of meaning loops that receive, suspend, cut off, return, and connect differences to downstream conditions.
- **Logical-depth axis**: a depth direction indicating how much unresolved material, contradiction, branching, residuals, and meaning pressure can be retained and reconstructed without being reduced to before and after in external time.

Under this connection, a cognitive axis is not merely a habit of attention.

It is also a historical structure formed by which differences an observer has committed to, which meaning loops it has closed, which residuals it has retained, and which residues it has taken on.

This section, however, is a high-strength research connection.

This document does not present intrinsic time, entropy, meaning loops, or the logical-depth axis as mechanisms of standard physics, neuroscience, or medicine.

Its role is to preserve a port showing to which depth-history a cognitive axis stabilized in planar logic may be connected.

For the public handling of physical-near vocabulary in this section, see [`Physics_Correspondence_Policy.en.md`](../00_Overview/Physics_Correspondence_Policy.en.md).

## 17. Non-Claim Boundaries

This document does not claim that:

- it replaces the concepts of energy or entropy in standard physics;
- it presents an alternative theory to psychology, cognitive science, neuroscience, psychiatry, or medicine;
- cognitive axes can diagnose an individual's personality, ideology, ability, pathology, or bodily tendency;
- cognitive axes may be used as classification tools for education, hiring, labor management, treatment, religious direction, persuasion, intervention, or governance;
- manipulating attention or abstraction makes it possible to guide another person into an arbitrary worldview;
- sensibility and understanding are redefined as replacements for their historical definitions in Kantian philosophy.

What this document proposes is a conceptual model for collating how a world begins to appear, what becomes invisible, and how that direction becomes historical.

## 18. Conclusion

Cognition is not fixed.

Sensibility is not fixed.  
Understanding is not fixed.

What attention is directed toward.  
What is abstracted away.  
What is retained as value.  
What is processed as cost.  
What remains as a residual.  
What sediments as residue.  
Which processes are repeated.

Through these, the felt appearance of the world and the form of understanding change.

A cognitive axis is a direction of observation stabilized through this repetition.

It is not a label for fixing the essence of an observer.

It is a historical coordinate for re-collating why this world appears in this way and why another world has become difficult to see.

Once this coordinate is made explicit, distinct worldviews need not immediately be reduced to rankings of superiority and inferiority. They can instead be compared in terms of what they see, what they discard, what they protect, and to whom they return costs.
